Introduction — How Far Must We Go to Find God?

The story of the Universal Father that began in Paper 1 comes down to the individual in Paper 5. How can so great a God have fellowship with a single person? The paper’s first answer is simple. An actual fragment of the living God, the Thought Adjuster, resides within every normal-minded and morally conscious mortal. Our assurance of divine fellowship must rest on this fact. (5:0.1)

Man does not have to go farther than his own inner experience of the soul’s contemplation of this spiritual-reality presence to find God and attempt communion with him. (5:0.1)

God has also reserved to himself the prerogative of bestowing personality and of maintaining direct, fatherly contact with all personal beings through the personality circuit. The Adjuster and the personality circuit—these two channels are the threads that run through the whole paper. (5:0.2)

I. The Approach to God — The Way Is Open

The finite creature cannot approach the Father directly, not because the Father is aloof, but because of our own finiteness. Were we transported to Paradise this very moment, we would not even know we were in the Father’s presence. Man must be spiritually translated many times. Yet apart from that long road, we can commune right now with the spirit of the Father within us. (5:1.1, 5:1.3)

This section stresses equality of spiritual opportunity. People differ greatly in intellectual ability and social environment, but the possibilities for spiritual progress are equal to all. Everyone enjoys the same divine presence, has the same privilege of communing with that spirit, and can equally choose to accept its leading. (5:1.4, 5:1.5)

To each of you and to all of us, God is approachable, the Father is attainable, the way is open (5:1.8)

What, then, hides God from us? The Father is not in spiritual hiding, but many creatures have hidden themselves in the mists of their own willful decisions. So long as the power of choice remains, a person may draw near God and may also turn away again and again. Final destiny is settled only when the desire to choose the Father’s will is lost forever—or when Adjuster fusion proclaims an irrevocable choice. (5:1.10, 5:1.11)

II. The Presence of God — Known by Its Fruits

God’s presence appears differently in different realms. His physical presence is the reality of the material universe; his mind presence depends on the depth of intellectual experience; his spiritual presence is differential, determined by spiritual receptivity and by the degree to which the will is consecrated to doing the divine will. And that presence is found most fully not in nature but in our attempted communion with the indwelling Adjuster. (5:2.1, 5:2.3)

What a mistake to dream of God far off in the skies when the spirit of the Universal Father lives within your own mind! (5:2.3)

Many believers are uneasy because they do not clearly feel the Adjuster’s presence. The paper says that this in no way disproves the experience. It is exceedingly difficult for the material mind to be distinctly conscious of divine realities, and a new consciousness evolves as the soul grows. The proof of our relationship with the Adjuster is not a feeling but the fruits of the spirit borne in our lives. (5:2.4, 5:2.5, 5:2.6)

III. True Worship — Adoration That Asks Nothing

In the highest sense we worship the Universal Father and him only. We may worship the Father as he is manifested in his Creator Sons, but in the end it is the Father who is worshiped. This section draws a clear distinction between worship and prayer: worship is for its own sake, while prayer contains an element of self- or creature-interest. (5:3.1, 5:3.3)

There is absolutely no self-request or other element of personal interest in true worship (5:3.3)

The moment self-interest intrudes, worship turns into prayer. Yet the paper adds that in practical religious life there is no reason why prayer should not be addressed to the Father as part of true worship. It then sorts out which Deity is concerned with the different parts of our lives. (5:3.4, 5:3.5)

Worship is the mobilization of all the powers of the human personality under the dominance of the soul and the direction of the Adjuster. The mind consents to worship, the soul craves and initiates it, and the Adjuster conducts it on behalf of mind and soul. Worship thus becomes an experience that takes place on several levels at once. The spiritual growth of the soul takes place wholly independently of the intellectual self-consciousness. (5:3.7)

IV. God in Religion — Many Paths and the Way of Jesus

The religions of evolution drive people toward God by fear; the religions of revelation draw them to seek a God of love because they long to become like him. Religion, though, is not a passive feeling of dependence but a living and dynamic experience of divinity attainment predicated on service to humanity. The truly religious person does not bend the universe to serve the self but dedicates the self to the service of the universe family. (5:4.1, 5:4.3)

The paper does not belittle the various religions. The morals of the Zoroastrians, the metaphysics of the Hindus, and the ethics of the Confucianists are all of value as valid approaches to the religion of Jesus. The Hebrews based their religion on goodness and the Greeks on beauty, but Jesus revealed a God of love, and love embraces truth, beauty, and goodness. (5:4.6, 5:4.7)

The spiritual status of any religion may be determined by the nature of its prayers. (5:4.8)
Source 5:4.10

Because these three streams were blended, Christian theology has found it hard to attain consistency. The paper urges us to view Jesus’ life positively—to think less of his sinlessness than of his righteousness and loving service. Jesus upstepped the passive love of the Hebrew concept to the active affection of a God who is the Father of every individual, even of the wrongdoer. (5:4.14, 5:4.15)

V. The Consciousness of God — No One Is Excluded

Morality has its origin in the reason of self-consciousness, but that alone does not reach the spiritual level of religious experience. Religion is an independent realm, grounded neither in the facts of science nor in the obligations of society, and it may permeate the material, social, moral, and spiritual levels alike. The same God comes to the scientist as the First Cause, to the artist as the ideal of beauty, to the philosopher as universal unity, and to the believer as the loving Father. (5:5.1, 5:5.2, 5:5.3)

Source 5:5.11
Level of God-consciousnessWhat is realized

Soul consciousnessThe realization of the ideal of God
Spirit consciousnessThe realization of the spirit reality of God

This section also treats the relation of faith and intellect with balance. The experience of God-consciousness stays the same from generation to generation, but the philosophic and theologic definitions of God must change with each advancing age. Religious experience must be willing to submit itself to intelligent criticism and reasonable interpretation; it must not seek to stand apart from the rest of human experience. (5:5.12)

The greatest comfort comes in the passage on the conditions of survival. Limits of intellect, lack of education and culture, or low social status cannot invalidate the presence of the divine spirit in a believing person. Just as parents need no diploma to have children, a mind that can discern right and wrong and worship God, united with an Adjuster, is enough to begin the growth of an immortal soul. What remains is the honest choice to seek God and do his will. (5:5.13, 5:5.14)

When the mind believes God and the soul knows God, and when, with the fostering Adjuster, they all desire God, then is survival assured. (5:5.13)

VI. The God of Personality — The Dignity of Free Will

The Universal Father is the bestower and conservator of every personality and the destiny of all who choose to do his will. Personality is one of the unsolved mysteries of the universes. Neither mind nor spirit nor energy alone makes a personality; personality is a quality bestowed exclusively by the Father. Nor is it achieved by degrees: there either is personality or there is no personality. (5:6.1, 5:6.2, 5:6.3)

Having given personality and provided for the growth of an immortal soul, the Father stands aside. Whether to create the surviving, eternal self now rests with man’s own choice. No being or force in all the universe can interfere with that choice. (5:6.7, 5:6.8)

As pertains to eternal survival, God has decreed the sovereignty of the material and mortal will, and that decree is absolute. (5:6.8)

Just as all gravity is circuited in the Isle of Paradise, all mind in the Conjoint Actor, and all spirit in the Eternal Son, so all personality is circuited in the personal presence of the Universal Father. This personality circuit unerringly carries the worship of all personalities to the Father. And the portal of eternity opens only in response to free-will choice. Closing the series, the author confesses that he can say nothing more helpful than this: God is our Father, and we are his children. (5:6.11, 5:6.12, 5:6.13)

No personal creature can be coerced into the eternal adventure (5:6.12)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 5 is the conclusion of the Father series. The infinite Father lives within us as the Adjuster, knows us through the personality circuit, and absolutely respects our free choice. The way is open, and what we need to walk it is not learning or status but an honest desire to seek God and to become like him.

With Paper 6 The Urantia Book turns its attention to the Eternal Son, the Second Source and Center. If we read on remembering the Father we have met in these five papers, it will become clearer that the Son and the Spirit are both ways in which this Father’s love is carried to the universe.
