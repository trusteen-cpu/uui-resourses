Introduction — The Last Instructors on Paradise

Paper 26 described the secondary supernaphim who help pilgrims on the circuits of Havona; Paper 27 introduces the primary supernaphim, who serve the Deities on the Isle of Paradise, the destination of the whole journey. These children of the Infinite Spirit are supreme in perfection, and from eternity not one of them has ever strayed from the paths of light and righteousness. The author records the fact in a short, firm sentence. (27:0.1)

The roll calls are complete (27:0.1)

They do not function widely outside Paradise, but there is an exception. On worlds isolated by rebellion and then reclaimed through the bestowal of a Paradise Son, a primary supernaphim takes command of the seraphic hosts there. The present “chief of seraphim” on Urantia is the second of this order on duty since the bestowal of Christ Michael. It is striking that the highest angels are sent to care for the world that was once among the darkest. (27:0.2)

The framework of the paper is seven orders of service. Yet ascenders meet them in the reverse order of their naming: the Paradise career begins with the instigators of rest and the training ends with the conductors of worship, after which the endless career of the finaliter begins. The sections of the paper follow this order of experience. (27:0.3, 27:0.11)

I. Instigators of Rest — The Sleep That Crosses into Eternity

The instigators of rest go out to the innermost circuit of Havona and work there with the complements of rest of the secondary supernaphim. They are the final instructors who make ready the pilgrims of time for eternity, and they preside over the last transition sleep that graduates a creature of space into the realm of the eternal. (27:1.1)

The one essential to the enjoyment of Paradise is rest, divine rest (27:1.1)

The section explains that rest is sevenfold. Among its forms, the deep sleep of metamorphosis is of a different kind. It is not progress through the stages of one status but the transition sleep that attends every real change of universe status. (27:1.2)

The last metamorphic sleep means more than all the earlier transition slumbers that marked the status attainments of the ascending career. Through it the creature crosses the innermost margins of time and space to take up residence on timeless and spaceless Paradise. Just as seraphim are essential to a mortal’s survival of death, the instigators and complements of rest are essential to this transcending metamorphosis. At every great transition of life, someone’s care is present, and this pattern binds together the beginning and the end of the ascent. (27:1.3)

The pilgrim who fell asleep on the last circuit of Havona awakens on Paradise and recognizes in the instigator of rest who greets him the very angel who brought on that final sleep. And he remembers the last reach of faith in which he once more committed the keeping of his identity into the hands of the Father. Even at the last threshold of the ascent, what is asked of us is not knowledge but trust. (27:1.4, 27:1.5)

II. Chiefs of Assignment — Service and Fellowship

The chiefs of assignment are the group designated by the chief of supernaphim to preside over the organization of all three orders of supernaphim. As a body the supernaphim are self-governing and self-regulating; only their common chief, the first angel of Paradise, is above them. (27:2.1)

The section reminds us that life on Paradise is not filled with study alone; service is an essential part of prefinaliter education. Ascenders must also fraternize with upwards of three thousand orders of Paradise Citizens and many other beings, and after long contact with such mighty intellects it is very restful to visit with angelic types of mind, which remind them of the seraphim they have known so long. That even in the highest places a conversation with a familiar kind of friend can be a rest is a very human touch. (27:2.2, 27:2.3)

III. Interpreters of Ethics — A Widening Circle of Associates

The higher one ascends in the scale of life, the more attention must be paid to universe ethics. The section defines the starting point of ethics very simply. (27:3.1)

Ethical awareness is simply the recognition by any individual of the rights inherent in the existence of any and all other individuals. (27:3.1)

From the world of origin onward, the ascender has added one group of associates after another, and with each new group came one more level of ethics to observe. So by the time he reaches Paradise he does not need to learn ethics anew, but he does need counselors to help him interpret what he has learned in the presence of countless unfamiliar beings. The interpreters of ethics assist in this adjustment from the attainment of residential status until admission to the Corps of the Finality. (27:3.2, 27:3.3)

All these celestial companionships are invariably mutual. (27:3.4)

The ascending mortal is not merely a guest who receives from higher beings. He imparts to them something of his own personality and experience, and they are forever made different and better for having been associated with a mortal from the evolutionary worlds. Our small and imperfect beginning turns out to be a unique gift to the universe. (27:3.4)

IV. Directors of Conduct — Guides for the Strangers Within the Gates

Conduct on Paradise springs not from meaningless formality or artificial caste but from inherent courtesy. All conduct is spontaneous and free, and yet there is a proper and perfect way of doing things. The directors of conduct stay beside the “strangers within the gates” to guide their steps and spare them the confusion and uncertainty that would otherwise be inevitable. (27:4.1, 27:4.3)

Proper conduct is essential to progress by way of knowledge, through philosophy, to the spiritual heights of spontaneous worship. (27:4.2)

Despite the long preparation and the long journey, Paradise is still inexpressibly strange and new to those who arrive. There is comfort in this admission. However well prepared we are, it is no shame to be awkward in a new setting, and guides have been provided to help us through that awkwardness. (27:4.4)

V. The Custodians of Knowledge — A Living Library

The custodians of knowledge are the “living epistles” read by all on Paradise. The facts of the universes are actually recorded in them, and it is inherently impossible for an untruth to lodge in their minds. Ask the living finders of knowledge at the northern extremity of the Isle, and the very beings who are what you wish to know will appear. Instead of engrossed pages, one learns face to face with living intelligences. (27:5.1, 27:5.2)

They are the divine records of truth, the living books of real knowledge. (27:5.1)

They have organized knowledge into seven grand divisions, each with about one million subdivisions. This living library, however, is available only to the central universe and the superuniverses; the local universes benefit from it only indirectly, by reflection. That is why time and space require their own vast organization of recording angels. (27:5.3, 27:5.5)

The wisdom of truth takes origin in the divinity of the central universe, but knowledge, experiential knowledge, largely has its beginnings in the domains of time and space (27:5.4)

VI. Masters of Philosophy — Mysteries That Never End

Next to the supreme satisfaction of worship is the exhilaration of philosophy. (27:6.1)

However high we climb, a thousand mysteries will remain that call for philosophy. The masters of philosophy, the “wise men of heaven,” use the truth of knowledge and the facts of experience to master the unknown, and at the southern extremity of Paradise they conduct courses in the seventy functional divisions of wisdom. One hour of their instruction, we are told, would equal ten thousand years of Urantia’s word-memory methods. (27:6.1, 27:6.2, 27:6.3, 27:6.4)

Placed side by side, sections five and six reveal the ladder of learning this paper describes: not stopping at knowing facts, but threading them into truth and raising experience into wisdom. The conclusions of philosophy cannot be as settled as facts, yet those who hear the masters find lasting satisfaction even before unsolved questions. These teachings are not broadcast; only those present can receive them. (27:6.2, 27:6.5, 27:6.6)

VII. Conductors of Worship — Worship That Overflows

Worship is the highest privilege and the first duty of all created intelligences. (27:7.1)

Worship is the conscious and joyous act of recognizing the intimate, personal relationship between the Creators and their creatures, and its quality is determined by the depth of creature perception. As pilgrims pass through the Havona circuits, worship becomes an ever-growing passion, until on Paradise its expression must actually be directed. Were it not for the chiefs of assignment periodically breaking up the assemblies, ascenders would remain forever in the attitude of worship. We can only guess at a place where worship has passed beyond duty into delight. (27:7.1, 27:7.2, 27:7.3)

What play does for your jaded minds on earth, worship will do for your perfected souls on Paradise. (27:7.5)

Interestingly, beings of inherent perfection can never fully appreciate the spiritual emotions of those who have slowly climbed up from the depths of darkness. So since the times of Grandfanda the supernaphim have never been able fully to accommodate the spirit of worship on Paradise. The insight here is that the gratitude of those who rose through struggle becomes the greatest worship. When the fullness of worship is reached, the ascender is ready for the seventh jubilee. (27:7.6, 27:7.8)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 27 describes the end of the ascending journey, but that end is another beginning. The door opens with rest; the ascender learns service, ethics, and courtesy; passes through knowledge and wisdom; and finishes training in worship. Then the finaliter stands before eternal service and the new challenge of God the Ultimate. The paper tells us that we can begin to appreciate the spirit of Paradise worship even here on Urantia, because the spirits of the Gods already indwell us. (27:7.5, 27:7.10)
