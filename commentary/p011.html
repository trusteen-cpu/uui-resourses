Introduction — A Spiritual Abode and a Material Sphere

If Paper 10 deals with the union of the three Deities, Paper 11 describes the place where they dwell. Paradise is the eternal center of the universe of universes and the abiding place of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit and their associates. The paper first makes clear that Paradise is not only a spiritual abode but also a material sphere. Since all intelligent creatures live on material abodes, the absolute controlling center must also be material, literal. (11:0.1)

The beauty of Paradise appears in three ways: its material beauty lies in its physical perfection, its grandeur in the intellectual accomplishments of its inhabitants, and its glory in divine spirit personality, the light of life. Yet the wonder of the whole is beyond the grasp of the finite mind. There are no records or traditions about the origin of the Isle; Paradise is from eternity. (11:0.2)

I. The Divine Residence — Where Is God?

To creatures, Paradise exists first of all as the dwelling place of Deity. The personal presence of the Father is at the very center of the upper surface; the presence of the Son immediately surrounds it, and both are invested by the glory of the Infinite Spirit. God has always dwelt at this center and always will. (11:1.1, 11:1.2)

The section compares God’s location to the cities of our world. Just as a navigator with ship, maps, and compass can find a distant city, so one who had the time, the means, the spiritual qualifications, and the guidance could journey inward from universe to universe until at last standing before the Father. Not having visited a city does not disprove its reality, and the fact that few have found God does not disprove his. (11:1.3)

That you have not visited these places in no way disproves their reality or actual existence. (11:1.3)

The Father is always to be found at this center. The universal lines of gravity from the ends of creation converge in him, and if he moved, universal pandemonium would follow. There are many lines we might trace, but all of them lead to the same place. (11:1.4)

II. Nature of the Eternal Isle — Absolutum, Without Time or Space

So vast a material universe must have a capital worthy of the dignity of its Ruler. Paradise is not spherical but ellipsoid: one-sixth longer north to south than east to west, and essentially flat, its thickness one tenth of its east-west diameter. These dimensions, together with the greater out-pressure of force at its north end, make it possible to establish absolute direction in the master universe. (11:2.1, 11:2.2, 11:2.3)

Source 11:2.8

The substance of the Isle is a homogeneous organization of space potency found nowhere else in the universe; the Melchizedeks of Nebadon named it absolutum. It is neither dead nor alive; it is the original nonspiritual expression of the First Source and Center. (11:2.9)

Paradise exists without time and has no location in space. (11:2.10)

Roughly speaking, space seems to originate just below nether Paradise and time just above upper Paradise. The citizens of Paradise are conscious of a nontime sequence of events rather than time, and there motion is not inherent but volitional. (11:2.11)

III. Upper Paradise — The Father’s House, the Father’s Mansions

Upper Paradise has three grand spheres of activity: the Deity presence, the Most Holy Sphere, and the Holy Area. The Most Holy Sphere surrounding the Deities is reserved for worship, trinitization, and high spiritual attainment; it has no material structures at all and is wholly spiritual. The author says it is useless to try to portray it to beings as material as we are. (11:3.1)

The outlying Holy Area is divided into seven concentric zones, often called “the Father’s Paradise mansions.” The first zone is home to the Paradise Citizens and Havona natives; the second is the residential area of the ascending creatures from the seven superuniverses. This second zone is divided into seven sectors, one for each superuniverse. (11:3.3)

Even this unimaginable number occupies considerably less than one per cent of the assigned area of the Holy Land. There is plenty of room for those still on their way, and even for those who will not begin the Paradise climb until the eternal future. In the Father’s house there is no shortage of room. (11:3.4)

IV. Peripheral Paradise — Where We Arrive, Where We Remember

The Isle ends abruptly at its edge, but it is so vast that the angle is hardly discernible. All personality transports bound for Paradise land on this periphery, for neither upper nor nether Paradise can be approached by transport supernaphim or other space traversers. Here too are the force-focal headquarters of the Seven Master Spirits and the flash stations that send certain energies to the seven superuniverses. (11:4.1, 11:4.2)

Peripheral Paradise holds the historic and prophetic exhibit areas assigned to the Creator Sons, seven trillion of them, now set up or in reserve. Even so, they occupy only about four per cent of the peripheral area so assigned, and the space provided is at least one million times what is actually required. The authors infer that it is reserved for creations yet to appear beyond the seven superuniverses. (11:4.3, 11:4.4)

You must wait, and ascend while you wait (11:4.5)

V. Nether Paradise — The Heart of Universe Energy

We know of nether Paradise only what is revealed. Personalities do not sojourn there, and all physical-energy and cosmic-force circuits originate there. Directly beneath the location of the Trinity lies the unknown Zone of Infinity; an unnamed area surrounds it; and on the outer margins lies a vast elliptical force center concerned with space potency and force-energy. (11:5.1, 11:5.2, 11:5.4)

All force-energy originally proceeded from nether Paradise and will return there after completing its space circuit. Yet the paper makes a careful distinction: space does not originate there. Space is not force, energy, or power. (11:5.9)

All space alternately contracts and expands. The authors say they do not know the mechanism; they only observe it. As the pervaded space of the universes expands horizontally, the reservoirs of unpervaded space above and below Paradise contract, and the reverse. To picture their outline, think of an hourglass. (11:6.1, 11:6.2)

Space does not exist on any surface of Paradise; only the quiescent midspace zones touch the Isle. A vertical cross section of all space would slightly resemble a Maltese cross. The quiet zones between space levels, together with the alternate clockwise and counterclockwise flow of the galaxies, act as a brake that keeps gravity pressure from building to disruptive levels. (11:7.1, 11:7.3, 11:7.9)

VIII. Paradise Gravity — The Strand That Holds All Things

Gravity is the all-powerful grasp of the physical presence of Paradise. The center of absolute material gravity is the Isle of Paradise, complemented by the dark gravity bodies encircling Havona and equilibrated by the space reservoirs. Space itself does not respond to gravity, but it acts as a cushion that balances it. (11:8.1, 11:8.2, 11:8.3)

Gravity is the omnipotent strand on which are strung the gleaming stars, blazing suns, and whirling spheres (11:8.1)

IX. The Uniqueness of Paradise — Origin and Destiny

Paradise is the realm of primal origin and the final goal of destiny for all spirit personalities. It is the geographic center of infinity, yet it is not part of universal creation, not even a real part of Havona; it is an eternal and exclusive existence. (11:9.1, 11:9.2)

When the Father gave infinite personality expression to his spirit self in the Eternal Son, he simultaneously revealed the infinity potential of his nonpersonal self as Paradise. Reality was thus divided into the personal and the nonpersonal. For this reason the paper insists that Paradise cannot properly be called Deity. It is not conscious, it is not ancestral to any being, and it is not a creator; it is the absolute of patterns. (11:9.3, 11:9.4, 11:9.5)

Deity may cause much that is not Deity, and Paradise is not Deity (11:9.4)

This distinction gives an important balance to faith. We do not worship a place or a substance. Paradise is the Father’s residence and the beautiful pattern for every universe headquarters world, but the one we worship is the personal God who dwells there. The place is simply the setting for the meeting. (11:9.6)

The paper’s conclusion, however, is not about cosmology but about our destiny. For mortals the most important fact about Paradise is that it is the real, far-distant destination that souls ascending from the evolutionary worlds will actually reach. (11:9.8)

Every God-knowing mortal who has espoused the career of doing the Father’s will has already embarked upon the long, long Paradise trail of divinity pursuit and perfection attainment. (11:9.8)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 11 is full of information about size, structure, and energy, but all of its descriptions point to one thing. The place we are heading toward truly exists, and ample room has been prepared there for us. God is as certainly located as a city on a map, and every circuit and every path finally converges on him. Whoever has chosen to do the Father’s will is already on the journey.
