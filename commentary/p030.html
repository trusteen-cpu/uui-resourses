Introduction — A Map That Is Shown but Not Fully Drawn

Paper 30 serves as a kind of index that gathers up the superuniverse series. The beings who function in Paradise and the grand universe are so numerous that even their major orders would bewilder human imagination. The author announces that he will present two classifications: a suggestion of the Paradise classification and an abridgment of the Uversa Personality Register. (30:0.1)

What is striking is that this classification is incomplete on purpose. Because not all groups are revealed, no fully consistent classification is possible, and completing it would take many more papers. Yet the author says this would not be desirable, since it would deprive thinking mortals of the next thousand years of the stimulus to creative speculation. (30:0.2)

It is best that man not have an overrevelation; it stifles imagination. (30:0.2)

This principle also tells us how to read the paper. Rather than memorizing long lists of names, it is more important to accept that the universe is orderly in its organization and yet holds wide margins we cannot yet fully know.

I. The Paradise Classification of Living Beings — By Origin

On Paradise, living beings are classified by their inherent and attained relationship to the Paradise Deities. At the great gatherings, too, those present are often grouped by origin: triune, dual, and single origin. Add the eventuated beings, the fragments, the superpersonal beings, and others, and there are seven categories. (30:1.1)

Looking for our own place in this classification is astonishing. Adjuster-fused mortals, together with Son-fused and Spirit-fused mortals, are listed among the ascending orders of dual-origin beings. A being who began in animal origin, united with a fragment of God, enters so high a category. The paper sums up God’s ways of working in three words. (30:1.50, 30:1.100)

God, as a superperson, eventuates; God, as a person, creates; God, as a preperson, fragments (30:1.99)

That fragment, the Adjuster, evolves the spirit soul upon the material mind in accordance with the freewill choices of the personality that the Father has bestowed on the mortal. A clear principle about personality is also stated: there are no personalities of “pure mind,” and no mind entity that is not associated with spiritual or physical energy is a personality. (30:1.99, 30:1.113)

no entity has personality unless he is endowed with it by God who is spirit. (30:1.113)

The author confesses that these papers cannot even begin to exhaust the story of the beings who live, worship, and serve in the universes of time and the central universe of eternity. Mortals are persons, so personalized beings can be described to us, but an absonitized being could never be explained. Passages like this, in which a revelation states its own limits, lead us to hold humbly what we know and keep an open mind before what we do not. (30:1.114)

Uversa registers the divine family of living beings in seven grand divisions. The chief purpose of the list is to present the beings revealed in these narratives, especially the orders mortals will meet on their way to Paradise. The vast hosts who work apart from mortal ascension are not mentioned. (30:2.1, 30:2.9)

III. The Courtesy Colonies — Guests Who Learn and Teach

The seven courtesy colonies are groups of guests who sojourn for longer or shorter periods on the architectural spheres. The star students choose worlds well suited to observation, such as Uversa, and any being from any world who has sufficient knowledge of continuing life and physical law may join this corps of celestial astronomers. Once enrolled, however, one may not withdraw for a millennium of Uversa time. (30:3.1, 30:3.2, 30:3.3)

Except on Paradise, knowledge is not inherent (30:3.5)

This statement—that understanding of the physical universe largely depends on observation and research—affirms the value of scientific inquiry on a cosmic scale. The passage about the extension-school instructors reveals a feature of the entire ascension plan: the practice of giving new truth and experience to others as soon as it is acquired. (30:3.5, 30:3.8, 30:3.9)

You work your way through the long school of Paradise attainment by serving as teachers to those pupils just behind you in the scale of progression. (30:3.9)

The colony of student visitors on Uversa alone now numbers over one billion individuals. Morontia mortals may be visitors only within their own local universe, yet the human desire to travel and see new peoples and worlds, the paper says, will be fully gratified on the way to Paradise. The wish to travel and learn is no idle curiosity; it is part of the ascending career. (30:3.11, 30:3.12)

The privilege of intrauniverse travel and observation is a part of the career of all ascending beings. (30:3.12)

IV. The Ascending Mortals — Seven Stages from Planet to Paradise

The last section of the paper is our own story. The author surveys the universe career of Adjuster-indwelt mortals in seven stages, explaining that he tells it in these terms because all the races of Urantia may look forward to this destiny. (30:4.1, 30:4.9)

The account of the sleeping survivors eases our fear about what follows death. The passing of time means nothing to those who sleep, and apart from the time delay they move through the ascension regime exactly like everyone else. (30:4.11, 30:4.12)

those who have slept five thousand years will react no differently than those who have rested five days. (30:4.12)

Resurrection is not the reassembly of the physical body. The Adjuster keeps the spirit transcript of the mind and the seraphim keeps the evolved soul, and when the two reunite their trusts, the new individual constitutes the survival of the old personality. The paper explains that the “mansions” of the Father’s house of which Jesus spoke refer to these training worlds. (30:4.15, 30:4.17)

The mind, personality, and character are unchanged by such an advance; only does the form undergo modification. (30:4.20)

After the spirit confirmation received before leaving the local universe, the ascender’s status is settled forever. In the superuniverse the ascender masters local and superuniverse administration; the journey to Havona is always made alone; and the first act on arrival is to thank the transport seconaphim who carried him there. A new career that begins with gratitude is a memorable picture. (30:4.21, 30:4.24, 30:4.27, 30:4.28)

Superuniverse wards have never been known to go astray. (30:4.21)

This journey is also one in which the form changes at every stage. On earth we were creatures of flesh and blood, in the local universe morontia beings, and in the superuniverse evolving spirits. Only on reaching Havona does spiritual education begin in earnest, until at last we appear on Paradise as perfected spirits. It is also worth noting that the path walked in classes is replaced in Havona by personal and individual training. (30:4.26, 30:4.27)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 30 hides a story within its long lists. In the midst of an almost endless order of beings lies the path of the mortal who begins in animal origin and advances to Paradise. Even if the Mortal Corps of the Finality had no further destiny, the author says, its present assignment would be fully adequate and glorious. (30:4.33)

Their present destiny wholly justifies the universal plan of evolutionary ascent. (30:4.33)
