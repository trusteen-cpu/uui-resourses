Introduction — The Waves That Flowed West

Paper 80 is the companion of Paper 79, tracing how the Andites spread to the west. It is presented by an Archangel of Nebadon. The text sums up that the European blue man did not of himself achieve a great civilization, yet when blended with Adamite and Andite strains he became the basis of one of the most potent stocks for aggressive civilization. The modern white peoples carry Adamic strains together with several Sangik strains, as well as Andonite and Nodite blood. (80:0.1, 80:0.2)

This paper records harsh events such as conquest and extermination more than once. The commentary presents them calmly as historical facts reported by the text and in no way treats any group’s deeds as an example to follow. It is also worth keeping in mind that at the end of the paper the text itself declares it a fallacy to divide the white peoples into a few types. (80:9.15)

I. The Adamites Enter Europe

Even before the last Andites left the Euphrates valley, many of their kin had entered Europe as adventurers, teachers, traders, and warriors. The Mediterranean was then an inland trough closed by the Gibraltar isthmus and the Sicilian land bridge, and there blue men, Saharans, Nodites, and Adamites met and began early maritime commerce. Egypt received Mesopotamians for more than thirty thousand years, but a great influx of Saharans brought it to its lowest cultural level some fifteen thousand years ago. (80:1.1, 80:1.3)

The text gives several reasons the Adamites turned toward Europe. The red and yellow races were too far away; the blue men of Europe had no religious practices the Adamites found repulsive; and there was strong attraction between the two peoples. Above all, the paths of migration were favorable. As a result, the early waves of Mesopotamian culture flowed almost entirely into Europe. (80:1.5, 80:1.6, 80:1.8)

And it was these circumstances that determined the antecedents of modern European civilization. (80:1.8)

II. Climatic and Geologic Changes — Roads Closed and Roads Opened

As the northern ice fields retreated, the rain-bearing west winds shifted north, and the open pastures of the Sahara turned to desert. The dwellers of the Sahara plateau dispersed, and their traces remain in a certain kinship among peoples from the Deccan to both shores of the Mediterranean. (80:2.1, 80:2.3)

About the same time England separated from the continent and Denmark rose from the sea. An earthquake broke the Gibraltar isthmus, quickly raising the inland lake to the level of the Atlantic, and the Sicilian land bridge also sank, making the Mediterranean one sea. (80:2.4)

This cataclysm of nature flooded scores of human settlements and occasioned the greatest loss of life by flood in all the world’s history. (80:2.4)

With the western route closed, the Adamites pushed north and east and entered Europe from the east by way of Turkestan. The culture of the blue man they found there lagged thousands of years behind Asia’s, having been almost entirely cut off from Mesopotamia. Terrain and climate set the pace at which civilization spread. (80:2.5)

III. The Cro-Magnoid Blue Man — Honest and Courageous Artists

The blue men lived along the rivers of Europe and were already a highly blended people thirty-five thousand years ago. The early post-Adamic civilization of Europe was a blend of the vigor and art of the blue men with the creative imagination of the Adamites. The text records both the light and the shadow of this meeting. (80:3.1, 80:3.2, 80:3.3)

About fifteen thousand years ago, as the rain winds moved north, the grasslands of Europe became forests, and hunters turned into herders, fishers, and farmers. The text says this change brought cultural advance along with biologic retrogression, because settled life led them to keep captives as slaves. The retrogression lasted until a fresh impetus came from the east. (80:3.8, 80:3.9)

IV–V. The Andite Invasions of Europe; The Andite Conquest of Northern Europe

The Andites entered Europe in seven major invasions, the last arriving on horseback in three great waves. The early expansions of the violet race had been peaceful, but the union of the peaceful Adamites with the belligerent Nodites, later mingled with Sangik strains, produced Andites who carried out actual military conquests. (80:4.1, 80:4.3)

But the horse was the evolutionary factor which determined the dominance of the Andites in the Occident. (80:4.4)

The slower waves broke up the farther they traveled, but the mounted waves reached Europe as coherent groups still carrying their culture. Crossing the Russian plains they blended into one people, the ancestors of the so-called Nordic races. For three thousand years the military headquarters of the northern Andites was in Denmark. (80:4.4, 80:4.5, 80:5.2)

The text does not hide how harsh this conquest was. The Cro-Magnons held the Somme valley for over five hundred years before they fell, and Thor, the victor of the final battle, was later revered as a god by some groups. Those not absorbed, it records, were done away with under the form of ritual. This passage shows plainly the dark reality of the history of conquest. (80:5.4, 80:5.5, 80:5.6)

The blue strain contributed many sturdy traits and much physical vigor to the white races of Europe, but the humor and imagination of the blended European peoples were derived from the Andites. (80:5.7)

By 5000 B.C. the evolving white races were dominant throughout northern Europe, including northern Germany, northern France, and the British Isles, while central Europe was for a time held by the blue man and the round-headed Andonites of the Danube valley. The text concludes that the Andite-blue union caused a temporary lapse of civilization, but that its latent potential finally bore fruit in present-day European civilization. (80:5.7, 80:5.8)

VI. The Andites along the Nile — Egypt Succeeds Mesopotamia

As the culture of the Euphrates declined, the center of civilization moved to the Nile. Around 5000 B.C. there were seven distinct groups in Egypt, all but one from Mesopotamia. In the last great exodus, skilled Andite artisans arrived and greatly advanced Egyptian metalworking. (80:6.1, 80:6.2, 80:6.3)

Egypt became the successor of Mesopotamia as the headquarters of the most advanced group on earth. (80:6.1)

The Egyptians gathered their city deities into a national system of gods and maintained a vast priesthood. Attempts to revive the Sethite teachings were short-lived. While Imhotep, an Andite architectural genius, served as prime minister, the first stone pyramid in Egypt was raised, but internal war and the influx of outside tribes set social progress back for more than five hundred years. (80:6.4, 80:6.5)

VII. Andites of the Mediterranean Isles — Crete and Greece

About 12,000 B.C. a smaller-statured tribe of Andites crossed to Crete and founded a civilization skilled in textiles, metals, pottery, plumbing, and stone building. Almost two thousand years later Sato, a direct descendant of Adamson and Ratta, led a company of Adamsonites to Greece, and those who finally settled there were three hundred and seventy-five chosen people. The text explains that this lineage is why the Greeks had traditions of descent from gods. (80:7.2, 80:7.3, 80:7.4, 80:7.5)

The Aegean region passed through five distinct cultural stages, each less spiritual than the preceding (80:7.6)

In Crete the “great mother” cult glorifying Eve spread widely, and it later entered early Christianity under the guise of the veneration of Mary. By about 6500 B.C. the spiritual heritage of the Andites had greatly declined. By 5000 B.C. the three purest strains of Adam’s descendants were in Sumeria, northern Europe, and Greece. (80:7.7, 80:7.8, 80:7.9)

And this decadence of Andite civilization, together with the disappearance of their religious standards, left the spiritually impoverished races of the world in a deplorable condition. (80:7.8)

Even amid decline the migrations did not stop. About 5000 B.C. progressive Mesopotamians settled Cyprus, only to be wiped out some two thousand years later by northern hordes, and another great colony arose near the later site of Carthage. As Egypt declined, able families moved to Crete and then on to Greece. The Greeks were not only great teachers and artists but the world’s greatest traders and colonizers, planting many outposts of culture to the west. (80:7.10, 80:7.11, 80:7.12, 80:7.13)

VIII. The Danubian Andonites

Between the two branches of the white race that spread north and west lay the broad-headed descendants of the Andonites in the central mountain regions; the ancient Hittites came from this stock. The Danubians, who moved north through the Balkans along the Danube, were farmers and herders who made pottery, and they deteriorated rapidly as they moved away from the source of their culture. Through missionaries from Crete they became mother worshipers, and they practiced cremation of the dead. (80:8.1, 80:8.2, 80:8.4, 80:8.5)

In southern France and Spain the New Stone Age went together with sun worship, and great stones were raised to the sun, as later at Stonehenge. In Brittany, after more than fifteen hundred years of Christianity, people still keep New Stone Age charms and thunderstones. It shows how stubbornly old beliefs survive. (80:9.13, 80:9.14)

But it is a fallacy to presume to classify the white peoples as Nordic, Alpine, and Mediterranean. (80:9.15)

The text says that distinctions once fairly clear have become impossible to identify because of widespread blending; even in 3000 B.C. the social groups were no more of one race than the present inhabitants of North America. Here the text itself draws a boundary against treating racial types as fixed categories. (80:9.15, 80:9.16)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 80 shows that the roots of Western civilization lie in the meeting and mixing of many peoples, and in the accidents of climate and terrain. At the same time it records again and again that wherever civilization advanced, decline followed as spiritual standards faded. The paper quietly teaches that achievements in technique and art alone cannot preserve a civilization. (80:7.6, 80:7.8)
