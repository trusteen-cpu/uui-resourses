Introduction — The Most Divine Affection in All Creation

Papers 107 to 109 dealt with the nature and mission of the Adjusters and their relation to universe creatures; Paper 110 narrows its focus to the single person. The author opens by observing that giving freedom to imperfect beings brings inevitable tragedy, and that it is the nature of perfect Deity to share those sufferings in loving companionship. (110:0.1)

I regard the love and devotion of a Thought Adjuster as the most truly divine affection in all creation. (110:0.2)

The love of the Sons for the races is superb, but the devotion of an Adjuster to the individual is sublime and divinely Fatherlike. The Paradise Father seems to have reserved this form of personal contact with his individual creatures as his own exclusive Creator prerogative. (110:0.2)

I. Indwelling the Mortal Mind — A Guest Whose Tabernacle Is the Body

The Adjuster indwells not the brain, a physical organ, but the mortal mind. Indirectly and unrecognized, it is constantly communicating with us, especially when the mind makes worshipful contact with spirit in the superconsciousness. The author repeatedly appeals to his readers to love their Adjusters more and co-operate with them more fully. (110:1.1, 110:1.2)

The Adjuster’s chief concern is our eternal future, but it is not indifferent to our temporal welfare. It is delighted to contribute to our health, happiness, and true prosperity, so long as these do not harm our eternal progress. Yet it is passive about purely temporal matters and divinely active in all that concerns eternity. (110:1.3, 110:1.4)

If this courtship is faithfully lived, there will at last follow a never-ending blending of identity. That oneness is so complete that even the most experienced personalities can never distinguish the fusion partners as separate identities. Small acts of faithfulness today prepare for that eternal union. (110:1.6)

II. Adjusters and Human Will — A Plan Foreordained but Never Forced

The Adjuster comes bearing an ideal life plan determined beforehand on Divinington. Yet no one is obliged to accept it. We are subjects of predestination, but it is not foreordained that we must accept that predestination. The Adjuster respects the sovereignty of personality and is always subservient to our will. (110:2.1)

survival is a gift of the Gods which must be desired by the creatures of time. (110:2.2)

The Adjuster does not control our thinking as such. Influencing human thought directly is our exclusive personality prerogative; the Adjuster improves and co-ordinates our thinking processes and, above all, builds morontia transcripts of our careers. When our minds see eye to eye with the Adjuster’s and our will carries out the decision, the Adjuster’s prepersonal will attains personality expression through our decision. (110:2.3, 110:2.5)

Two realities, then, are centered in the human mind circuits: a mortal self evolved from the plans of the Life Carriers, and an immortal gift from Divinington. To the extent that their identity is realized, we approach the morontia order of existence. The key is a will, human in origin, that is becoming divine through identifying the human mind with the mindedness of God. (110:2.4, 110:2.6)

III. Co-operation with the Adjuster — Devotion That Goes with Joy

The Adjuster’s success depends less on the theories of our beliefs than on our decisions, determinations, and steadfast faith. The Adjuster itself never fails; we speak of success or failure only in terms of human survival. The author regrets that so many spend so much time on the trifles of living while overlooking the essential work of co-operating with the Adjuster. (110:3.2, 110:3.3, 110:3.4)

the ideal life is one of loving service rather than an existence of fearful apprehension. (110:3.4)

Confusion and discouragement do not necessarily mean resistance. Neither ignorance nor confusional doubts can prevent survival; only conscious resistance to the Adjuster’s leading can. Co-operation is not a particularly conscious process; it consists of motives, decisions, and supreme desires, and it can be consciously augmented in four ways. (110:3.5, 110:3.6)

Only conscious resistance to the Adjuster's leading can prevent the survival of the evolving immortal soul. (110:3.5)

IV–V. The Adjuster’s Work in the Mind; Erroneous Concepts of Adjuster Guidance

The Adjuster is in full touch with the wisdom and truth of the universes, but for lack of commonness of nature it can transmit little of it to the mortal mind. A thought that arrives suddenly may at times be the Adjuster’s work, but far more often it is an idea that has been forming in the subconscious. The Adjuster’s revelations appear not through the subconscious but through the superconscious. (110:4.1, 110:4.3)

Trust all matters of mind beyond the dead level of consciousness to the custody of the Adjusters. (110:4.4)

Even when the Adjuster manages to flash a gleam of new truth, it often precipitates a convulsion of fanaticism or an intellectual upheaval. Many a new religion and strange “ism” has arisen from garbled Adjuster communications. So the paper draws clear distinctions about what not to take for the Adjuster’s voice. (110:4.5, 110:5.1)

But a human being would do better to err in rejecting an Adjuster's expression through believing it to be a purely human experience than to blunder into exalting a reaction of the mortal mind to the sphere of divine dignity. (110:5.5)

The human being through whom this revelation came teaches the same lesson. He had a highly experienced Adjuster but was almost completely indifferent to its outward manifestations, and that very indifference gave the Adjuster a wide scope of activity. Living calmly is a healthier path than chasing spiritual phenomena. (110:5.7)

VI. The Seven Psychic Circles — A Ladder of Balanced Growth

The sum of personality realization on a material world lies in the successive conquest of the seven psychic circles. Entering the seventh circle marks the beginning of true human personality function; completing the first marks relative maturity. The circles are neither purely intellectual nor wholly morontial, and passing through them requires the harmonious functioning of the whole personality. (110:6.1, 110:6.3)

When intellect outpaces spirituality, communication with the Adjuster becomes difficult and dangerous; overspiritual development, on the other hand, leads to fanatical interpretations of spirit leading. When body, mind, and spirit grow in harmony, the most light and truth can be imparted with the least risk. And not only the weight of a decision matters, but also the number of decisions and their persistent repetition, which make the response a sure habit. (110:6.4, 110:6.6)

VII. The Attainment of Immortality — The Day the Two Become One

Attaining the seven circles is not the same as fusion. Fusion comes when the mortal will is finally and completely attuned to the will of God as it resides in the Adjuster. When fusion occurs during physical life, the material body is instantly consumed, and those watching would see the mortal disappear “in chariots of fire.” (110:7.1, 110:7.2)

After fusion, all experience and all values become the possession of both, so that the two are actually one entity. But for the vast majority of Urantians the Adjuster must patiently await the deliverance of death. So few mortals are real thinkers, and the ear of the human mind is almost deaf to the spiritual pleas the Adjuster translates. (110:7.4, 110:7.6)

Adjusters rejoice to contact the mortal mind, but because of animal resistance they must endure long years of silent sojourn. Those below the third and second circles seldom hear the Adjuster’s direct voice except in moments of supreme desire and after a supreme decision. Put the other way around, lofty desire and decision open the door to hearing that voice. (110:7.7, 110:7.9)

At the end of the paper, the author shares the plea of a self-acting Adjuster to its human partner in the reserve corps of destiny: to co-operate more faithfully, to endure its tasks more cheerfully, and not to disappoint it. (110:7.10)

On the human will our achievement of personality depends. (110:7.10)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 110 invites us to see the Adjuster not only as an object of awe but as our closest companion, waiting for our co-operation. Co-operation is not a mystical experience; it is made of everyday motives, decisions, and repetition. The Adjuster’s voice is always within us, and it grows clearer in moments of supreme desire and decision. (110:3.6, 110:7.9)
