Introduction — Our Unseen Neighbors

In Paper 77 an Archangel of Nebadon introduces the midway creatures of Urantia. They are a unique group existing on a life-functioning level about midway between mortals and the angelic orders. They may seem an accident of time, yet they are accepted as one of the essential orders of planetary ministry. On Urantia there are two orders: the primary midwayers, who appeared in the days of Dalamatia, and the secondary midwayers, whose origin dates from the times of Adam. (77:0.1, 77:0.2)

existing on a life-functioning level about midway between those of the mortals of the realms and of the angelic orders; hence are they called midway creatures. (77:0.1)

The paper follows two lines of descent. After telling of the birth of the primary midwayers, it turns back to the Nodites and the tower of Babel, who supplied half the ancestry of the secondary midwayers, and then to Adamson and Ratta, who supplied the other half. It then treats in turn the rebellion, the union of the two orders, and their mission as permanent citizens.

I. The Primary Midwayers — An Origin Hard to Explain

The one hundred corporeal members of Prince Caligastia’s staff were, as morontia citizens of Jerusem, supermaterial beings without reproductive prerogatives, yet as ministers on Urantia they were material sex creatures. The narrator frankly admits being unable to explain satisfactorily how they could function as parents on a supermaterial level. But that is exactly what happened. (77:1.2)

A supermaterial (nonsexual) liaison of a male and a female member of the corporeal staff resulted in the appearance of the first-born of the primary midwayers. (77:1.2)

Once the usefulness of the first fifty was established, the Prince, after a year of observation, authorized reproduction without restriction. Each couple produced one every half year until a thousand had been born to it, and then the power was exhausted; thus came the original corps of 50,000. They served as the intelligence corps of the Prince’s administration, ranging far and wide to study the races. The rebellion ensnared a little over four fifths of them, and the loyal corps served the Melchizedek receivers under the leadership of Van. (77:1.4, 77:1.5, 77:1.6, 77:1.7)

II. The Nodite Race — An Eighth Race from Modified Germ Plasm

Because the staff carried germ plasm of the Andonic strains, their offspring were expected to resemble other Andonite children. But when the sixty rebels who followed Nod had children, an unexpected difference appeared. The text traces it to the life circuits of the Satania system acting in the bodies of the staff and altering the germ plasm, likening the process to the way scientists modify germ plasm with X rays. (77:2.4, 77:2.5)

This unexpected excellence characterized not only physical and intellectual qualities but also spiritual capacities. (77:2.4)

The sixty rebel staff members and the forty-four modified Andonites who joined the rebellion—104 in all—became the ancestors of the Nodites, the eighth race of Urantia. They gradually mingled with the evolutionary peoples, and ten thousand years after the rebellion their life span was little more than that of the other races. The text explains the long reigns of the Sumerian king lists and the record of a man who lived over nine hundred “years” as traditions arising from the long lives of early rulers and from confusing the twenty-eight-day month with the year. (77:2.8, 77:2.9, 77:2.10, 77:2.11)

The ancient tale of “sons of the gods” taking the daughters of men also begins here. Evolutionary mortals regarded the staff and their descendants in that way, and tradition even magnified their stature. It is an interpretation that finds traces of real history at the bottom of myth. (77:2.3)

III. The Tower of Babel — A Community Divided over Purpose

After Dalamatia sank, the Nodites founded Dilmun. About fifty thousand years after Nod’s death, as their numbers grew and intermarriage with neighboring tribes spread, the leaders adopted the plan of Bablot and began a temple with a great tower. But after four and one-half years a bitter dispute arose over the purpose of the tower, and all work stopped. (77:3.1, 77:3.2, 77:3.4)

About twelve thousand years ago the Andites tried to raise a new temple on the ruins, but it too failed. The lesson of this section is that a plan to glorify ourselves scattered the community. (77:3.8, 77:3.9)

IV. Nodite Centers of Civilization

The internal war over the tower dispersed the Nodites, and their culture declined for over one hundred and twenty thousand years until the Adamic infusion. Even so, in the times of Adam they were still an able people, and members of this line were among the Garden builders and on Adam’s staff. (77:4.1)

The text uses this lineage to explain why the Sumerians appeared suddenly with a mature culture. They remembered Dilmun as a paradise where the Gods first showed mankind civilized life. The northern Vanites taught that their seven commandments had been given to Van on Mount Ararat, and these regional traditions were later woven into the story of Noah’s flood. (77:4.7, 77:4.8, 77:4.11, 77:4.12)

V–VI. Adamson and Ratta; The Secondary Midwayers

The other half of the secondary midwayers’ ancestry comes from Adam’s eldest son, Adamson. He set out with twenty-seven companions in search of the northern highland people he had heard of from Van and Amadon, and after a little over three years he found and married Ratta, who claimed to be the last pure-line descendant of the Prince’s staff. His departure was pleasant, yet Adam and Eve grieved deeply at losing their eldest son. (77:5.2, 77:5.4, 77:5.5)

The couple had sixty-seven children, and every fourth child was of a unique order, often invisible. Ratta was greatly perturbed, but Adamson, knowing of the primary midwayers, concluded that something similar was happening, and he mated these children. (77:5.6)

this is the origin of the secondary order of midwayers. (77:5.6)

Sixteen of these unique children formed eight couples, each able by a combined sex and nonsex liaison to produce a secondary midwayer every seventy days; each couple produced 248, for a total of 1,984. The sixteen parents lived and died as mortals, but their offspring live on, free of the limitations of the flesh. With these helpers the Adamsonites maintained a high culture for almost seven thousand years. (77:6.2, 77:6.3, 77:6.4, 77:5.9)

And such a phenomenon was never possible on earth before that time, nor has it ever occurred since. (77:6.2)

VII. The Rebel Midwayers — The Reality and the End of Possession

A table makes the comparison of the two orders clear. Most of the primary midwayers sided with Caligastia, while the secondary midwayers, after Adamson’s death, drifted without organization until they made their final decisions in the time of Michael. (77:7.1, 77:7.2, 77:6.6)

Older writers called the rebel midwayers evil spirits and demons. Before Michael came, they could influence the minds of certain inferior mortals and somewhat control their actions. Yet even before Pentecost they could not dominate a normal human mind, and since the Spirit of Truth was poured out even the weakest minds are free from that possibility. The whole group of rebel midwayers is now held prisoner. (77:7.4, 77:7.5, 77:7.7, 77:7.8)

Since the day of Pentecost there never again can be such a thing as demoniacal possession. (77:7.8)

This teaching asks for a twofold balance: do not dismiss the demoniacal possession of former ages as a mere figure of speech, but do not blame present-day mental distress on demons either. The text states plainly that the supposed casting out of devils since the coming of the Spirit of Truth has confused such belief with hysteria and insanity. (77:7.6, 77:7.7)

VIII. The United Midwayers — Four Services Beside the Seraphim

After Michael’s planetary adjudication the two orders were joined into one shortly after Pentecost, and 10,992 now serve as the United Midwayers, governed alternately by the senior member of each order. They neither eat nor sleep nor reproduce, yet they enjoy human humor and worship and enter into the spirit of our work and play. (77:8.1, 77:8.3)

Midwayers are not men, neither are they angels, but secondary midwayers are, in nature, nearer man than angel (77:8.4)

Because the secondary midwayers can make physical contact with material things, they performed many of the more literal phenomena ascribed to angels. Their chief work today is to serve as unperceived liaison associates of the men and women of the reserve corps of destiny, and they helped bring about the co-ordination that made this revelation possible. But they have nothing to do with so-called spiritualism or mediumship. (77:8.11, 77:8.12, 77:8.13)

IX. The Permanent Citizens of Urantia

The seraphim come and go, but the midwayers remain. As natives and ministers of the planet they provide the one continuing regime that links the changing seraphic administrations. Any of them could leave the planet, but they have pledged not to until released, and the only loyal midwayer ever to depart Urantia is 1-2-3 the first, now one of the twenty-four counselors on Jerusem. (77:9.2, 77:9.4, 77:9.5)

Their determination is suggested by the motto of their order: “What the United Midwayers undertake, the United Midwayers do.” (77:9.3)

Midwayers are brought into being fully developed, yet they never cease to grow in wisdom and experience. The text says that the civilization of Urantia is the joint product of mortals and midwayers. Human generations pass, but the midwayers keep the memory—and it was from that memory that the story of Jesus’ life was given to us. (77:9.7, 77:9.8, 77:9.9)

The generations of men forget; the corps of midwayers remembers, and that memory is the treasure house of the traditions of your inhabited world. (77:9.9)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 77 shows that even amid stories of rebellion and failure the way of loyalty was always open. Some of the primary midwayers stood firm with Van and 1-2-3 the first, and most of the secondary midwayers made the right choice in Michael’s day. They are now our elder brethren and comrades, and they will do their part until the day when peace does reign on earth. (77:9.5, 77:9.10)
