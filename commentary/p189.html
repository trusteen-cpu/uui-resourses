Introduction — What No Creature Could Do

Shortly after Jesus was buried, the chief of the archangels then on Urantia convened a council of resurrection on his own initiative to consider how Jesus might be restored. It was not Gabriel who called them; Michael’s creatures came together on their own responsibility. By about midnight they had concluded that there was nothing creatures could do to assist their Creator’s resurrection. (189:0.1)

Gabriel reminded them that Michael had laid down his life of his own free will and therefore had the power to take it up again. Then Jesus’ Personalized Adjuster, who was directing the celestial hosts, explained to those waiting what was now taking place. (189:0.1, 189:0.2)

That which you observe is the mortal transit of Jesus of Nazareth from life in the flesh to life in the morontia. (189:0.2)

The Adjuster told them that their Creator-father had chosen to pass through the whole experience of his mortal creatures — from birth on a material world, through natural death and morontia resurrection, into true spirit existence. From Gabriel down to the humblest cherubim, all waited patiently without understanding. (189:0.2, 189:0.3)

I. The Morontia Transit — Stone and Seal Undisturbed

At a quarter to three on Sunday morning, the incarnation commission of seven Paradise personalities took its place around the tomb. At ten minutes before three, intense vibrations of combined material and morontia activities began to issue from the tomb, and at two minutes past three on April 9, A.D. 30, the resurrected morontia form and personality of Jesus came forth from the tomb. (189:1.1)

Yet the body of flesh in which he had lived for almost thirty-six years still lay in the niche, wrapped in linen. The stone had not been moved, Pilate’s seal was unbroken, and the guards noticed nothing. At this point the narrator raises the question of how matter, morontia, and spirit are related. (189:1.2, 189:1.3)

How long before you will regard time as the moving image of eternity and space as the fleeting shadow of Paradise realities? (189:1.3)

The text says that no creature of this universe and no personality of any other universe had any part in this resurrection. The seven Paradise personalities surrounded the tomb but did nothing. He accomplished it all by a power that lay within himself. (189:1.4, 189:1.5, 189:1.13)

And he has done all this through power inherent within himself; no personality has rendered him any assistance. (189:1.13)

The risen Jesus first told Gabriel to continue in charge of universe affairs, had his brotherly greetings conveyed to Immanuel, and asked for the certification of the Ancients of Days. Then he spoke the first words of his postmortal career to the morontia groups of the seven mansion worlds who had gathered to welcome him. (189:1.10)

Having finished my life in the flesh, I would tarry here for a short time in transition form that I may more fully know the life of my ascendant creatures and further reveal the will of my Father in Paradise. (189:1.10)

Jesus then began his induction into morontia life, a process that took more than an hour of earth time and was twice interrupted by his wish to communicate with his former associates in the flesh who came to look into the empty tomb. That the sovereign of a universe, in the very first hour of his new career, turned first to his grieving friends shows how personal his love is. (189:1.12)

II. The Material Body of Jesus — Why the Tomb Was Empty

At ten minutes past three, the chief of archangels asked Gabriel for custody of Jesus’ mortal remains. The celestial hosts did not want to remember the slow decay of the human form of their universe’s Creator. When permission was given, they took charge of the body with the help of the midwayers, since a material body could not pass out of the sealed tomb as the morontia form had done. (189:2.1, 189:2.2, 189:2.3)

When the secondary midwayers rolled away the huge, millstone-like stone, the guards saw it move apparently of its own accord and fled in terror. The leaders, on hearing their report, did not punish the guards; instead they paid each of the twenty to say that the disciples had taken the body away in the night. (189:2.4, 189:2.5)

It was indeed a fact that the tomb was empty, but this is not the truth of the resurrection. (189:2.6)
Source 189:2.7

Here the text offers a principle of thinking. Individual facts may be materially true, yet a conclusion drawn by joining them is not necessarily true spiritually. The truth of faith rests not on a combination of apparent facts but on living spiritual experience. (189:2.6, 189:2.9)

Although individual facts may be materially true, it does not follow that the association of a group of facts must necessarily lead to truthful spiritual conclusions. (189:2.6)

III. The Dispensational Resurrection — The Roll Call of the Sleeping

About half past four in the morning, as Gabriel prepared for the general resurrection that would end the Adamic dispensation, the morontia Michael appeared and, declaring that the Father had given the Son to have life in himself, told him to begin the planetary roll call of resurrection. The circuit of the archangels operated from Urantia for the first time, and the survivors who had fallen asleep since the days of Adam appeared in the resurrection halls of mansonia. (189:3.1, 189:3.2)

By the mandate of Michael, let the dead of a Urantia dispensation rise! (189:3.2)

Ordinarily their seraphic guardians would have been present in mansonia when they awoke, but on this occasion they were still on this world, because Gabriel had to remain on Urantia in connection with Jesus’ resurrection. That the resurrection of one person coincided with the awakening of so many sleepers adds weight to this section. (189:3.2)

When the roll call began, the Personalized Adjuster relinquished command of the celestial hosts and departed for Salvington to register the completion of Michael’s mortal transit. Only Gabriel remained on Urantia with the morontia Jesus. The narrator explains that this account is written not from the limited human viewpoint but from that of those who actually saw these things. (189:3.4, 189:3.5)

IV. Discovery of the Empty Tomb — “Mary”

Early that morning ten apostles were asleep in the upper room of Mark’s home. Thomas, unable to bear the sight of his fellows, had gone off to Bethpage to grieve alone. The text says the apostles’ distress arose less from doubt and despair than from fear, grief, and shame. (189:4.1)

A little before three, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of the Alpheus twins, Salome, Joanna, and Susanna set out for the tomb with spices and bandages. They knew nothing of the guards or the seal. They were astonished to find the stone rolled away, and Mary Magdalene boldly went in and saw the empty niche and the grave cloths lying in order. (189:4.2, 189:4.3, 189:4.4, 189:4.6)

As they went out through the Damascus gate, they paused when they met soldiers fleeing in terror toward the city, but then they hurried on. The faithfulness of these women, determined even in their grief to care for the Master’s body to the end, brought them to be the first to meet the risen Lord. (189:4.3, 189:4.5)

At Mary’s scream the other women fled, but Joanna, ashamed to have deserted Mary, brought them back. They supposed the body had been moved, yet they could not explain the undisturbed grave cloths. Then a stranger appeared, and Mary took him to be the gardener. (189:4.7, 189:4.9, 189:4.10)

When he called her by name in his familiar voice, “Mary,” she knew him and fell to her knees. His appearance was so changed that she had not recognized him, but a single call of her name made him known. Human eyes could behold the morontia form only through the special ministry of the transformers and the midwayers. (189:4.10, 189:4.11)

Touch me not, Mary, for I am not as you knew me in the flesh. (189:4.12)

These words were not a rejection but guidance into a new relationship. Jesus was no longer a man of the flesh but a morontia being who would remain with them for a season before ascending to the Father. We can read them as a call to move beyond the wish to hold on to a loved one as he once was, and to accept a meeting on a higher level. (189:4.12)

Jesus told the women to go and tell his apostles — and Peter — that he had risen. At first the apostles thought it was a vision and would not believe, but when Peter heard that he had been named, he rushed out of the upper room, with John following. (189:4.12, 189:4.13)

V. Peter and John at the Tomb — Between Fear and Hope

As Peter ran, his mind swung between fear and hope. He was afraid to meet the Master, but the news that a message had been sent especially to him revived his hope. Only then did the promise of the third day come back to his mind. In John’s soul there welled up an ecstasy of joy and hope. (189:5.1)

John, arriving first, looked in from the entrance, while Peter went straight inside. Peter thought the enemies had stolen the body, but John replied that if it had been stolen, the tomb would not have been left in such order. They went in again and examined the grave cloths closely. (189:5.2, 189:5.3)

After they left, Mary, discouraged because no one would believe her, came back to the tomb, and as she wept the Master appeared to her again. (189:5.3)

Be not doubting; have the courage to believe what you have seen and heard. (189:5.4)

When Mary told them again, the apostles still did not believe; but when Peter and John returned, they no longer mocked her and were filled with fear and apprehension. So the news of the resurrection began with the testimony of women. (189:5.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 189 presents the resurrection not as a mysterious miracle story but as the Creator going first along the path every mortal will walk. Jesus came forth in the same morontia form as the ascenders of the mansion worlds. His resurrection is therefore the most concrete promise of our own survival after death. (189:1.9, 189:0.2)
