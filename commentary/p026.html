Introduction — The Highest Order of the Angelic Hosts

Paper 26 turns to the lowest group among the children of the Infinite Spirit, the ministering spirits, that is, the angelic hosts. The highest of these are the ministering spirits of Paradise and the central universe, the supernaphim. The paper traces, circuit by circuit, how the supernaphim guide the pilgrims of time through the seven circles of Havona. (26:0.1)

So this paper is both a study of angels and a guidebook to the last school of the ascending mortal. Havona is a place we must one day actually pass through, and the paper shows in advance what we will learn there, what tests we will face, and who will be beside us. (26:3.2)

I. The Ministering Spirits — An Unbroken Chain of Ministry

Angels are the ministering associates of evolving and ascending will creatures, and every order consists of distinct, highly individualized personalities. They are very social and enjoy seasons of rest and change. Angelic status is not wholly fixed: angels may become Paradise Companions, Celestial Recorders, or Technical Advisers, and evolutionary seraphim may even attain the spiritual levels of the ascending sons. (26:1.1, 26:1.10)

Of these, the supernaphim, seconaphim, and seraphim are the ones employed on a large scale in the ascension plan. Working respectively in the central universe, the superuniverses, and the local universes, they form a single chain of ministry to all who seek the Father through the Eternal Son. Angels are sustained directly by the spiritual energy of the primary circuits of the universe, and they take in knowledge in much the same way. (26:1.15, 26:1.17)

II. The Mighty Supernaphim — Three Orders and Their Origins

The primary supernaphim are offspring of the Conjoint Creator. They divide their ministry almost equally between the Paradise Citizens and the ascending pilgrims, helping each group understand the other: the one the highest of perfect will creatures, the other beings who have evolved to perfection from the lowest types. Their work is described in the next paper. (26:2.2, 26:2.3)

The secondary supernaphim are created individually by each of the Seven Master Spirits and so fall into seven types. The tertiary supernaphim are made by the seven Circuit Spirits under the authority of the Infinite Spirit. The Circuit Spirits made comparatively few tertiary supernaphim before the pilgrims came, but the Master Spirits did not even begin creating secondary supernaphim until Grandfanda arrived. Here we glimpse a creative capacity that responds to the growth of the experiential Deities and the evolving universes. (26:2.5, 26:2.6, 26:2.7)

III. The Tertiary Supernaphim — Keeping Havona Running Smoothly

In Havona the pilgrims of time pass inward from the outer seventh circuit, while the descending pilgrims of Paradise pass outward from the first. There is no time limit on any circuit, and although some ascenders are excused from parts of the local universe training worlds, no one escapes passing through all seven circuits of Havona. (26:3.1, 26:3.2)

IV. The Secondary Supernaphim — Arriving with Perfection of Purpose

The secondary supernaphim serve the pilgrims in seven working groups on the seven circuits of Havona. A pilgrim is always tutored by supernaphim who originate in the Master Spirit presiding over his own superuniverse of nativity. We of Orvonton, then, will be understood and comforted by teachers who come from the same Master Spirit as we do. (26:4.2, 26:4.10)

The pilgrim arrives on the receiving world of Havona with only one kind of perfection: perfection of purpose. That purpose has been thoroughly proved, the faith has been tested, and the pilgrim is known to be disappointment proof. Now, inside the door that faith has opened, perfection of understanding must be developed. (26:4.12, 26:4.13, 26:4.14)

Ability to comprehend is the mortal passport to Paradise. Willingness to believe is the key to Havona. (26:4.15)

V–VI. The Pilgrim Helpers and the Supremacy Guides — First Lessons and the Experience of the Supreme

The pilgrim helpers welcome the much-traveled ascenders to the settled worlds of Havona and teach them the elementary course on the Trinity, the Father-Son partnership, and the Infinite Spirit. The paper adds that long before reaching Havona these pilgrims have learned to turn uncertainty, disappointment, and difficulty into nourishment for growth. (26:5.1, 26:5.2, 26:5.3)

“In liaison with God, nothing—absolutely nothing—is impossible.” (26:5.3)

In Havona time matters little; achievement is the final test. On the sixth circle the ascenders, now “spiritual graduates,” come under the supremacy guides and reach a new realization of God the Supreme. No personalized presence of Supremacy can be perceived, yet they undergo a new integration of consciousness and a new sensitivity for divinity. The author says it looks as if God the Supreme were affectionately bestowing enhancements upon his experiential children. (26:5.5, 26:6.1, 26:6.3)

VII–VIII. The Trinity Guides and the Son Finders — The Deity Adventure and the Meaning of Failure

On the fifth circle the pilgrim becomes a “candidate for the Deity adventure” and discovers, for the first time, what real study and mental effort are. Each pilgrim receives the undivided attention and affection of a Trinity guide. When he finally sets out for Paradise, he goes with the transit trio: his superaphic circle associate, the Graduate Guide, and the Guide’s servital associate. The quest for the Infinite Spirit seldom fails. (26:7.1, 26:7.2, 26:7.4, 26:7.6)

The fourth circuit is the “circle of the Sons,” where the pilgrims of time and the pilgrims of eternity first truly understand one another. After the Infinite Spirit is attained there are no more examinations; progress is decided purely by individual spirituality. And if a pilgrim fails, no one is ever chided. (26:8.1, 26:8.3)

On Paradise, disappointment is never regarded as defeat; postponement is never looked upon as disgrace; the apparent failures of time are never confused with the significant delays of eternity. (26:8.3)

Defeated candidates serve for no less than a millennium in whichever superuniverse best suits their retraining, then return of their own accord and resume on the circle where their career was interrupted. The secondary supernaphim never fail to pilot their subjects successfully on the second attempt, and the same ministers attend them throughout. (26:8.5)

IX–X. The Father Guides and the Counselors — The Command to Be Perfect Is Obeyed

On the third circle the most experienced Father guides prepare the pilgrims for the final adventure. Attaining the Father is the passport to eternity. At this moment, the author declares, the whole ascension plan has proved feasible, and the justice of the command “Be you perfect, even as I am perfect” has been forever demonstrated. (26:9.1, 26:9.2, 26:9.3)

Step by step, life by life, world by world, the ascendant career has been mastered, and the goal of Deity has been attained. (26:9.4)

On the second circle the counselors and advisers teach the responsibilities of eternity while also comforting the disappointed pilgrims who have been advanced there directly from the circle of their failure. Their failure lay not in a defect of spirit but in some part of their technique of approach or their experiential background. Certified by the councils of perfection, they return joyfully to service in time and become the most loving ministers to those who suffer temporal disappointments. (26:10.2, 26:10.3, 26:10.4)

These are they who have drained the experiential cup to its dregs (26:10.2)

For the successful pilgrims, some of the anticipative enthusiasm fades. Many look back on their long struggle with a joyous envy and wish they could begin it all again, the paper says, just as mortals in old age sometimes long to relive the struggles of their youth. (26:10.5)

XI. The Complements of Rest — No One Sleeps Alone

On the first circle the ascenders learn to adjust to the many groups of beings they will meet on Paradise. Here also are gathered the conjoint corps of the creature-trinitized sons, preparing for future work. The complements of rest care less about training than about promoting understanding fellowship among these various groups. (26:11.1, 26:11.2, 26:11.3)

At this point the author corrects a common misunderstanding. It is a mistake to call divine mercy and tenderness toward the weak a sign of a manlike God. Rather, the mercy and forbearance shown by human beings are evidence that the spirit of the living God dwells within them. (26:11.5)

The mortal creature must find God. The Creator Son never stops until he finds man—the lowest will creature. (26:11.5)

When we fall asleep on the pilot world of the last circuit, we will not be alone. The complement of rest who has been our associate since we reached the first circle comes to our side and enters the rest as one with us. Only those who go to sleep on the first circle of Havona and awaken on the shores of Paradise become children of eternity. (26:11.6, 26:11.7)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 26 pictures Havona not as an examination hall but as a school woven of love. The pilgrim arrives carrying only perfection of purpose, yet on every circuit a teacher who understands him is waiting; failure brings no reproach; and even the last sleep is shared. The disappointments and uncertainties we face now are part of building the faith that will admit us to that school. (26:4.13, 26:8.3, 26:11.7)
