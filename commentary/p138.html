Introduction — A Widening Gulf with the Family

On the afternoon of the sermon on the kingdom that closed Paper 137, Jesus called only the six apostles together and began to unfold his plans for visiting the cities around the Sea of Galilee. His brothers James and Jude were deeply hurt not to be invited. Jesus had already decided to have no close relatives in this corps of apostolic directors. (138:0.1)

This decision, together with his apparent aloofness from his mother since Cana, became the starting point of an ever-widening gulf between Jesus and his family. Those differences were not fully removed until after his death and resurrection; only Ruth, the youngest, remained unswervingly loyal. So this paper is, on one side, the story of how the twelve were gathered and trained, and on the other, the story of a man not understood by those closest to him. (138:0.1)

If a prophet is not without honor save in his own country, he is not without understanding appreciation save in his own family. (138:0.2)

I. Final Instructions — Two by Two, Person to Person

On June 23 Jesus sent the six apostles out two by two. They were not to baptize, and he advised against public preaching. He wanted them first to gain practical experience in dealing personally with their fellow men. The apostles were somewhat disappointed, but they partly understood his reason and set out in good heart. (138:1.1)

Before they left, Jesus made a new announcement. He intended to ordain twelve apostles to carry on the work after his departure, and each of the six was to choose one man from among the early converts. The six discussed this at length, amid fear and timidity, and in the end Andrew’s counsel prevailed. (138:1.2, 138:1.3)

The Master is right; we are too few to encompass this work. There is need for more teachers (138:1.3)

Meanwhile Jesus visited his family in Nazareth and did everything humanly possible to keep their confidence and affection. He was strongly tempted to use his powers to free John from prison, but once more he chose to wait upon the Father’s will. (138:1.4, 138:1.5)

II. Choosing the Six — Religion as Personal Experience

The first missionary tour was a great success. The apostles discovered the value of direct, personal contact, and they came back understanding more fully that religion is, after all, purely a matter of personal experience. They began to sense how hungry ordinary people were for words of comfort and spiritual good cheer. On their return Andrew took charge, called on each in turn for his report and nomination, and all six candidates were accepted unanimously. (138:2.1, 138:2.2)

The apostles now saw the wisdom of the Master’s plan to have them work quietly and personally before launching any public effort. The principle is that one learns first from meeting a single person, not from a large stage. (138:2.10)

III. The Call of Matthew and Simon — A Feast with Publicans and Sinners

Jesus said “Follow me” to Matthew, who was waiting for him at the toll house, and with the same words he called Simon the Zealot at his old place of business, now run by his nephew. That evening Matthew gave a banquet at his home, and many of his guests would have been classed by the Pharisees as “publicans and sinners.” As was the custom, many Pharisees lingered about to watch how Jesus would behave. (138:3.1, 138:3.3, 138:3.4, 138:3.5)

As the gathering grew merry, one Pharisee criticized Jesus to Peter. Before giving the parting blessing, Jesus answered. (138:3.6)

I have come to proclaim joy to the socially downtrodden and spiritual liberty to the moral captives. (138:3.6)

A man of righteous character mingling freely and joyously with people of poor reputation was a strange sight in all Jewry. Andrew persuaded Simon not to make a speech, so that the coming kingdom would not be confused with the Zealots’ movement. As the guests went home, they spoke of only one thing: the goodness and friendliness of Jesus. (138:3.7, 138:3.8)

IV–V. The Call of the Twins; The Call of Thomas and Judas — A Door Wide Open

On the shore at Kheresa the twins James and Judas were called. That afternoon Jesus instructed the apostles about attending festive gatherings and told them never to refuse to eat with anyone. This teaching answered the criticism of Matthew’s banquet with a principle. (138:4.1, 138:4.2)

No man may close the door of mercy in the face of any hungry soul who may seek to gain an entrance thereto. (138:4.2)

At Tarichea Thomas and Judas Iscariot were called, and Jesus spoke differently to each. He received Thomas while knowing his lack of faith, and to Judas he said he prayed that he would always be loyal to his Galilean brethren. Yet the apostles largely failed to understand his teaching about the Holy Spirit and the seraphim. Again and again they made the mistake of trying to fit the new gospel into their old forms of belief. (138:5.1, 138:5.2, 138:5.3)

On that Sabbath Jesus urged them to avoid any clash with the civil authorities. The same evening Judas asked why nothing was being done to get John out of prison, and he was not wholly satisfied with Jesus’ attitude. This small scene casts a shadow over what lies ahead. (138:5.4)

VI. The Week of Intensive Training — A Day of Rest and a Warning Against Bypaths

Through the following week each older apostle took the man he had nominated and reviewed with him all that had been taught, and in the evenings they gathered in Zebedee’s garden to hear Jesus. At this time Jesus made Wednesday a weekly day of rest and recreation. He himself did not need the rest, but he knew it was best for his human associates. (138:6.1, 138:6.2)

My children, go for a day of play. (138:6.2)

Jesus also made clear what the center of their message must be. They were not to be sidetracked into preaching about him, but were to proclaim the gospel of the kingdom and portray his revelation of the Father. The apostles did not understand why, and no one dared to ask. Though he avoided controversy, he never let a wrong concept of the Father go uncorrected. (138:6.3, 138:6.4)

do not be misled into the bypaths of creating legends and building up a cult having to do with beliefs and teachings about my beliefs and teachings. (138:6.3)

Though he possessed unlimited power, he was content with means and persons that seemed insignificant, and he insisted on the most quiet and undramatic way of going about his Father’s business. This refusal to hurry results runs through the whole training of the twelve. (138:6.5)

VII. Another Disappointment — Back to the Nets

Peter, James, and Judas Iscariot came to ask when the kingdom would be set up and what place each of them would hold. Jesus reminded them that he had called them as ambassadors of a spiritual kingdom. Shaken, the apostles went off two by two to pray, and then pledged their loyalty, as Thomas put it, even though they did not fully understand. (138:7.1, 138:7.2, 138:7.3)

Banish from your minds this idea that my kingdom is a rule of power or a reign of glory. (138:7.1)

When it turned out that they hardly had money enough for two weeks, Jesus said it was not the Father’s will to begin this way and sent them fishing for two weeks. His instruction to organize under Andrew’s guidance was their first clear hint that public work lay ahead. Many of the boats they used had been built by Jesus’ own hands. The more they worked with him, the more they loved him. (138:7.4, 138:7.5, 138:7.6)

The more you understand some people, the less you admire them, but of this man, even the less I comprehend him, the more I love him. (138:7.6)

VIII–IX. First Work of the Twelve; Five Months of Testing

From mid-August the apostles fished for two weeks and then spent two weeks in personal work, two by two. Jesus went out at least once with each pair. He taught them to preach forgiveness through faith, without penance or sacrifice, and told them not to speak of John’s work and imprisonment, the voice at the baptism, or the wine at Cana. (138:8.1, 138:8.2, 138:8.5)

The disciples learned early that the Master had a profound respect for every human being he met. In the middle of a deep discourse he would go out to the road to cheer a burdened woman, and he would break off a serious conference to make friends with a child. (138:8.9)

Nothing ever seemed so important to Jesus as the individual human who chanced to be in his immediate presence. (138:8.9)

The five monotonous months were a grueling test, but the apostles endured it. The text says that what held them together to the end was not his superb teachings or his marvelous doings but a human sentiment: friendship-loyalty. In the same months, his family, all but Ruth, practically deserted him. Here the difference shows between those who lived close to him and those who only held expectations from a distance. (138:9.1, 138:9.2)

X. Organization of the Twelve

The apostles organized themselves early on, and each one’s temperament and former occupation show clearly in the task he took. This structure lasted until the desertion of Judas made a reorganization necessary. (138:10.1, 138:10.11)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 138 shows how a great work is prepared quietly. Jesus gave the apostles responsibility for choosing men, had them learn first through personal contact, and gave them a rhythm of work and rest. What grew from it was not a complete grasp of doctrine but a deep friendship with the Master. (138:2.1, 138:9.1)
