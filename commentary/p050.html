Introduction — The First Ruler of an Evolving World

Paper 49 showed the variety of the inhabited worlds; Paper 50 introduces the one who first comes to govern such a world. The Planetary Princes belong to the order of Lanonandek Sons, but their service is so specialized that they are treated as a distinct group. Secondary Lanonandeks certified by the Melchizedeks serve in the constellation reserves and in various assignments before being commissioned as Planetary Princes. (50:0.1)

The signal for sending one comes from the Life Carriers. When they ask for an administrative head for a planet on which they have established life and developed intelligent evolutionary beings, the System Sovereign assigns a ruler. Every planet inhabited by evolutionary mortals has a ruler of this order. The author of the paper is himself a Son of this order, and when he writes of a colleague’s betrayal, his pain shows through. (50:0.2, 50:4.12)

Understanding the Planetary Prince unlocks many chapters of Urantia’s history. Because this world’s prince, Caligastia, joined the rebellion, the sequence of civilization that would have been natural on a normal world was twisted here. So the paper first sketches an average evolutionary world, letting us compare it with Urantia as it is. Only by knowing the normal picture can we see our own world’s wounds clearly. (50:4.11, 50:6.1)

I. Mission of the Princes — The Nearest Son, the Loneliest Post

The Planetary Prince is the last of the orders of personal Sons reaching out from Paradise to humankind. The Infinite Spirit draws near through the angels, and the Universal Father through the Mystery Monitors; but the Planetary Prince is the nearest approach, aside from incarnation, that the Eternal Son and his Sons make to us. On a newly inhabited world he alone represents complete divinity. (50:1.1)

But nearness brings risk. To sympathize with an evolutionary world, its ruler must be of a lower order, close to the human; yet the closer he is, the greater the danger that he will exalt his own mind above the will of the Supreme Rulers. Standing alone as representatives of divinity, these Sons are severely tested, and the fact that the Father is not personally present in the local universe is itself a test of faith and loyalty. (50:1.2, 50:1.3)

But not often do these world princes fail in their missions of organizing and administering the inhabited spheres (50:1.4)

This passage holds up a mirror to anyone in a position of leadership. The greater our authority and the farther we stand from those above us, the easier it is to lose the sense of proportion about our own importance. Even so, most princes pass the test and prepare their planets for the Material Sons and the Paradise Sons who follow. (50:1.3, 50:1.4)

II. Planetary Administration — A Fatherly Rule

The Planetary Prince is under the jurisdiction of Gabriel, the chief executive of Michael, and directly subject to the mandates of the System Sovereign. He may at any time seek the counsel of the Melchizedeks or of the four and twenty counselors of the system—who in Satania are at present all natives of Urantia. The Melchizedeks, however, do not interfere unless they are asked. (50:2.1, 50:2.2)

In their early years the evolutionary planets are ruled largely autocratically. The prince usually has a supreme council of twelve, and his staff, averaging about one thousand, may grow to a hundred thousand or more as the planet advances. Every planet provides tribunals of justice, and the judicial system of the local universe begins in the tribunals of the prince. (50:2.3, 50:2.4, 50:2.5)

the decrees of such courts reflect a highly fatherly and discretionary attitude. (50:2.5)

Yet discretion has its limits. No arbitrary execution is ever carried out without the consent of the Constellation Father. The principle that the Most Highs rule in the kingdoms of men is honored down to the lowest level of planetary government. (50:2.6)

Roving commissions of conciliators supplement the planetary tribunals, and the controllers and transformers can help make celestial beings visible to mortals. The chief reason for bringing ascending mortals onto the prince’s staff is to make communication with the inhabitants easier. Good rule begins not with commands alone but with being understood. (50:2.6, 50:2.7)

III. The Prince’s Corporeal Staff — Ascenders Who Put On Bodies Again

When a prince goes to a young world, he takes with him a group of volunteer ascenders from the system headquarters. They are the material link between the prince and the world races, and Caligastia had one hundred such helpers. They are citizens of the system capital who have not yet fused with their Adjusters; leaving their Adjusters at headquarters, they take on new physical bodies prepared by the Life Carriers. (50:3.1, 50:3.2, 50:3.3)

The staff seldom mate with the world races, but always mate among themselves. Their children join the mortal races only in emergencies and by the prince’s direction, and in that case the grandchildren stand in status with the superior races of their day. The uplifting of a planet’s races is thus planned with care, not left to chance. (50:3.5)

These volunteers fall a whole dispensation behind their Jerusem classmates, but in exchange they gain a rare chapter in the ascending career. It is striking that the choice to turn back and help others, rather than press on ahead, becomes in the end a treasure of unique experience. (50:3.6)

IV. The Planetary Headquarters and Schools — and Caligastia’s Betrayal

The corporeal staff early organize the planetary schools of training and culture, where the cream of the evolutionary races are taught and sent back to teach their own peoples. In the headquarters settlement every home was given abundant land, and students and teachers alike were farmers and gardeners. Their time was divided roughly equally among five pursuits. (50:4.1, 50:4.3)

The prince himself is invisible to mortals, so believing what his staff report is a test of faith. Still, the schools are so well suited to each planet’s needs that the races vie keenly for admission, and the children trained there return home to found new centers of culture. (50:4.9, 50:4.10)

On Urantia, too, the plan was going well. But when Caligastia joined the Lucifer rebellion, he deliberately perverted and poisoned the teaching of every planetary school, and the schools were swiftly and completely wrecked. Even so, many descendants of the staff remained loyal and kept spiritual truth from vanishing altogether. (50:4.11, 50:4.12, 50:4.13)

The work of these loyal evangels helped to prevent the total obliteration of spiritual truth on Urantia. (50:4.13)

V. Progressive Civilization — Seven Epochs of Development

A loyal prince stays permanently with the planet of his first assignment. Paradise Sons come and go, but the prince’s work is to foster planetary civilization steadily. Civilization takes different shapes on different worlds, yet every evolutionary world passes through seven epochs in well-defined directions. (50:5.1, 50:5.2, 50:5.3)

The effort to execute knowledge results in wisdom, and when a culture has learned how to profit and improve by experience, civilization has really arrived. (50:5.7)

This chart can be read against the growth of a single person as well as a society. Once bread and safety are reasonably secure, it is easy to chase comfort, but the next stages are knowledge and wisdom, brotherhood, and spiritual striving. When the era of light and life opens, the prince is elevated to Planetary Sovereign. (50:5.6, 50:5.11)

The description of the third epoch is especially sharp. An age in which luxury vies with necessity is too often marked by tyranny and intolerance, gluttony and drunkenness, and the pleasure-seeking weaklings are finally subdued by the stronger, truth-loving elements. From the fourth epoch education becomes the watchword, and in the fifth people become truly moral beings, able to establish brotherhood. (50:5.6, 50:5.7, 50:5.8)

VI–VII. Planetary Culture; The Rewards of Isolation

Compared with the loyal worlds, Urantia appears most confused and retarded, intellectually and spiritually. All of Satania has rested under a spiritual ban for two hundred thousand years, and this world suffered the double tragedy of a rebellious Planetary Prince and a defaulting Material Son. Yet even an ideal world is no flowery bed of ease; the early life of the mortal races always involves struggle. (50:6.2, 50:6.3, 50:6.5)

Effort and decision are an essential part of the acquirement of survival values. (50:6.3)

The author explains that Urantia’s isolation prevents him from describing in detail the life of our Satania neighbors. Even the bestowal of Christ Michael did not immediately set aside the temporal consequences of the early blunders in this world’s administration. He also warns that culture depends on the quality of mind: inferior minds will spurn the highest culture even when it is handed to them ready-made. (50:6.1, 50:6.4, 50:6.5)

Culture presupposes quality of mind (50:6.4)

The final section holds a remarkable reversal. Isolated worlds are deprived of the help of superhuman personalities, but in exchange they gain a unique opportunity to develop faith and a confidence in cosmic reliability that does not depend on sight. Ascenders from such worlds are very early entrusted with special assignments that require unquestioned faith. (50:7.1)

known as the agondonters, meaning evolutionary will creatures who can believe without seeing, persevere when isolated, and triumph over insuperable difficulties even when alone. (50:7.2)

The agondonter grouping persists even in the Corps of the Mortal Finality, and Tabamantia, the finaliter who inspects the experimental worlds, is himself an agondonter. A handicapped beginning does not end in eternal loss; it becomes the source of a distinctive quality. (50:7.2, 50:7.3)

All through the Paradise career, reward follows effort as the result of causes. (50:7.3)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Through the story of a planet’s first ruler, Paper 50 shows us leadership, loyalty, and the growth of civilization. At the same time it offers comfort to those of us who live on a world where that rule broke down. Faith that believes without seeing is a special asset that an isolated world can cultivate, and such effort always leads to its reward. (50:7.1, 50:7.3)
