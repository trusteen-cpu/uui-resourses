Introduction — Telling Evil, Error, Sin, and Iniquity Apart

Paper 53 recorded the course of the Lucifer rebellion; Paper 54 takes up the philosophical questions it raised. The author is a Mighty Messenger who lived through a rebellion himself and survived. He begins by noting that evolutionary man finds it hard to grasp the meanings of evil, error, sin, and iniquity, and so he starts by distinguishing these four words clearly. (54:0.1)

The Gods neither create evil nor permit sin and rebellion. (54:0.2)
Source 54:0.2

ErrorWhere truth and untruth conflictThe willful rejection of truth
SinImperfect beings able to choose between good and evilThe deliberate choice of evil
IniquityRealms that continually reject the pursuit of divinityThe persistent pursuit of sin and error

This distinction helps us see our own faults clearly. Being imperfect is not in itself sin; it is the unavoidable condition of a growing universe. Sin begins with a choice made knowingly, and iniquity hardens when that choice is persisted in. (54:0.2)

I. True and False Liberty

Of all the problems that grew out of the rebellion, none caused more difficulty than the failure to distinguish true liberty from false. True liberty is the reward of long evolutionary progress and rests on the reality of justice—intelligence, maturity, fraternity, and equity. Liberty that is unintelligent, unconditioned, and uncontrolled, by contrast, becomes a self-destroying technique. (54:1.1, 54:1.2, 54:1.3)

Source 54:1.6

License masquerading in the garments of liberty is the forerunner of abject bondage. (54:1.5)

The central principle of this section is that liberty does not exist in a vacuum. Cut off from material justice, intellectual fairness, social forbearance, moral duty, and spiritual values, it destroys itself. And when the Supreme Rulers of the universe themselves stand back in respect for the creature’s will, no being has the right to rob his fellows of their rights in the name of his own liberty. (54:1.4, 54:1.9)

Even wisdom is divine and safe only when it is cosmic in scope and spiritual in motivation. (54:1.7)

The author says there is no greater error than the self-deception that makes intelligent beings crave power over others in order to take away their natural liberties; the golden rule cries out against all such fraud and injustice. He adds that on a primitive world people may have to fight tyrants and oppressors for their material liberties, but on worlds of normal advancing civilization physical combat has long since fallen into disrepute. As a civilization matures, the struggle for liberty moves from weapons to fairness. (54:1.8, 54:1.10)

II. The Theft of Liberty

Ever since God projected Havona with the Son and in the Spirit, the eternal pattern of shared participation in creation has held. Every creature of an evolving universe who aspires to do the Father’s will is destined to become a partner of the Creators in the adventure of experiential perfection. Were this not so, the Father would hardly have given such creatures free will or come to indwell them. (54:2.1, 54:2.2)

Seen this way, Lucifer’s declaration was not an enlargement of liberty but a theft of it. His folly was trying to short-circuit time in an experiential universe; his crime was trying to rob every personality of the joy of contributing something of its own to the long struggle toward light and life. Dressed in the garments of liberty, it would actually have taken from men and angels the divine privilege of helping to create their own destinies. (54:2.3, 54:2.4)

No being in all the universe has the rightful liberty to deprive any other being of true liberty, the right to love and be loved, the privilege of worshiping God and of serving his fellows. (54:2.5)

III–IV. The Time Lag of Justice; The Mercy Time Lag

People ask why the all-wise Creators permit evil and sin. The author’s answer is plain: both are inevitable if the creature is to be truly free. The ability to choose good or evil is not a philosophic symbol but a universe reality, and the Supreme Rulers will not let any being take this liberty from a single personality. (54:3.1)

There is a further reason for the delay of justice. Even though wholehearted identification with sin amounts in the end to annihilation, enough time must pass between that point and the execution of the penalty for the verdict to be recognized as fair by all concerned—even by the sinner himself. The Ancients of Days annihilate no one until all moral values are extinct in the evildoer and in his possible sympathizers. (54:3.2, 54:3.3)

The delay of mercy can be understood with a parent’s heart. Like the father in Jesus’ story of the prodigal son, a loving father can wait long for an erring child to repent. If extended mercy might lead to repentance and rehabilitation, any length of delay in executing justice is justified. (54:4.2, 54:4.3)

Ironically, Lucifer already had most of the liberties he demanded, and the rest he was to receive in the future. He lost these precious gifts by yielding to impatience—to wanting what he craved now—and by disregarding his obligation to respect the rights of every other being. Impatience does not bring the gift sooner; it makes us lose it. (54:4.4)

Ethical obligations are innate, divine, and universal. (54:4.4)

Nor do these delays go on forever. While this revelation was being made, the first hearing of the case of Gabriel vs. Lucifer was held on Uversa, and soon afterward the Ancients of Days ordered Satan confined to the prison world with Lucifer, so that he can no longer visit the fallen worlds. (54:4.8)

Justice in a mercy-dominated universe may be slow, but it is certain. (54:4.8)

This delay is also the saving interval between seedtime and harvest. If the seed is good, the interval tests and builds character; if it is evil, it gives time for repentance and correction. When reward or punishment does not come at once in our own lives, we need neither lose heart nor grow careless; that time is room for grace. (54:4.6)

The author lists the reasons he knows and is permitted to recite. On Uversa, he says, forty-eight reasons are taught for allowing evil to run the full course of its own moral bankruptcy and spiritual extinction, and he suspects there are as many more unknown to him. (54:5.1, 54:5.14)

Two points stand out in this list. First, evil that is hastily covered over does not disappear; it lingers in hiding. Only when rebellion is fully exposed can every being see it clearly and decide for himself. Second, our impatience with time is a matter of perspective: what seems a long delay to us may be very brief in a wider view. (54:5.11, 54:5.13)

From the Paradise viewpoint the adjudication is simultaneous with the enactment. (54:5.13)

The explanation of the Paradise emergency adviser deserves special notice. Unless every creature of Norlatiadek had full and free opportunity to choose, the Paradise quarantine against halfhearted or doubt-stricken creatures would have had to be extended to the whole constellation. The delay, then, was not leniency toward rebels; it was a measure to keep the doors of ascension open to all, the loyal included. The entire Uversa corps of counselors likewise advised letting the rebellion take its natural course, even if it took a million years to wind up the consequences. (54:5.10, 54:5.12)

VI. The Triumph of Love

The final section says that the whole manner of dealing with the rebellion is a vindication of divine love. The Creator Son and the Paradise Father are love dominated in all their dealings with intelligent beings, and God as a Father takes precedence over every other phase of Deity. Without remembering this, we cannot understand the attitude of the universe rulers toward the rebels. (54:6.1, 54:6.2)

Of course, the innocent suffer because of mercy’s delay. Families, nations, worlds, and systems are all relationships of association, so their members profit from one another’s right conduct and also bear the consequences of one another’s wrongdoing. But the author offers an eternal assurance that such hardships are only temporary. (54:6.3, 54:6.4)

you may rest secure in the eternal assurance that such tribulations are transient afflictions. (54:6.4)

Further, the rebellion eventually brought forth good. The Jerusem citizens who withstood the test advanced in status and spiritual worth, and the Melchizedeks now teach that the good resulting from the Satania rebellion is more than a thousand times the sum of all the evil. The sum of evil has become almost stationary, while its beneficial repercussions keep spreading, even to Havona. Yet this harvest was possible only because Lucifer’s superiors took a wise and merciful attitude. (54:6.5, 54:6.6, 54:6.7)

This does not justify evil as a tool for good. Good cannot come of evil to the one who contemplates and performs it; but all things work together for good to those who know God and love to do his will. And the ability to decline survival did not begin with the rebellion; it has always been part of man’s gift of free will. (54:4.7, 54:6.9)

Shortsighted and time-bound mortal minds should be slow to criticize the time delays of the farseeing and all-wise administrators of universe affairs. (54:6.8)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 54 binds liberty, justice, and mercy into one picture. True liberty grows within self-control and brotherhood, justice waits to give mercy time to save, and the Father’s love presides over the whole process. The author admits that he himself fully understood these complexities only after serving in superuniverse administration. So it is no shame that we do not understand everything now; it is the natural course of the ascent. (54:6.10)

Paper 55 lifts our gaze from the shadow of rebellion far into the future, picturing the spheres of light and life that evolutionary planets finally attain. There we see the goal toward which this merciful delay is moving.
