Introduction — The Summit of Evolution, Yet a Beginning

Paper 52 followed the planetary epochs from primitive man to the age of the Teacher Sons; Paper 55 pictures the era of light and life that lies at the end of that road. It begins when the Teacher Sons conclude their final mission and continues indefinitely on the inhabited worlds, and as far as the author knows, worlds so settled are destined to go on throughout all future eternal ages. (55:0.1, 55:0.2, 55:0.3)

The age of light and life is the final evolutionary attainment of a world of time and space. (55:0.1)

I. The Morontia Temple — The Certificate of Light and Life

The appearance of a morontia temple at the planetary capital is the certificate that a world has entered light and life. On that day the planet’s Paradise bestowal Son comes in person and proclaims the long-serving Planetary Prince as Planetary Sovereign. The temple has three parts: at the center, the sanctuary of the bestowal Son; on the right, the seat of the Planetary Sovereign; on the left, the seat of the acting chief of finaliters. (55:1.1, 55:1.2)

they preside on that day when the “holy temple comes down upon earth.” (55:1.1)

The temple is said to “come down from heaven,” but no material is actually transported; it is built on the planet from a miniature design worked out on the system capital. Seating about three hundred thousand, it is used not for worship or play but for communication with the System Sovereign, silent cosmic contemplation, graduation exercises and recognition of service, and above all as the place to witness the translation of living mortals. (55:1.3, 55:1.4, 55:1.5)

II. Death and Translation — Where Funerals Become Graduations

Natural, physical death is not a mortal inevitability. (55:2.1)

As a planet advances, more and more mortals fuse with their Adjusters and pass alive into the morontia state. By the terminal mission of the Teacher Sons about one quarter escape natural death, and by the fourth stage more than half leave by translation. When fusion approaches, a summons is issued; the candidate resigns his duties, bids farewell, and waits in the morontia temple, surrounded by family and friends, for the translation flash. (55:2.2, 55:2.3, 55:2.8)

On worlds settled in light and life, “funerals” are occasions of supreme joy, profound satisfaction, and inexpressible hope. (55:2.5)

The author says it would help if less advanced mortals could learn to view natural death with something of this same cheerfulness. Souls translated in this way bypass the mansion worlds and go straight to the universe headquarters, but ascenders who did not have that experience are given the chance on the seven mansion spheres to learn everything they missed. The universe applies equalizing techniques so that no ascender is deprived of anything essential to the ascent. (55:2.6, 55:2.9, 55:2.12)

III. The Golden Ages — Not Perfect, Yet Heaven on Earth

In this era the world moves forward with one language, one religion, and, on normal spheres, one race. Yet the author makes clear that the age is not perfect. There are still hospitals; accidents and the infirmities of old age must still be cared for; disease has not been entirely vanquished. Even so, were we transported there, we would call it heaven on earth. (55:3.1)

The way this first-stage world the author visited finances its public life reveals the community’s values. Natural resources are administered as community property, the highest honor is the order of “supreme service,” and most social and judicial posts are held jointly by men and women. That even such a world still presents plenty of evil, real and potential, to stimulate choice tells us that virtue does not arise automatically; it is still grown by choosing. The great handicap confronting Urantia in the matter of attaining the high planetary destiny of light and life is embraced in the problems of disease, degeneracy, war, multicolored races, and multilingualism. (55:3.21)

IV. Administrative Readjustments — Finaliters Return to Serve

A settled world advances under the administration of the volunteer Corps of the Finality, ascenders who reached Paradise and came back to serve their brethren in the flesh. In the first stage the Planetary Sovereign, the chief of finaliters, and Adam and Eve, who unify the two, govern together, with the liberated midwayers serving as interpreters. Counselors are added stage by stage, and Adam and Eve are finally released from planetary duty and choose one of five careers. (55:4.1, 55:4.4, 55:4.24)

In the third or fourth stage an elected mortal chief executive appears and serves with the Planetary Sovereign for twenty-five years of planetary time. The ministering-spirit quartette that assists him never offers counsel unless asked. It is striking how these higher beings stand back out of respect for human self-direction. (55:4.15, 55:4.16)

One of the last acts of the Teacher Sons on their terminal mission is to liberate the midwayers and give them responsible places in the new administration. Thanks to the final discoveries of physical science, mortals can now recognize these cousins, invisible since the early Adamic regime. Those who served unseen for so long are at last acknowledged as fellows. (55:4.8)

V–VI. The Acme of Material Development; The Individual Mortal

On worlds in the advanced stages, poverty and social inequality have all but vanished, war is a matter of history, and there are no armies or police. Self-control is rendering human laws obsolete, and the schools are devoted to training the mind and expanding the soul. Life is refreshingly simple, and the pursuit of happiness is an experience of joy and satisfaction. (55:5.2, 55:5.4, 55:5.6)

The extent of civil government and statutory regulation, in an intermediate state of advancing civilization, is in inverse proportion to the morality and spirituality of the citizenship. (55:5.4)

The people there become more altruistic and fraternal without losing their independence or devotion to family. Still, they are mortals who breathe, eat, and sleep; this world is not heaven but a foreshadowing of the divine worlds of the ascent. The author conjectures that there can never be a limit to intellectual evolution and the attainment of wisdom. (55:6.1, 55:6.2, 55:6.5)

Spiritual growth continues as well. Mortals commune ever more deeply with the indwelling Father fragment, and the ministry of the Holy Spirit and the angels grows more effective with each epoch. In the fourth stage, advanced mortals seem to experience considerable conscious contact with the Master Spirit of their superuniverse, and their philosophy focuses on understanding new revelations of God the Supreme. (55:6.4)

“old things are passing away; behold, all things are becoming new.” (55:6.4)
Were there no future of eternal creature progression, still the superb evolutionary attainments of the mortal races on such settled worlds of perfected achievement would amply justify man’s creation on the worlds of time and space. (55:6.9)

VII–X. From the Planet to the Local Universe

In the first stage the outwardly visible rulers are the Planetary Adam and Eve, and it is this arrangement that gave prestige to the idea of kings and queens throughout the universe. The author says that when Urantia reaches this era, Machiventa Melchizedek, now vicegerent Planetary Prince, will no doubt occupy the seat of Planetary Sovereign. When the youngest world of a system has been settled for a millennium, the whole system enters the second stage. (55:7.2, 55:7.3, 55:7.4)

In the system stage a legislative assembly is formed, with ten representatives from each planet, and the System Sovereign no longer changes. In the constellation stage the Most Highs are exalted to their true dignity, and in the local universe stage a supreme council of unlimited authority, made up of one hundred Faithfuls of Days, is established. Only then does the finaliter corps for the first time acknowledge an authority this side of Paradise. (55:8.1, 55:8.3, 55:9.3, 55:10.1, 55:10.7)

Once a local universe is settled, the Creator Son becomes administratively free and spends much time counseling the finaliter groups, and Michael finds fuller fraternity with glorified mortals. The author refrains from speculating about the Creator Sons’ role in the outer universes, yet he is sure that if they go, the Divine Ministers will go with them, and that the Melchizedeks will remain in their universes of origin. (55:10.8, 55:10.9, 55:10.11)

XI–XII. From the Sectors to the Superuniverse — A Future No One Has Yet Seen

Not one of the seven superuniverses has yet been settled in light and life. So the author says again and again, with humility, that about the fifth and later stages “we really do not know.” The settling of the whole grand universe will be the most profound event since the appearance of the central universe, and some hold that the Supreme Being will then emerge as the experiential sovereign of the perfected creation. (55:11.1, 55:12.5)

The greatest comfort in this section concerns the individual. The administrative machinery of a universe cannot limit or retard the spiritual progress of an individual planet or mortal. Even on an isolated world, environmental limitations cannot thwart personal attainment. (55:11.6, 55:11.7)

Jesus of Nazareth, as a man among men, personally achieved the status of light and life over nineteen hundred years ago on Urantia. (55:11.7)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 55 is a distant landscape showing where our world is heading. A society in which death becomes a graduation, law gives way to self-control, and income is shared for truth, beauty, and goodness still seems far away. Yet through the life of Jesus the paper confirms that this status can be attained personally, by one person, even on an isolated world. (55:3.2, 55:11.7)
