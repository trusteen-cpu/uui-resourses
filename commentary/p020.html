Introduction — Sons Who Come Down from Heaven

With Paper 20 the book enters the great theme of the “Sons of God.” In Orvonton the Sons of God are classified under three heads: descending, ascending, and trinitized. Descending Sons are personalities of direct and divine creation; ascending sons, such as mortals, achieve their status by taking part in evolution; trinitized Sons are a group of composite origin embraced by the Trinity. (20:0.1, 20:0.5)

All descending Sons have high origins, yet they are devoted to helping lowly evolutionary creatures in their Paradise climb. This paper names seven orders of them and treats in detail the three that come from Paradise. (20:1.1)

I. The Descending Sons of God — Three Origins, One Spirit

The three Paradise Sons differ in origin. Yet the paper stresses their oneness before it stresses their differences. (20:1.11)

Source 20:1.11
SonOrigin
Creator Sons (primary)The Universal Father and the Eternal Son
Magisterial Sons (secondary)The Eternal Son and the Infinite Spirit
Trinity Teacher SonsThe Father, the Son, and the Spirit
From the standpoint of service, worship, and supplication the Paradise Sons are as one; their spirit is one, and their work is identical in quality and completeness. (20:1.11)

If the orders of Days are divine administrators, the Paradise Sons are divine ministers: creators, servers, bestowers, judges, teachers, and truth revealers. In a local universe the Magisterial and Teacher Sons serve under the direction of the Creator Son. The Creator Son is personally conscious of every act and emotion of every descending Son serving in his domain, a local universe duplication of the spiritual drawing power of the Eternal Son. (20:1.12, 20:1.13)

In a sevenfold Creator Son, Creator and creature are forever blended in understanding, sympathetic, and merciful association. (20:1.15)

II–IV. The Magisterial Sons — Judges and Bestowers

Every time an absolute concept of being formulated by the Eternal Son unites with a new ideal of loving service conceived by the Infinite Spirit, a new Magisterial Son appears. The Avonals number about one billion. They are not creators in the personal sense, but they are closely associated with the Michaels in all their work. Each Avonal has an exclusive personality; no two are alike. (20:2.1, 20:2.2, 20:2.3)

Source 20:2.4

As dispensational judges the Avonals preside over the awakening of the sleeping survivors, bring an age of suspended justice to an end, and reassign the planetary ministers to the tasks of the new dispensation. But although they may render judgments extinguishing the identity of personal creatures, they do not execute such sentences; that is done only by superuniverse authorities. Once again we see the careful steps through which the Father’s justice passes. (20:3.1, 20:3.2)

In planetary affairs the Avonals are supported by the full power and authority of their Creator Son, so their service is just as effective as if the Creator Son himself had come. When incarnated on magisterial or bestowal missions they have experienced Adjusters, and these Adjusters are often later personalized to form the supreme Divinington council of direction. (20:2.9, 20:4.3)

Urantia has never been host to an Avonal on a magisterial mission. In the usual plan one would have come between the days of Adam and the bestowal of Michael, but the final bestowal of our Creator Son wholly deranged that sequence. Urantia is now Michael’s personal ward, and only time can reveal which Sons will come, and when. (20:4.4, 20:4.5)

one thing is certainly authentic—his promise to come back to your world. (20:4.5)

V–VI. Bestowal — When the Word Is Made Flesh

On Urantia there is a widespread belief that a Son’s bestowal is meant to change the attitude of the Universal Father. The paper corrects this plainly. Bestowal is not appeasing the Father; it is the experiential process through which the Sons become safe and sympathetic rulers of the peoples of time. (20:5.2)

The career of sevenfold bestowal is the supreme goal of all Paradise Creator Sons. (20:5.2)

Bestowal is also necessary for the planet. Only when a bestowal Son returns to universe headquarters is the Spirit of Truth poured out, and only then do Thought Adjusters come to all normal human beings. The Adjuster within us is thus directly linked to the life of a bestowal Son. (20:5.3)

This is also why Urantia draws so much interest in Nebadon. Our small planet matters because it is the mortal home of Jesus of Nazareth, the arena where Michael won supreme sovereignty. Yet the paper adds that no planet could ever require a Creator Son for its rehabilitation; any bestowal Son would have sufficed. It is a balanced view that keeps us humble. (20:5.5, 20:5.6)

They never fail to achieve the goal of the bestowal mission. (20:5.7)

A bestowal Son is always born of woman, grows up as a child, labors like the people of his race, and makes petitions to the Father; the one exception is that he begets no offspring. The revelator urges us not to waste time speculating on how such an incarnation is effected, but to let the knowledge of Jesus’ life sink into our souls. (20:6.1, 20:6.2, 20:6.3)

A bestowal Son must pass through death and reappears on the third day. But the cruel death Jesus suffered was not a requirement of the divine plan. It made Urantia known as “the world of the cross,” while most planets have received their bestowal Sons more considerately. Death is not to satisfy justice or appease wrath; it is the voluntary completion of the bestowal, “to drink the cup” of a creature’s life. (20:6.6, 20:6.7)

Bestowal is a planetary and a universe necessity, and physical death is nothing more than a necessary part of a bestowal mission. (20:6.7)

VII–IX. The Trinity Teacher Sons — Universal Educators

Daynals is their name in Havona; in Orvonton they are Trinity Teacher Sons, and on Salvington they are sometimes called Paradise Spiritual Sons. Their numbers keep growing, now a little more than twenty-one billion. They are neither creators nor judges nor rulers, and they are concerned less with administration than with moral enlightenment and spiritual development. (20:7.1, 20:7.2, 20:7.3)

In all universes they are the embodiment of service and the discretion of wisdom. (20:7.4)

They receive no preliminary training in the central universe but go directly to the superuniverses, and having no spiritual drawing power of their own, they use the spiritual influence of the Creator and Magisterial Sons. They begin in the local systems and advance through constellation service to the highest work of the local universe; the staff of the University of Salvington alone, including these Sons, numbers over one hundred thousand. They care for the instruction of seraphim as much as for that of ascending mortals. (20:7.5, 20:8.1, 20:8.2)

They are the exalted teachers of all spirit personalities, even the tried and true teachers of the Sons of God themselves. (20:8.4)

On the planets the Teacher Sons open a spiritual age. They usually remain a thousand years, one of them presiding over the millennial reign with seventy associates. They do not incarnate but keep contact with the world through the Brilliant Evening Stars. Urantia has never yet known such an age, but even now they visit our world to plan their sojourn, and they will appear when its people have gained comparative deliverance from the shackles of animalism and the fetters of materialism. (20:9.1, 20:9.3)

This passage leaves a quiet task for our own time. A spiritual age does not simply descend from above; it comes to a world grown ready to receive it. The Teacher Sons neither end dispensations nor judge; they make real the spiritual counterparts of material knowledge. After their final mission, the planet is ushered into light and life. (20:9.2, 20:9.4)

X. United Ministry of the Paradise Sons

The last section draws the work of the three Sons together. All are divine in origin and nature, and all are gifts of the eternal Deities to every creature with ascension potential. (20:10.1, 20:10.2)

The work of each Paradise Son in behalf of each world is just as if the Son of service were the first and only Son of God. (20:10.1)

Together these three Sons help actualize and reveal the personality and sovereignty of God the Supreme in time and space, and they always function in the vanguard as divinity expands without end into the depths of space. (20:10.4)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 20 shows that God does not merely command from afar; through his Sons he comes down to live, teach, and judge among us. Bestowal is not a sacrifice to appease the Father’s anger but an experience gained for the sake of understanding and sympathy, and each Son serves his world as though it were the only one. And our own world remains open to a spiritual age that has not yet come.

Regard ordinary work and labor as holyBestowal Sons have followed nearly every vocation
Work at loosening the fetters of materialismOnly then does a spiritual age arrive
Live as both a learner and a teacherThe Teacher Sons teach even the Sons of God

The Creator Sons, the order of Michael whose story this paper set aside, are taken up fully in Paper 21. It is an important bridge that lets us see the life of Jesus within the larger picture of Michael’s seventh bestowal.
