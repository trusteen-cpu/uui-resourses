Introduction — A Month in Jerusalem

Through the month of April Jesus and the apostles worked in Jerusalem and went out each night to rest at Bethany. Once or twice a week Jesus stayed at the home of Flavius, a Greek Jew, where many prominent Jews came secretly to see him. This paper records that month and the stay in southern Judea that followed. (142:0.1)

On the first day Jesus called on Annas, his old friend and the former high priest, but was received coldly. Jesus left at once, leaving behind a short question. Annas made no reply, and the two did not meet again until Annas sat in judgment on the Son of Man. (142:0.2)

Fear is man’s chief enslaver and pride his great weakness (142:0.2)

Fear and pride are also keys to the whole paper. Nicodemus came by night out of fear, and the leaders rejected new truth out of pride. By contrast, Jacob and Flavius opened their hearts and found joy and liberty. (142:6.1)

I. Teaching in the Temple — The Gospel Goes Out to the World

Throughout the month Jesus or one of the apostles taught in the temple every day. When the crowds grew too large, the apostles held separate meetings outside the temple courts. The message they proclaimed can be summed up in four lines. (142:1.1)

Source 142:1.2
OrderThe heart of the message
1The kingdom of heaven is at hand
2By faith in the fatherhood of God you may enter the kingdom and become the sons of God
3Love is the rule of living within the kingdom: supreme devotion to God, and love for your neighbor as yourself
4Obedience to the Father’s will, yielding the fruits of the spirit, is the law of the kingdom

The Passover pilgrims carried this news back to the farthest parts of the Roman Empire and to the East. The text says this was the beginning of the spread of the gospel of the kingdom to the outside world. The quiet personal ministry to individuals turned out to travel the farthest. (142:1.7)

II. God’s Wrath — Jacob, the Merchant of Crete

Jacob, a wealthy merchant from Crete, asked how the God of wrath taught by the prophets could be the same as the merciful Father Jesus proclaimed. Jesus answered that God had not changed; human understanding of him had grown. (142:2.1, 142:2.2)

Our Father in Paradise is changeless. But the concept of his nature has enlarged and grown from the days of Moses down through the times of Amos and even to the generation of the prophet Isaiah. (142:2.2)

Jacob had six children, and Jesus explained by way of a father’s experience. A young child sees only an angry father in discipline, but as an adult should come to understand the farsighted love within it. In the same way humanity should see God more clearly from generation to generation; to cling to the old picture is to lose the benefits of spiritual enlightenment. For any reader who still carries a fear of God, this illustration is an invitation to turn that fear into mature understanding. (142:2.3, 142:2.4)

III. The Concept of God — Six Stages of Growth

That night, in answer to the apostles’ questions, Jesus taught how the concept of Deity had evolved among the Jewish people. The apostles were more shocked than ever before and could not even ask questions. (142:3.2, 142:3.9)

Source 142:3.2
StageConcept of GodWhat the text says
1YahwehThe god of the Sinai clans, exalted by Moses to the Lord God of Israel
2The Most HighEl Elyon, proclaimed to Abraham by Melchizedek
3El ShaddaiThe concept of the God of heaven learned in Egypt
4ElohimThe teaching of the Trinity handed down from the days of Adam
5The Supreme YahwehIn Isaiah’s day, the almighty and merciful Creator of the universe
6The Father in heavenA religion in which the believer becomes a son of God

The comforting part of this list is the note attached to the first stage: the Father never fails to accept the sincere worship of his children, however crude their concept of Deity or by whatever name they call him. Jesus also pointed out that the Scriptures themselves show this growth, as when a later record assigns to Satan an event that the books of Samuel had attributed to Yahweh. (142:3.3, 142:3.9)

As the concept of God grew, the understanding of divine law grew with it. Jesus traced that development in three stages. (142:3.10)

Source 142:3.21

At SinaiNew ten commandments, fitting the enlarging concept of Yahweh

And it is this supreme law of love for God and for man that I also declare to you as constituting the whole duty of man. (142:3.22)

IV. Flavius and Greek Culture — Beauty and Worship

Flavius was a proselyte of the gate, neither circumcised nor baptized, and a lover of art who had filled his home with statues collected from around the world. He worried that these so-called images would offend Jesus, but Jesus instead looked at each piece with interest and asked appreciative questions. (142:4.1)

Jesus said that Moses had been justified in his day in trying to combat idolatry, but that his children now made false gods of the very prohibition of images. Once the Father is revealed in the life of the Son, there is no longer any danger of confusing the Creator with images of stone or gold, and people can enjoy the beauty of art without mistaking it for worship. (142:4.2, 142:4.3)

If you love your neighbor as you love yourself, you really know that you are a son of God. (142:4.2)

Jesus said that in the coming kingdom people would no longer be concerned with lists of things not to do, but with one supreme duty: to worship the Father truly and to serve their fellows in love. The next day Flavius was baptized by John’s disciples and gave a banquet for sixty of his friends. (142:4.2, 142:4.4)

V. The Discourse on Assurance — A Son Is Not Left in Suspense

A man from Damascus asked how he could be sure that Jesus was sent from God, and that he himself could enter the kingdom. Jesus first told him to judge the message by its fruits, saying that the spirit would witness in his heart to its truth. Then he answered with an illustration: no kindhearted father keeps his son in anxiety about his place in the family. (142:5.1, 142:5.2)

whosoever has been born of the spirit has in himself the power to overcome all doubt, and this is the victory that overcomes all uncertainty, even your faith. (142:5.3)

The ground of assurance is not a feeling but a relationship. If you receive God as your Father, you are his son, and if you are a son, your standing in divine sonship is secure. And one outward evidence of this inner assurance is named. (142:5.2)

the evidence to all the world that you have been born of the spirit is that you sincerely love one another. (142:5.4)

VI. The Visit with Nicodemus — Born from Above

Nicodemus, a wealthy and elderly member of the Sanhedrin, came by night to the home of Flavius, fearing what others might see. Jesus neither favored him, nor turned him away, nor spoke to him with sarcasm, but received him calmly and with dignity. Nicodemus came not as an official representative but out of personal and sincere interest. (142:6.1, 142:6.2)

When the wind blows, you hear the rustle of the leaves, but you do not see the wind (142:6.5)

When Nicodemus asked how he could begin to lay hold of the spirit, Jesus answered that the Father’s spirit already dwelt within him. If he sought to be led by that spirit, he would begin to see with the eyes of the spirit, and by wholeheartedly choosing its guidance, he would be born of the spirit. The result would be the abundant fruits of the spirit in daily life. (142:6.7)

Nicodemus excelled in self-control and moral qualities, yet he did not know how to submit his will to the Father’s will as a child follows a father. Even so, he developed enough faith to lay hold of the kingdom; later he protested when his colleagues sought to condemn Jesus without a hearing, and with Joseph of Arimathea he claimed the body of Jesus. The difference between moral achievement and spiritual surrender is worth pondering, especially for earnest believers. (142:6.8, 142:6.9)

VII. The Lesson on the Family — The Relation of God and Man

On a Wednesday of rest at Bethany, Thomas raised social questions such as slavery, property, law, and justice. Jesus first explained that the kingdom of heaven is an evolutionary experience, beginning on earth and progressing through successive life stations to Paradise. He then said that he used the figure of a “kingdom” because the Jews were expecting one, and that people of another age would better understand the gospel when it was presented in terms of the family. (142:7.1, 142:7.3, 142:7.4)

Source 142:7.5
Seven facts of a true familyWhat the father is like
The fact of existenceChildren are born of their parents and inherit their traits
Security and pleasureHe delights to provide for their needs and even their pleasures
Education and trainingHe prepares them for greater responsibilities later
Discipline and restraintHe provides guidance and correction for immature children
Companionship and loyaltyHis ear is always open to the petitions of his children
Love and mercyHe forgives freely and holds no grudge; he is no judge or creditor
Provision for the futureHe leaves an inheritance and carries family life on to the next generation

Jesus said that he already knew in perfection this whole relationship of a son to the Father. The text adds that he revealed a God of perfection to man and presented in himself the perfected son of the realms to God. Thomas, however, pointing to suffering and unanswered prayer, was still not satisfied. (142:7.13, 142:7.15, 142:7.16)

Will you not allow me to use the earth family as an illustration of divine relationships without so literally applying my teaching to material affairs? (142:7.17)

Jesus pleaded with them not to translate what he said in the language of the spirit into the language of the flesh. The apostles were ambassadors of a spiritual kingdom, and the family illustration pointed to spiritual relationships, not to a blueprint for social institutions. Yet he also promised to bear with them until the end of their association. (142:7.17)

VIII. In Southern Judea — A Season of Quiet Fruit

When opposition became clear at the end of April, the party moved to Bethlehem and Hebron and spent the month of May meeting people house to house without public preaching. With Abner, Jesus visited the Nazarite colony at Engedi; many believed, but many rejected him because he did not teach fasting and self-denial. (142:8.1)

In June they camped in the garden of Gethsemane and did no public teaching. Nicodemus and Joseph of Arimathea came as far as the tent entrance but turned back in fear. Late in June, when Simon, a member of the Sanhedrin, openly espoused Jesus’ teachings, the move to arrest him grew stronger again, and Jesus decided to withdraw into Samaria and the Decapolis. (142:8.4, 142:8.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

The conversations of Paper 142 come together around one theme. God does not change, but our concept of God must grow, and that growth moves from a law of prohibitions to a law of love, and from fear to the assurance of sonship. And the evidence of a life born of the spirit is not doctrine but sincere love for one another. (142:2.2, 142:5.4)
