Introduction — Why Is There a Foreword?

The Foreword is the first thing anyone meets on opening The Urantia Book. Yet it does not begin with a story. It begins as a guide to terms, in which a Divine Counselor of Orvonton defines in advance the words that will be used later. The reason is stated plainly in the first paragraph: in human minds there is great confusion about the meaning of words like God, divinity, and deity, and still more uncertainty about the relationships of the divine personalities called by those names. (0:0.1)

Because of this conceptual poverty associated with so much ideational confusion, I have been directed to formulate this introductory statement (0:0.1)

The revelators knew well how narrow human language is. So they state that they will use existing English words wherever possible and introduce new terms only when no suitable word can be found. The Foreword feels difficult partly because it deals with the highest realities of the universe, but also because it sets up at once the conceptual framework for all 196 papers that follow. It is therefore less a text to read once and finish than a map to return to whenever an unfamiliar concept appears. (0:0.2)

This Foreword is not, therefore, a finished statement within itself (0:0.4)

The Foreword also begins by telling us where we are in the universe. Urantia is a planet in the local universe of Nebadon; Nebadon belongs to Orvonton, one of the seven superuniverses; the seven superuniverses circle the central universe of Havona; and at its heart lies Paradise. Keeping this address in mind makes everything that follows much clearer. (0:0.5, 0:0.6)

I. Deity and Divinity — Telling the Words for God Apart

The first distinction the Foreword draws is between Deity and divinity. Deity may be personalized as God, but it also exists in prepersonal and superpersonal ways that man cannot fully comprehend. The quality that characterizes Deity is unity, and creatures best comprehend this unifying quality as divinity. (0:1.2)

Deity functions on seven levels. The first three are Deity as it already exists in eternity; the later levels are Deity as it is disclosed in time and space through creation and evolution. (0:1.3)

The term God always denotes personality. (0:2.10)

On the prepersonal level he functions through Father fragments such as the Thought Adjusters; on the personal level, in the experience of created beings; on the superpersonal level, in the existence of absonite beings. That the Adjuster indwelling our minds is the Father in the first of these ways is the starting point of personal religion. (0:2.3, 0:2.4, 0:2.5)

The Foreword tells how these seven “differentiated” as if it were a story in time. Of course in eternity there is no before and after; the sequence is given so that finite minds can grasp it. Through his free will the I AM achieved liberation from unqualified infinity, producing the first divinity-tension, which the Universal Absolute resolves. The key point to remember here is that everything springs from the Father’s will. The universe did not begin as a machine of chance or necessity, but from the free choice of a loving person.

Deity is of two kinds: existential Deity, which exists eternally, like the Father, the Son, and the Spirit; and experiential Deity, which is being actualized as the universe grows, like God the Supreme, God the Ultimate, and God the Absolute. (0:7.1)

The essence of Deity is eternal, but only the three original persons of Deity are unqualifiedly eternal. (0:7.5)

The Supreme Being is not a direct creator but a synthetic co-ordinator of all creature-Creator activity, and when finally actualized he will constitute the eternal fusion of the finite and the infinite. This teaching means a great deal to us. The choices we make and the experience we gather in time do not vanish; they actually contribute to the actualization of the evolving God. (0:7.9, 0:7.10)

IX–X. God the Ultimate; God the Absolute

Just as the Supreme Being evolves in the grand universe, God the Ultimate eventuates in the domains that transcend time and space. The Foreword contrasts the two concisely. (0:9.1)

The Supreme is the Trinity unification comprehended by finite beings; the Ultimate is the unification of the Paradise Trinity comprehended by absonite beings. (0:9.2)

God the Absolute lies further still. He will be realized through the unification of the Absolute Trinity, and he is the goal of all superabsonite beings. The revelators deliberately hold back from speaking of this realm, frankly admitting that its reality goes beyond our concepts. This honesty is an attitude that runs through the whole Foreword. (0:10.1, 0:10.2)

Acknowledgment — How the Revelation Was Written

The close of the Foreword frankly explains how the revelation was written. Wherever possible, the revelators gave preference to the highest concepts humans had already expressed, and resorted to new expression only when those were inadequate. They selected more than one thousand human concepts from God-knowing mortals as the basis of the papers. (0:12.11, 0:12.12)

Finally, they explain why truth can be conveyed however inadequate language may be. (0:12.13)

But we know that there dwells within the human mind a fragment of God, and that there sojourns with the human soul the Spirit of Truth (0:12.13)

This sentence is the key to reading the whole Foreword. Its terms are difficult, but we are not left to understand them alone. The Adjuster within us and the Spirit of Truth turn unfamiliar concepts into living truth. So there is no need to be discouraged if we do not understand all of the Foreword. It is a text that grows a little clearer each time we come back to it as we read through the book.
