Introduction — The Second Half of a Million Years of Human History

Paper 64 tells the story of Urantia’s evolutionary races from the days of Andon and Fonta, through the times of the Planetary Prince, to the end of the ice age. Its author is again a Life Carrier resident on Urantia. He divides the nearly one-million-year history of mankind into two halves: the first roughly matches the days before the Planetary Prince, and the second begins with his arrival and the appearance of the six colored races. (64:0.1, 64:0.2)

The paper deals with races, but from a Life Carrier’s point of view: its purpose is to record how biological evolution and cultural and spiritual progress rose and fell. The text sets down both the strengths and the limitations of each race, and it states plainly that every race has the same standing before the celestial powers. This commentary follows what the text says, calmly and within those bounds. (64:6.27)

I. The Andonic Aborigines — Between Mountains, Water, and Ice

Primitive man was hemmed in on every side. To the east rose the high Tibetan plateau; to the south and west lay the expanded Mediterranean, reaching to the Indian Ocean; and to the north the ice was advancing. Even so, the more intelligent groups never turned back to their tree-dwelling cousins in the south. The text suggests that many of man’s earliest religious feelings grew out of this sense of geographic helplessness. (64:1.1, 64:1.2)

mountains to the right, water to the left, and ice in front. (64:1.2)

The Andonites avoided the forests, and the cold and hunger of the open lands stimulated action, invention, and resourcefulness. By 950,000 years ago they had spread west as far as France and England and east as far as Java and Tasmania. But as the eastern groups mingled with backward strains, fewer and fewer settlements kept up the worship of the Breath Giver, and this dawn civilization was threatened with extinction. (64:1.3, 64:1.6, 64:1.7)

human evolution has made progress only in the open and in the higher latitudes. (64:1.3)

II–III. The Foxhall Peoples; The Badonan Tribes — Two Embers in an Age of Decline

By 900,000 years ago the arts of Andon and Fonta and the culture of Onagar were vanishing; culture, religion, and even flintworking had reached their lowest ebb. Mixed groups arrived in Europe, and after them the tribes known as the Heidelberg race spread over the continent. Yet through this long decline, two groups at the western and eastern edges kept alive the traditions of Andon. (64:2.1, 64:2.3, 64:2.4)

About 850,000 years ago the Badonan tribes waged a war of extermination against their animalistic neighbors, and the mixed descendants that resulted appeared on the scene as an apparently new people. IV. The Neanderthal Races — Half a Million Years Moving with the Glaciers

The Neanderthalers were excellent fighters and hunters. They spread from northwestern India to France, China, and northern Africa, and they dominated the world for almost half a million years. The reindeer supplied them with food, clothing, and tools, and their flintwork improved until it nearly reached the level of Andon’s day. This section is also a record of men and animals moving north and south as the glaciers advanced and retreated. (64:4.1, 64:4.3)

Yet there was very little progress. For almost a quarter of a million years these peoples lived by hunting and fighting, and the text goes so far as to say that the attempt to produce a new type of intelligent life seemed about to fail. In these spiritually dark ages the Neanderthalers had no religion beyond superstition, and they were deathly afraid of mists, fogs, and the dark. A religion of fear sought to placate unseen forces and in the end led to human sacrifice. (64:4.11, 64:4.12, 64:4.13)

And this terrible practice of human sacrifice has been perpetuated by the more backward peoples of Urantia right on down to the twentieth century. (64:4.12)

V. Origin of the Colored Races — The Nineteen Children of the Sangik Family

Five hundred thousand years ago the Badonan tribes fell into a war that lasted more than a hundred years, and only about one hundred families were left; but these survivors were the most intelligent of all the descendants of Andon and Fonta then living. Then a couple in the northeastern part of the highlands suddenly began to have unusually intelligent children. This was the Sangik family. (64:5.1, 64:5.2)

This was the Sangik family, the ancestors of all of the six colored races of Urantia. (64:5.2)

The nineteen Sangik children showed a unique tendency for their skin to turn different colors when exposed to sunlight. These colors grew more pronounced as they matured, and their offspring tended toward the skin color of the Sangik parent. (64:5.3)

The text stresses how unusual this was. On an average evolutionary planet the red man appears first and roams the world for ages before the other races emerge one by one; on Urantia all six races were born at once in one family. The evolution of the Andonites, a race of will creatures, ahead of the colored races was also without precedent in Satania. (64:6.1, 64:6.2)

The simultaneous emergence of all six races on Urantia, and in one family, was most unusual. (64:6.1)

As the Life Carrier introduces the six races one by one, he is careful to name, along with each race’s distinctive traits, the great leader who awakened it. Amid repeated decline and revival, every race had a teacher who led it to the worship of the true God; this is the hidden thread running through the section. (64:6.29)

While the text records the indigo race as the least progressive, it immediately adds that degree of progress has nothing to do with spiritual standing. Being ahead or behind in culture is a matter of time and circumstance, not of worth in the sight of heaven. (64:6.25, 64:6.27)

Notwithstanding their backwardness, these indigo peoples have exactly the same standing before the celestial powers as any other earthly race. (64:6.27)

These were ages of intense struggle between the races, though near the headquarters of the Planetary Prince the more enlightened groups lived together in comparative harmony. The text says that besides the great teachers named here, many lesser teachers arose in different regions, and together they helped prevent the total collapse of civilization during the long dark ages between the Caligastia rebellion and the arrival of Adam. The share of those who taught without fame was by no means small. (64:6.28, 64:6.29)

Why, then, evolve several races at all? The Life Carrier sets out five reasons worth noting, even if mortals may not fully appreciate them. (64:6.30)

VII. Dispersion of the Colored Races — Settling the Continents

While the fifth glacier spread widely, the Sangik peoples lived together around the foothills for almost a hundred thousand years, though a natural antipathy between the different races showed itself early. When the ice retreated, the red man went northeast, closely followed by the yellow man, and the blue man moved northwest into Europe. The orange, green, and indigo races entered Africa one after another over a newly raised land bridge. (64:7.2, 64:7.3, 64:7.13)

In Europe the blue man met the Neanderthalers, descendants of their common ancestor Andon, and mingled with them. The red race left Asia as eleven tribes of a little over seven thousand people, and the small mixed groups accompanying them went south to found the civilizations of Mexico, Central America, and South America. The text adds that any effort to trace the Sangik ancestry of modern peoples must take into account the later admixture of Adamic blood. (64:7.5, 64:7.9, 64:7.12)

The western Andon tribes, pushed by the glaciers to the very edge of the sea, built boats and put out to sea when the last glacier came. They were the first marine adventurers; most perished on the open sea, but some reached Iceland and Greenland, and their Eskimo descendants later crossed to North America. The Life Carrier regrets that so many of the sturdy qualities of these early ancestors have been lost to later races. (64:7.17, 64:7.18, 64:7.20)

we miss the magnificent persistency and superb devotion of your early ancestors, which oftentimes bordered on grandeur and sublimity. (64:7.20)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 64 is a record in which progress and retrogression endlessly alternate. Superior strains are lost in war, culture sinks to its lowest point, and then it rises again through a single teacher. Through it all the Life Carrier stresses two things: that the tendency to fight among themselves destroyed races and civilizations from within, and that no race stands lower than any other before heaven. (64:7.7, 64:6.27)
