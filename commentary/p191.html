Introduction — A Resurrection Day Behind Bolted Doors

Resurrection Sunday was a terrible day for the apostles. Fearing arrest by agents of the Sanhedrin, the ten spent it in the upper room with the doors bolted. Thomas brooded alone at Bethpage. The text remarks that he would have fared much better had he stayed with his fellows. (191:0.1)

Their isolation made things harder. David’s messengers, who had always brought them news from outside, had all gone off to announce the resurrection. John Mark brought rumors and reports from around the temple, but it did not occur to him to gather news from the groups of believers to whom Jesus had already appeared. For the first time the apostles realized how much they had depended on David’s messengers. (191:0.3)

The text portrays the minds of the ten one by one on that day. Faced with the same news, each reacted according to his own temperament. The scene shows how differently a crisis of faith can come to different people. (191:0.2, 191:0.4)

Jesus had reasons for delaying his first appearance to the apostles. He wanted them to reflect on his forecasts, he wanted Peter to wrestle through his difficulties, and he wanted Thomas to be with them. Thomas’s pride kept him from returning on his own; the apostles waited for him, and he waited for them to come for him. So they would not leave for Galilee without Thomas. (191:0.13)

I. The Appearance to Peter — Forgiven Before He Asked

About half past eight that evening, Jesus appeared to Peter in the garden of the Mark home. It was his eighth morontia manifestation. Since his denial, Peter had been fighting the fear that he might no longer be an apostle, and he wondered whether his presence was keeping Jesus from coming to the others. (191:1.1)

He thought of the Master’s loving look as he passed by on Annas’s porch, and of the women’s message, “Go tell my apostles—and Peter.” His faith began to surmount his doubts, and he declared aloud that he believed. Just then the Master spoke to him in familiar tones. (191:1.2)

I knew it was not from the heart that you disowned me; therefore I forgave you even before you asked (191:1.2)
No longer should you be concerned with what you may obtain from the kingdom but rather be exercised about what you can give to those who live in dire spiritual poverty. (191:1.2)

Forgiveness did not stop at covering the past; it led straight into a new task. To stop thinking about oneself and one’s troubles and to think instead of what one can give to others is the most practical remedy for a person bound by guilt. Peter ran to the upper room crying that the Master had forgiven him. But when Andrew hinted at the vision of the night Peter walked on the water, he was crestfallen, and only the twins came to him saying they believed him. (191:1.4, 191:1.5)

II. First Appearance to the Apostles — Faith in One Another

Shortly after nine, with all the doors bolted, the Master appeared in the upper room. It was his ninth appearance. He asked whether he had not told them, while still in the flesh, of his betrayal, his death, and his rising on the third day, and he pointed to the doubts with which they had argued over the reports of the women, the Emmaus brothers, and Peter. (191:2.1, 191:2.2)

Have faith in God; have faith in one another (191:2.1)

Jesus reminded them that one of them was absent, and told them to go into Galilee once they were all together and certain. Setting faith in one another beside faith in God is significant, for that was exactly what these brothers needed, having just belittled Peter’s testimony. (191:2.1)

III. With the Morontia Creatures — The Seventh Bestowal Completed

The next day, Monday, Jesus spent with the morontia creatures present on Urantia. More than a million morontia directors and associates, together with transition mortals from the mansion worlds of Satania, were taking part in his transition experience. He taught them, and he also learned from the directors about the morontia career that mortals pass through on the mansion worlds. (191:3.1)

About midnight that Monday his form was adjusted to the second morontia stage. The higher the stage, the more difficult it became to make him visible to mortal eyes. His later progress is recorded by date. (191:3.2)

That the Creator has gone ahead of us on the mansion-world path we will one day walk is a great comfort for the ascending journey. (191:3.4)

IV. The Tenth Appearance (At Philadelphia) — Disciples Who Love One Another

On the evening of Tuesday, April 11, Jesus appeared in the synagogue at Philadelphia to about one hundred and fifty believers, among them Abner, Lazarus, and more than fifty of the seventy evangelists. It was his tenth appearance. With the resurrected Lazarus among them, they found the report of the resurrection easy to believe. Apart from the five hundred in Galilee, this was the largest group to see him at one time. (191:4.1, 191:4.5)

You are to love all men as I have loved you; you are to serve all men as I have served you. (191:4.3)

Jesus said there is but one gospel, and urged them to fellowship all their brethren who proclaim it, whether Jew, Greek, or Persian. His question — if you are ennobled to love unbelievers, should you not love your fellow believers just as much? — still speaks to communities of faith today. The next morning they went out at once to proclaim the resurrection. (191:4.3, 191:4.6)

On the following Wednesday Jesus again spent the day with his morontia associates. In the afternoon he received morontia delegates from the mansion worlds throughout the constellation of Norlatiadek. They rejoiced to know their Creator as a universe intelligence of their own order. That the Creator had come down to the place of the creature and walked with them was a joy even to heavenly beings. (191:4.7)

V. Second Appearance to the Apostles — Those Who Believe Without Seeing

Thomas spent a lonely week in the hills around Olivet, and on Saturday night two apostles brought him back. Even then he refused to believe, claiming Peter had excited the others. The text analyzes his doubt delicately: it was tangled with emotional stubbornness and embarrassment at having run away, and he unconsciously enjoyed the attention of his fellows. (191:5.1)

At supper the next evening, when he said he would not believe unless he could touch the nail marks, the Master appeared right before him. Jesus first renewed the commission to them all: as the Father had sent him, so he sent them, and they were to reveal divine love not only in words but in daily living. (191:5.2, 191:5.3)

I send you forth, not to love the souls of men, but rather to love men. (191:5.3)

Jesus told them that for now they were hiding their light behind closed doors out of fear, but that when baptized with the Spirit of Truth they would go forth bravely and joyously. He called this change the shock of the transition from the false security of the authority of traditionalism to the authority of faith in living experience. He knew that letting go of a familiar authority to stand on experienced truth is not easy, and so he allowed them time to recover. (191:5.3)

Then Jesus turned to Thomas. He said there were no nail marks on his hands because he had been raised in the form Thomas himself would have, and he told him that even while he was stoutly asserting his unbelief, he had already begun to believe in his heart. (191:5.4)

Your doubts, Thomas, always most stubbornly assert themselves just as they are about to crumble. (191:5.4)
Blessed are those in the ages to come who will believe even though they have not seen with the eye of flesh nor heard with the mortal ear. (191:5.5)

Thomas fell on his knees and confessed, “My Lord and my Master!” Now the eleven were fully convinced, and before dawn the next day they set out for Galilee. That final blessing is addressed less to Thomas than to today’s reader, for we are the very people of the ages to come, called to believe without seeing. (191:5.5, 191:5.7)

VI. The Alexandrian Appearance — A Gospel That Belongs to All

On the evening of April 18, as the apostles were nearing Galilee, Jesus appeared at Alexandria to Rodan and some eighty believers. It was his twelfth appearance. It came at the very moment Nathan of Busiris was finishing his account of the crucifixion with David’s report that the Master had said he would rise again. (191:6.1)

it shall not be committed to the custody of mere priests. (191:6.3)

Jesus told them the gospel belongs not to any one race or group of teachers but to all who believe, little children included. When David’s herald of the resurrection arrived two days later with the news, they answered that they already knew. (191:6.2, 191:6.4)

Yes, we know, for we have seen him. (191:6.4)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

In Paper 191 the risen Jesus lifts up broken people one at a time. Peter, who had denied him, received forgiveness along with a task; Thomas, who had doubted, received understanding rather than rebuke. And the words he gave to the apostles, to Philadelphia, and to Alexandria come together in one message: a life of loving and serving people as he loved and served them is itself the proclamation of the gospel. (191:4.3, 191:5.3, 191:6.2)
