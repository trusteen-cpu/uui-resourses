Introduction — Another Family Born of the Trinity

After the Supreme Trinity Personalities of Paper 18, Paper 19 introduces seven more groups of Trinity origin. The author is a Divine Counselor, and because he is describing his own order, the paper carries a special vividness. Except for the Teacher Sons and possibly the Inspired Spirits, these groups are of definite numbers, and their creation is a finished event. (19:0.1, 19:0.9)

I. The Trinity Teacher Sons — and the Right Way to Approach Truth

Of all the high personalities revealed to us, the Teacher Sons alone act in a dual capacity. They are of Trinity origin, yet in function they are devoted to the services of divine sonship, and so they bridge the gulf between Trinity- and dual-origin personalities. They are also the supreme co-ordinating personalities of Trinity origin. (19:1.1, 19:1.4)

Here the author turns suddenly to a question of method. In so vast a universe there is great danger of the error of the circumscribed viewpoint and a segmentalized conception of reality. The human mind usually wants to begin with the simple and lower and climb to the higher, but that path reveals only origins and says little or nothing about divine destiny. True perspective comes only from studying origin, history, and destiny together. (19:1.4, 19:1.5, 19:1.6)

The present can be truly interpreted only in the light of the correlated past and future. (19:1.11)

This is why The Urantia Book begins at the Paradise Source and Center and journeys outward into time and space. The passage reveals the organizing principle of the whole book. The same wisdom applies to a personal life: I should not define myself only by where I came from; only when I also look at where I am called to go can I rightly understand who I am today. (19:1.12)

II–IV. Wisdom, Counsel, and Judgment — Three Co-ordinate Orders of the Superuniverses

The Perfectors of Wisdom, the Divine Counselors, and the Universal Censors all passed through the wisdom of Paradise and Havona and were then permanently assigned to the service of the Ancients of Days. What these three orders have in common is that they do not merely “reflect” something of the Trinity; they are that very thing. (19:2.2, 19:3.1, 19:4.1)

They do not reflect the wisdom of the Paradise Trinity; they are that wisdom. (19:2.3)

Yet even this perfect wisdom is not sufficient alone. Wisdom has two origins: the divine insight inherent in perfect beings, and the personal experience acquired by evolutionary creatures. So in superuniverse administration the Perfectors of Wisdom are always associated with ascendant personalities who have come up through the tribulations of experience. That the perfect and those who grew through hardship need each other shows how precious our experience is in the universe. (19:2.4, 19:2.5)

These three orders are also the highest beings engaged in revealing truth to the planets. From initial-life worlds to worlds settled in light and life, they take part from time to time in service to ascending mortals. When we remember that the very paper we are reading is a fruit of such service, revelation feels less like a distant story and more like concrete care directed toward us. (19:2.6)

When the three orders work together they form the highest mobile advisory body. A tribunal of nine, one Perfector of Wisdom, seven Divine Counselors, and one Universal Censor, is called a fact-finding or truth-revealing tribunal, and in all the annals of the superuniverses its verdict has never been reversed. (19:3.4)

The author says that seven Divine Counselors in liaison with an evolutionary trio of a Mighty Messenger, One High in Authority, and One without Name and Number come nearest to uniting the human viewpoint with the divine attitude. Only the Paradise bestowal Sons, who are God and man, surpass this. That the place where God’s will and human circumstances meet is shown most fully in a bestowal Son invites us to think again about the life of Jesus. (19:3.7)

In this juridical trio the Perfector of Wisdom would be the “I was,” the Divine Counselor the “I will be,” but the Universal Censor is always “I am.” (19:4.4)

One Universal Censor is also stationed on each of the billion worlds of Havona. The other two orders have no permanent assignment there, and even the author does not altogether understand why the Censors do. He suspects they are there in anticipation of some future age in which the Havona population may partially change. The honesty of revelators who admit what they do not know runs all through this paper. (19:4.2)

The Censors are “universe totaling personalities” who gather all testimony, wisdom, and counsel and reveal the true total of what has transpired. When a Censor has spoken, no one else may speak. The author confesses that the Censors seem to formulate new meanings and originate new values, and that even after allowing for all that is known, their verdicts cannot be predicted. They seem in some manner to be in liaison with the Deity Absolute. (19:4.5, 19:4.6, 19:4.7)

When he speaks, there is no appeal. (19:4.5)

Together with the seven orders of Supreme Trinity Personalities, these three orders make up the ten groups called the Stationary Sons of the Trinity. Their numbers slightly exceed thirty-seven billion, and they uphold the administration, rule, counsel, and judgment of the universe. (19:4.8)

V. Inspired Trinity Spirits — The Unseen Teachers

The Inspired Trinity Spirits are one of the few wholly secret orders of beings. They cannot fully reveal themselves even to a Divine Counselor, whose origin is so near their own. They may be superpersonal spirits, and they act well-nigh independently of time and space. The author says their character can be deduced only from the results of their activities. (19:5.1, 19:5.2)

The beings who can sense them are the Solitary Messengers, who detect their presence as unfailingly as a magnetic needle points to a pole. Interestingly, on a planet like Urantia whose inhabitants are indwelt by Adjusters, a Solitary Messenger feels a qualitative excitation. This suggests that Thought Adjusters are in some manner connected with the Inspired Spirits. Still, they are not the same order: Adjusters come from the Father alone, Inspired Spirits from the Trinity. (19:5.5, 19:5.6)

The author reports that even as he writes, an Inspired Spirit of the third volume of power presence is with him, not over twenty-five feet away. He goes further, concluding that there is essential spiritual knowledge indispensable to high attainment which cannot be consciously received, and that the Inspired Spirits may be teachers who work by superconscious techniques. The insight that self-consciousness can hinder reception makes us reflect on why the stillness of prayer and meditation matters. (19:5.7, 19:5.9)

I am satisfied that there are no arbitrary secrets in the universe (19:5.11)

If even high beings cannot see everything, how much more is this true of mortals who are just beginning the journey. So the author commends faith and revelation to us. (19:5.12)

You will long use faith and be dependent on revelation if you hope to progress quickly and safely. (19:5.12)

VI–VII. Havona Natives and Paradise Citizens — Eternal Friends

The Havona natives are perfect beings created directly by the Trinity, and their number and endowments are beyond our imagining. Ascending mortals, passing through the billion worlds of Havona, will form eternal friendships with them. The picture of a deep friendship growing between creatures from the lowest place and natives of the highest is the warmest scene in the paper. (19:6.1, 19:6.2)

The good to both ascending mortal and Havona native is great and mutual. (19:6.2)

Mortals are compensated for the spiritual impoverishment of their early progress, while the Havoners overcome the experiential handicap of having always lived in perfection. Unlike the Stationary Sons, Havoners can also evolve in status; some fuse with a non-Adjuster Father fragment and join the Mortal Corps of the Finality. Their numbers are therefore slowly diminishing, and the revelators speculate that the future Havona may be peopled by a mixed group of residents. (19:6.2, 19:6.3, 19:6.4)

The Paradise Citizens comprise more than three thousand orders of beings; because they are not directly concerned with the ascension scheme, little is revealed about them. The paper closes the story of Trinity-origin beings by noting that none has ever gone astray, though in the highest sense all are freewill endowed. The author adds a homely detail: his journey from Uversa to Urantia took 109 days. (19:7.1, 19:7.3, 19:7.4)

None of them have ever gone astray. (19:7.3)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 19 tells of perfect beings, yet unexpectedly it holds up a mirror to us. Perfect wisdom must be paired with the wisdom of experience, and even the Havona natives learn through friendship with ascenders. The way to truth lies in seeing origin, history, and destiny together, and the deepest teaching may come on the quiet level of the superconscious. In a universe where everything changes, God alone is changeless.
