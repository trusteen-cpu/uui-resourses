Introduction — The Father Rules Through the Son

If Paper 32 described how a local universe grows, Paper 33 shows how such a universe is run once it has grown. The Universal Father most certainly rules over his vast creation, but in the administration of a local universe he works only through the person of the Creator Son. The Son forms and executes the plans and policies, and together with his Spirit associate he delegates executive power to Gabriel and jurisdictional authority to the Constellation Fathers, System Sovereigns, and Planetary Princes. (33:0.1)

I. Michael of Nebadon — The One Who Is God to Us

Our Creator Son is the personification of the 611,121st original concept of infinite identity of simultaneous origin in the Universal Father and the Eternal Son. His headquarters is the threefold mansion of light on Salvington, so ordered because he has lived all three phases of creature existence: spiritual, morontial, and material. Because of his seventh and final bestowal on Urantia, he is sometimes called Christ Michael. (33:1.1)

Michael is not the Eternal Son, nor is he a member of the Paradise Trinity. Yet in his realm he possesses all the divine attributes and powers the Eternal Son would show were he present on Salvington, and beyond that he represents the personality presence of the Universal Father in this universe. That is why he is the most powerful and versatile of all divine beings who can deal directly with the immature creatures of an evolutionary universe. (33:1.2, 33:1.3)

To our universe and all its inhabited worlds the Sovereign Son is, to all practical intents and purposes, God. (33:1.4)

This sentence brings real comfort to the life of faith. We can never fully grasp infinite Deity, but all of divinity that evolving mortals can discerningly comprehend is personified in this Son. A ruler and parent as mighty and beneficent as the Father and the Son would be if both were present on Salvington is already near us. (33:1.4, 33:1.5)

II. The Sovereign of Nebadon — Sovereignty Earned by Bestowal

Some Creator Sons resemble more the Father, some the Son; our Michael resembles more the Eternal Son. His power is limited only by the pre-existent gravity circuits centering at Paradise and by the Ancients of Days’ reservation of final judgments on the extinction of personality. Personality is the sole bestowal of the Father, but with the approval of the Eternal Son a Creator Son may initiate new creature designs. (33:2.1, 33:2.2)

The decisive turning point was the Urantia bestowal. When Michael returned from his final bestowal, the Creative Mother Spirit subordinated herself to him, and he thereby acquired jurisdiction over “all power in heaven and on earth.” His experience of living as a creature qualifies him to portray the experiential divinity of the Supreme Being, and no other being holds such qualifications for solitary sovereignty. (33:2.3, 33:2.4)

No other beings in the universes have thus personally exhausted the potentials of present finite experience (33:2.4)

Being sovereign does not mean staying at court. Michael spends much of his time visiting constellation and system headquarters and even individual planets, and he journeys to Paradise and often to Uversa. While he is away, Gabriel takes his place as regent. (33:2.5)

III. The Universe Son and Spirit — Like a Father and a Mother

The Universe Mother Spirit of Salvington belongs to the sixth group of Supreme Spirits and is the 611,121st of that order; she volunteered to accompany Michael when he left Paradise. The Son is the personal sovereign of the universe, but the Spirit is codirector in every detail of its management. The Spirit ever acknowledges the Son as ruler, and the Son always accords the Spirit a co-ordinate position and equality of authority. (33:3.2, 33:3.3)

Such a Divine Minister is in reality the mother of spirits and spirit personalities, the ever-present and all-wise adviser of the Creator Son, a faithful and true manifestation of the Paradise Infinite Spirit. (33:3.3)

At the jubilee of jubilees, when the Son is enthroned as a Master Son after his seventh bestowal, the Universe Spirit for the first time publicly acknowledges her subordination to him. Michael then acknowledges his eternal dependence on her, makes her coruler of his domains, and requires all creatures to pledge loyalty to the Spirit as they had to the Son. Then goes forth the “Proclamation of Equality,” declaring her equal to him in all endowments of personality and attributes of divine character. (33:3.5, 33:3.6)

This is, in deed and in truth, the high ideal of the family and the human institution of voluntary marriage. (33:3.6)

In this scene one partner lays down authority and the other shares it, and each honors the other; it becomes a pattern for the family and for every community. When a free act of subordination is matched by a proclamation of equality, order and equality do not collide. (33:3.6, 33:3.7)

IV. Gabriel — The Chief Executive

The Bright and Morning Star is the personalization of the first concept of identity conceived together by the Son and the Spirit, and only one such being is brought forth in each local universe. Gabriel of Salvington is like the Universe Son in divinity of nature, though considerably limited in the attributes of Deity, and he does not share in creative prerogatives. He embraces the will of the Son combined with the creative imagination of the Spirit, and he understands both the seraphic hosts and material mortals. (33:4.1, 33:4.2, 33:4.3)

Gabriel and his staff were never known to leave their regular work except when Michael was incarnated on a bestowal, and at those times Gabriel was ever attendant on the will of the incarnated Son. Mortals seldom meet him except at resurrection roll calls, but anyone who becomes an administrator, of whatever order, comes under his direction. (33:4.7, 33:4.8)

Gabriel and his staff are not teachers; they are administrators. (33:4.7)

V–VI. The Trinity Ambassadors; General Administration

Local universes are marked by dual supervision, father and mother, yet personalities from Paradise are present too. At their head is the ambassador of the Paradise Trinity, the Union of Days known as Immanuel of Salvington. He is not subject to the local universe government and gives counsel only on request. (33:5.1, 33:5.2, 33:5.3)

He bears the distinction of being the only personality in all Nebadon who has never acknowledged subordination to his brother Michael. (33:5.2)

His liaison brethren, the Faithfuls of Days, serve on the constellation headquarters, and they too never offer advice unless it is asked for. They are the final personal presence of the Stationary Sons of the Trinity serving in advisory roles in the local universes. Below them, the local systems are administered exclusively by personalities native to the local universe. (33:5.3, 33:5.4)

Each level of administration has its own concerns. System governments attend especially to the physical and biologic status of living beings; constellation governments to social and governmental conditions, unification, and stabilization; the universe rulers to the spiritual status of the realms. Universe broadcasts reach every inhabited world whatever its spiritual status, but planetary intercommunication is denied to worlds under spiritual quarantine. (33:6.3, 33:6.5)

Representation within and between universes is also arranged. Ambassadors are appointed by judicial decree to represent this universe to other universes; consuls are appointed by legislative decree to represent constellations; observers are commissioned by executive decree of a System Sovereign to represent their system. Because responsibility is spread so evenly, Michael’s absence in no way interferes with the orderly conduct of universe affairs. (33:6.1, 33:6.4)

VII–VIII. The Courts of Nebadon; The Legislative and Executive Functions

Michael is supremely concerned with only three things, creation, sustenance, and ministry, and he takes no personal part in judicial work. Judging belongs to creatures of high training and actual creature experience. The high courts of Salvington function in seventy branches, and every case is heard by a dual magistracy: one judge of perfection antecedents and one magistrate of ascendant experience. (33:7.1, 33:7.2)

Creators never sit in judgment on their creatures (33:7.1)

Authority is divided by level. Salvington has no true legislative bodies and is concerned largely with adjudication; the legislative assemblies sit on the headquarters of the one hundred constellations; the systems handle executive and administrative work. The high advisory councils of Salvington are in reality superlegislatures, yet they only recommend, without authority of enactment or power of execution. (33:8.1, 33:8.5)

The supreme council on Salvington is made up of three members from each system and seven representatives from each constellation; systems in isolation have no representation and may send only observers. The paper also cautions that “courts” and “assemblies” in the universe are very different from the more primitive and material institutions of Urantia that bear the same names. (33:8.3, 33:8.6)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 33 can look like an organizational chart, but personal relationships run beneath it. The Creator does not judge his creatures, the sovereign proclaims his companion’s equality, and the highest ambassador counsels only when asked. Even when we suffer unfair judgments on earth, the paper assures us that the universe stands on an order in which justice and mercy prevail.
