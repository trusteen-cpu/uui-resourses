Introduction — The Cradle of the Mortal Races

Paper 49 turns our gaze back from the mansion worlds to the starting point. Every inhabited world is evolutionary in origin and nature, the cradle of the mortal races of time and space. And the principle that each stage of the ascent is a training school for the next holds just as true on an evolutionary planet as it does in the Melchizedek school at the universe headquarters. (49:0.1)

These spheres are the spawning ground, the evolutionary cradle, of the mortal races of time and space. (49:0.1)

A local system is limited to about one thousand evolutionary worlds, but Satania is an unfinished system with only 619 inhabited worlds. Urantia is number 606 among them—the 606th world on which human beings appeared. The oldest, world number one, is Anova, one of forty-four satellites circling an enormous dark planet, and it has already reached an advanced stage of civilization. (49:0.2, 49:0.3, 49:0.5)

Not every planet is fit for life. Small planets with a high rate of axial revolution are wholly unsuited, and planets that are too large produce oppressive gravity. Yet the moons that circle such giant planets are often close to Urantia in size and almost ideal for habitation. Anova is just such a satellite world. (49:0.4, 49:0.5)

I. The Planetary Life — Evolution, Not Accident

Cosmic evolution is not always predictable, but it is never a matter of chance. The Life Carriers are the living catalyzers who set off the first reactions of material life, and they design the plant and animal life of each world differently. Those differences, however, are not whims; they are decisions made in keeping with the evolutionary pattern of Nebadon. (49:1.1, 49:1.2, 49:1.3)

Cosmic evolution may not always be understandable (predictable), but it is strictly nonaccidental. (49:1.1)

Evolution is the rule of human development, but the process varies greatly from world to world. Life may begin in one center, or in three as it did on Urantia, and on atmospheric worlds it usually has a marine origin. The Life Carriers have great latitude at this initial stage, yet that latitude operates within law and order. (49:1.3, 49:1.4)

Mortal man is not an evolutionary accident. (49:1.6)

Vegetable life always comes before animal life, and every animal type is developed from the basic patterns of the plant kingdom. Nor is the rate of reproduction what drives evolution: mice multiply far faster than elephants, yet elephants evolve more rapidly. The paper adds that superior strains of living protoplasm should be guarded with care, and that most inhabited worlds value them far more highly than Urantia does. (49:1.5, 49:1.6, 49:1.7)

II. Planetary Physical Types — Seven Body Designs

The Life Carriers keep a standard pattern for each system but modify it to fit the physical conditions of each planet. The result is seven distinct physical types and thousands upon thousands of minor variants. The greatest single factor in these differences is the atmosphere. (49:2.1, 49:2.10)

The account of the elemental types stirs the imagination. On some worlds the first civilization of a primitive race takes shape in the air and treetops; on others, amid the shallow waters of sheltered tropic basins and in the marine gardens of the dawn races. Even on Urantia there was a long age when primitive man lived mostly in the treetops. Yet these beings are neither human fishes nor human birds; they are distinctly mortal. Height, too, varies with a planet’s gravity, and the average in Nebadon is a trifle under seven feet. (49:2.17, 49:2.18, 49:2.20)

What is striking is that none of these differences touches the intellectual or spiritual life. Neither the variations among electric types nor those of the unnamed types affect mind or spirit. The body’s design is a garment fitted to the environment, not the essence of who a person is. (49:2.22, 49:2.26)

III. Worlds of the Nonbreathers — An Extreme Adjustment

Mortals live even on planets with little or no air. This type accounts for less than seven per cent of the inhabited worlds of Orvonton and less than three per cent in Nebadon, and there are only nine such worlds in Satania. Because Satania still abounds in meteoric bodies, worlds without a protective atmosphere suffer incessant bombardment, so the advanced nonbreathing races build electrical installations that consume or shunt the meteors. (49:3.1, 49:3.2, 49:3.3)

They neither eat food nor drink water; their metabolism and nervous reactions are radically different; and even in survival they differ, being candidates for Spirit fusion. Still, they live out their lives with the same relative trials and joys as other mortals. And the paper tells us that such a race inhabits a sphere in close proximity to Urantia. (49:3.4, 49:3.5, 49:3.6)

In mind and character the nonbreathers do not differ from other mortal types. (49:3.5)

IV. Evolutionary Will Creatures — Different, Yet Alike

All mortals of will dignity are erect, two-footed animals. There are six basic evolutionary races; the average endowment of special physical senses is twelve; and the span of life ranges from twenty-five years on primitive worlds to nearly five hundred on the older spheres. Sex equality prevails on all advanced worlds. (49:4.1, 49:4.2, 49:4.3, 49:4.6)

Family life is fairly uniform on every type of planet, and children are usually born one at a time. Agriculture is universal on all atmospheric worlds, and mortals in their early days struggle against microscopic foes. Human beings are gregarious, both tribal and racial, and these tendencies can be modified only by advancing civilization and gradual spiritualization. (49:4.4, 49:4.5, 49:4.7)

We do not regard a planet as having emerged from barbarism so long as one sex seeks to tyrannize over the other. (49:4.4)

However different the bodies, mind is the bestowal of the Infinite Spirit and functions in much the same way everywhere, and the careers that follow death are very much alike. Yet mind itself is mortal. Survival depends on the birth and growth of the immortal soul through the ministry of the Adjuster; at the very least, a person must not have turned against the Adjuster’s work. (49:4.8, 49:4.9)

The mind of man is mortal; only the bestowed spirit is immortal. (49:4.9)

V. The Planetary Series of Mortals — Seven Viewpoints

Mortals can be classified from seven viewpoints: adjustment to planetary environment, brain type, spirit reception, planetary epochs, creature kinship, Adjuster fusion, and the techniques of terrestrial escape. In several of these Urantia holds an exceptional place, because it is an experimental planet, a world where the Life Carriers try to produce beneficial variations in the standard life designs. (49:5.1, 49:5.11)

VI. Terrestrial Escape — Many Roads Beyond Death

Mortals of every type alike receive the ministry of Thought Adjusters and guardian angels, and all alike go on to the morontia worlds. How they leave the planet, though, depends on their spiritual development. Sleeping survivors awaken at the dispensational roll calls or at the special resurrections held at least every millennium, while those who reach the third circle receive personal guardians of destiny and may be repersonalized on the first mansion world on the third day. (49:6.1, 49:6.2, 49:6.8, 49:6.9)

These orders shift as a planet matures. Primitive mortals of the early ages are too immature for fusion with their Adjusters, so the guardian seraphim awaken them in conjunction with an individualized portion of the spirit of the Third Source and Center. The cessation of nonsalvable personalities, too, takes place not on the planet but on the mansion worlds. On worlds nearing light and life, by contrast, those who fuse with their Adjusters are spared death, submitted to Son seizure, and carried directly to the resurrection halls of the higher morontia spheres. (49:6.6, 49:6.7, 49:6.19)

Those who pass by the mansion worlds do not simply skip them. Before going farther, they journey back as instructors to the worlds they missed, gaining experience as teachers in the places they passed by as students. The universe road leaves no blanks unfilled. (49:6.17)

Natural death becomes decreasingly frequent on these spheres long settled in light and life. (49:6.21)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

The universe of Paper 49 is astonishingly diverse. Ways of breathing, heights, brain structures, and planetary histories all differ. Yet beneath all that variety lie the same mind, the same Adjuster, and the same path of ascent. The paper trains us to see past differences in appearance to a sameness of essence. (49:4.8, 49:6.1)
