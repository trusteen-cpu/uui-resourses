Introduction — From Paradise Sons to Local Universe Sons

The Sons of God introduced in earlier papers were of Paradise origin. Nebadon has one Creator Son, Michael; its full quota of 1,062 Avonal or Magisterial Sons; and, by the author’s estimate, between fifteen and twenty thousand Trinity Teacher Sons. Paper 35 now turns to the Sons born in the local universe itself, the offspring of the Creator Son in varied association with the Universe Mother Spirit. (35:0.1, 35:0.2)

The pattern shows that what happens above is repeated below. Just as Paradise Deity brings forth three orders of Sons, so the Son and the Spirit of the local universe bring forth three orders of their own. This paper introduces each in turn and leads us through the worlds of the Salvington circuit where each order teaches ascending mortals. (35:0.7)

I–II. The Father Melchizedek; The Melchizedek Sons — The Eldest Sons of the Universe

After administrative personalities such as Gabriel come into being, a new creative union of the Son and the Spirit produces the original Melchizedek, the Father Melchizedek, who then collaborates with them to bring into existence the whole group bearing that name. He is Gabriel’s first executive associate: Gabriel is occupied more with policies, Melchizedek with practical procedures and the emergency commissions. The two are never away from Salvington at the same time. (35:1.1, 35:1.2)

The Melchizedeks are the first order of divine Sons to draw near enough to lower creature life to serve the evolutionary races without incarnation. Standing about midway between the highest Divinity and the lowest will creatures, they are natural intermediaries. All intelligent life finds in them understanding friends, sympathetic teachers, and wise counselors. (35:2.1)

The seraphic orders, the angels, delight to work with the Melchizedeks (35:2.1)
not once throughout all the superuniverse of Orvonton have these Melchizedek Sons ever betrayed their trust. (35:2.2)

They are the pattern of self-government. Like the eldest son in a large family they take on much work of their own accord, patrol their native universe on their own initiative, and report to the Creator Son independently of the regular channels. Yet they are not infallible. Alone on planetary missions they have sometimes erred in minor matters; then they receive instruction on Salvington and return to service on the third day. (35:2.3, 35:2.8)

The Melchizedeks are well-nigh perfect in wisdom, but they are not infallible in judgment. (35:2.8)

III–IV. The Melchizedek Worlds; Special Work of the Melchizedeks

The seventy primary spheres of the Salvington circuit, each with six tributaries, make 490 worlds known together as the Melchizedek University. The first group of seven is supervised by the Melchizedeks, and every evolutionary mortal who graduates from constellation training lands on the pilot world Melchizedek to begin the Salvington educational regime. The author says this is probably the most interesting place in all Nebadon for an ascender. (35:3.1, 35:3.2, 35:3.11)

And never will you forget your reactions to the first day of life on this unique world, not even after you have reached your Paradise destination. (35:3.11)

This method of reviewing and co-ordinating the life already lived, before stepping into a new stage, is striking. Experience does not become wisdom merely by piling up; it becomes a foundation for the next step only when we look back and connect its parts. Though Nebadon stands low in spiritual achievement, its administrative troubles have made it a vast clinic for nearby creations, so that more than one hundred thousand foreign students always attend the Melchizedek schools. (35:3.19, 35:3.22)

The pilot world holds the schools of universe administration and spiritual wisdom, along with schools devoted to single lines of research such as energy, matter, organization, communication, records, and ethics. In the Melchizedek College of Spiritual Endowment, all orders of Sons, even the Paradise orders, join Melchizedek and seraphic teachers in training evangels who proclaim spiritual liberty and divine sonship to distant worlds. The highest course in administration, the College of High Ethics, is presided over by the Father Melchizedek himself. (35:3.20, 35:3.21, 35:3.22)

Melchizedeks act first in every emergency; on Edentia they are called the emergency Sons. Seven times in Nebadon one of them has served in the likeness of mortal flesh, and one such was the Prince of Salem in Abraham’s day on Urantia. Fearing that the light of life would be extinguished in a time of growing spiritual darkness, the planetary receivers approved his incarnation, and he passed the truth on to Abraham and his associates. (35:4.2, 35:4.4, 35:4.5)

Whenever and wherever special help is needed, there you will find one or more of the Melchizedek Sons. (35:4.2)

V–VII. The Vorondadek Sons; The Constellation Fathers; The Vorondadek Worlds

The second order, the Vorondadeks, are better known as Constellation Fathers, since one of them heads each constellation government. Nebadon has one million of them, and their number does not increase. They lack the brilliant versatility of the Melchizedeks but are even more reliable as rulers and administrators, and they excel all orders of sonship in stability of purpose and divinity of judgment. (35:5.1, 35:5.2, 35:5.3)

never in all the history of Nebadon has a Vorondadek been found in contempt of the universe government. (35:5.4)

They serve as ambassadors to other universes, as consuls representing constellations, and as Most High observers on isolated worlds; as a result of all this service they have become the historians of the local universes. At least three Vorondadeks are assigned to each constellation, and the one hundred Constellation Fathers form the supreme advisory cabinet of the Creator Son. (35:5.5, 35:5.6, 35:5.7, 35:6.2)

The principle that no major policy goes forward unless all three Most Highs agree on every detail shows that the higher the authority, the more it is exercised through agreement. On the forty-nine Vorondadek worlds, ascenders take part in enacting the actual general legislation of the local universe under the tutelage of the senior Vorondadeks. (35:6.3, 35:7.1, 35:7.2)

The author admits that while revealing the new activities ascenders will meet on the Vorondadek worlds is not forbidden, there are no words to convey them to the material mind and no human occupations to serve as illustrations. Such moments, where revelation halts at the limits of language, honestly tell us that the ascending life ahead exceeds anything we can now imagine. (35:7.3)

VIII–IX. The Lanonandek Sons; The Lanonandek Rulers — The Risks and Rewards of Freedom

The third order, the Lanonandeks, are best known as System Sovereigns and Planetary Princes. Being lower in divinity level, they were the first students of the Melchizedek University. Nebadon began with exactly twelve million Lanonandeks, and once divided into three classes in their final tests, they cannot advance from one class to another. (35:8.1, 35:8.2, 35:8.3, 35:8.7)

System Sovereigns rule in commissions of two or three on each system headquarters, and the Constellation Father names one of them chief every dekamillennium. System governments do not suddenly change personnel unless some tragedy occurs, and when a sovereign is recalled, the constellation’s supreme council fills the place from the reserves. (35:9.2, 35:9.3)

Because Lanonandeks can draw nearer to the lower creatures, they render greater service, but they also stand in greater danger of going astray. A System Sovereign is well-nigh sovereign in local affairs and is not supervised by Trinity observers, so that office is the one place where disloyalty to Michael’s will could most easily entrench itself. Over seven hundred Lanonandeks in Nebadon have rebelled, and only three of them were System Sovereigns. (35:8.15, 35:9.5, 35:9.6)

The author insists this is no fault in creatorship. They could have been made perfect, but were created as they are so as to understand evolutionary creatures better. Freedom of choice causes great trouble in early ages, yet the high rulers hold that the greater loyalty of thoroughly tested Sons will more than compensate for that confusion. (35:9.7, 35:9.8)

They could have been made divinely perfect, but they were so created that they might better understand, and draw near to, the evolutionary creatures dwelling on the worlds of time and space. (35:9.7)
Only a bestowal Son can re-establish interplanetary lines of communication on such a spiritually isolated world. (35:9.9)

There is a plan of salvage even for the wayward Sons, and many have accepted it. Yet they never again serve in the positions in which they defaulted; after rehabilitation they are assigned to custodial duties and physical administration. (35:9.10)

X. The Lanonandek Worlds — A School Where We Practice What We Learn

On the third group of seven worlds in the Salvington circuit, former System Sovereigns serve as administrative teachers of ascenders and seraphim. Here ascenders take part in actually co-ordinating the administrative pronouncements of the ten thousand local systems. Most of the salvaged Lanonandeks also serve in connection with these worlds as custodians of planetary affairs. (35:10.1, 35:10.4)

The universe educational system sponsored by the Melchizedeks is practical, progressive, meaningful, and experiential. (35:10.3)

The paper’s final words are fair-minded. Many Lanonandeks in the older systems have set wonderful records of service and spiritual achievement; despite their tendency to err through fallacies of personal liberty, they are a noble, faithful, and loyal group. (35:10.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

The three orders of Sons in Paper 35 each display a different virtue. The Melchizedeks show willing service and trustworthiness; the Vorondadeks, steady judgment and agreement; the Lanonandeks, the risks of freedom and the worth of loyalty that has stood the test. And since every one of these Sons appears as a teacher of ascenders, universe administration turns out to be a system of education as well.
