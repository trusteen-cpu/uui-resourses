Introduction — Triads Are Not the Trinity

Paper 104 begins by drawing a boundary: the Trinity concept of revealed religion must not be confused with the triad beliefs of evolutionary religion. People came to think in threes for homely reasons. Fingers have three joints, three legs are the fewest that will steady a stool, and three support points keep up a tent. For a long time primitive man could not even count beyond three. (104:0.1)

Human experience is woven in triads: yesterday, today, and tomorrow; sunrise, noon, and sunset; father, mother, and child. So many peoples, from Persia to Scandinavia, had triad gods, yet these were not true trinities. At times evolutionary triads mingled with revealed concepts until the two could hardly be told apart. The author is a Melchizedek of Nebadon. (104:0.2, 104:0.3, 104:5.13)

I. Urantian Trinity Concepts — Revealed Again and Again

The paper reports that the Trinity was presented on Urantia several times, only to fade each time. Each revelation was received differently according to the understanding and existing beliefs of its age. (104:1.1)

Most of Melchizedek’s disciples thought the Trinity consisted of the three Most Highs of Norlatiadek; a few took it to be the System Sovereign, the Constellation Father, and the local universe Creator Deity. The Salem missionaries spread the teaching through Eurasia and northern Africa, but in later ages it became mixed with the evolutionary triads. (104:1.3, 104:1.4)

Hinduism developed the trinitarian concept as Being, Intelligence, and Joy, and Buddhism as Teacher, Law, and Brotherhood, and later as Supreme Lord, Holy Spirit, and Incarnate Savior. The paper regards these as real trinitarian postulates: the idea of a threefold manifestation of one God. (104:1.5, 104:1.6)

A true trinity conception is not just a grouping together of three separate gods. (104:1.7)

The Hebrews and the Muslims, by contrast, in their zeal for the one God, found it hard to distinguish between worshiping three gods and worshiping one Deity in triune manifestation. The paper says the trinity idea takes best hold in religions that combine a firm monotheistic tradition with doctrinal elasticity. (104:1.8, 104:1.9)

The first Christian trinity, proclaimed at Antioch, consisted of God, his Word, and his Wisdom. Paul knew of the Paradise Trinity, but like the other apostles he confused Jesus, the Creator Son, with the Eternal Son. The Christian concept that gained recognition near the close of the first century was made up of the Universal Father, the Creator Son of Nebadon, and the Divine Minister of Salvington. (104:1.11, 104:1.12)

But though the Christian concept of the Trinity erred in fact, it was practically true with respect to spiritual relationships. (104:1.13)

II. Trinity Unity and Deity Plurality

Monotheism grew as a philosophic protest against the inconsistency of polytheism. It passed through the departmentalizing of the gods, through henotheism, which exalted one god above the many, and finally to the exclusion of all but one. But a solitary God without relationships tends, given time, to be reduced in philosophy to an impersonal Absolute. Trinitarianism grew out of the experiential protest against this difficulty. (104:2.1, 104:2.2)

Personality in Deity demands that such Deity exist in relation to other and equal personal Deity. (104:2.2)

The Trinity concept helps us grasp love and law together. One who has come to know the Father’s love through faith soon discovers that this faith does not change the laws of the material universe. Widening horizons require him to recognize, alongside the Paradise Father, the reality of Paradise Deity as universal law—the Trinity sovereignty reaching out even to the local universes. (104:2.3)

not a personality but nonetheless a true and absolute reality (104:2.4)

The Trinity is not the sum of the attributes of the three Deities but a supersummative reality that emerges from their union. Justice is an example. Jesus taught that justice is never a personal act; it is always a group function. The Gods themselves do not administer justice as persons; they do so collectively, as the Trinity. (104:2.4, 104:2.5)

Neither do the Gods, as persons, administer justice. (104:2.5)

A mind at home with this concept is prepared to accept other threefold relationships. Theology may be satisfied with the Paradise Trinity, but philosophy and cosmology require the recognition of other triune associations in which the Infinite functions in non-Father capacities—force, energy, causation, potentiality, gravity, pattern, and more. (104:2.6)

III. Trinities and Triunities — Relationships among the Seven Absolutes

Consistency requires us to see, beyond the Trinity of three Deities, relationships among all seven Absolutes. Reason demands the unity of reality, but finite experience requires the postulate of plural Absolutes and their coordination. Without coordinate existences there could be no diversity of relationships, no qualifying or modifying of the absolute. (104:3.1, 104:3.4)

Mankind is passing through an age of expanding horizons, and its cosmic philosophy must grow as fast. Man believes in the unity of the cosmos yet sees the diversity of existence; he believes in the changelessness of Deity yet lives in a universe of constant growth. The fullness of infinity must be reconciled with the incompleteness of the evolving universes, and segmented and qualified so that the mortal mind can grasp it. (104:3.2, 104:3.3)

The key to the seven triunities is that the Universal Father is always the primal member of each. He is the Absolute Center, the Primal Cause, and the Limitless Energizer. (104:4.1)

The Universal Father is the personal cause of the Absolutes; he is the absolute of Absolutes. (104:4.1)

The contrast between the first and second triunities stands out. The first concerns evolving personalities; the second concerns evolving universes. In religious experience we meet the God who is love, but that insight must never eclipse our recognition of the cosmic fact of the pattern which is Paradise. The paper declares that the power-pattern and the loving person are one and the same universal reality. (104:4.14, 104:4.15)

Love may characterize the divinity of the first triunity, but pattern is the galactic manifestation of the second triunity. (104:4.15)

The description of the fourth triunity is especially vivid. The endless possibilities of the Unqualified Absolute center around the absolutum of the Isle of Paradise, and from what would otherwise be static quiescence there emanate unimaginable agitations. The paper pictures the material heart of the infinite cosmos throbbing in harmony with the Father’s plan. (104:4.26, 104:4.28)

The paper discloses that there are fifteen triune associations of the First Source and Center, eight of which are not revealed; these concern realities beyond the level of supremacy. The triunities do not add to the Father’s infinity, but they make possible subinfinite and subabsolute expressions of his reality, disclosing new values and meanings. (104:4.45, 104:4.47)

The triunities are the functional balance wheel of infinity, the unification of the uniqueness of the Seven Infinity Absolutes. (104:4.46)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 104 is one of the most abstract in The Urantia Book, yet beneath it lie insights that touch daily life. Personality seeks relationship; unity coordinates diversity rather than erasing it. Love and law, personality and pattern, are not opposites but two manifestations of a single Source. (104:3.14, 104:4.15)
