Introduction — A Strange Presence in the Upper Room

About one o’clock on the day of Pentecost, the one hundred and twenty believers at prayer became aware of a strange presence in the room. They were conscious of a new and profound sense of spiritual joy, security, and confidence, and soon afterward they felt a strong urge to go out and proclaim the gospel publicly. Peter declared that this was the promised Spirit of Truth and proposed that they go to the temple. (194:0.1, 194:0.2)

They had been trained to preach the fatherhood of God and the sonship of man, but in their moment of triumph the greatest news they could think of was the risen Master. Here the narrator points out the crossroads that would shape all later history. (194:0.3)

but they unintentionally stumbled into the error of substituting some of the facts associated with the gospel for the gospel message itself. (194:0.3)
Source 194:0.4
MessageWhat it combined
The gospel of the kingdomThe fact of the fatherhood of God + the truth of the sonship-brotherhood of men that follows from it
Christianity as it developed from that dayThe fact of God as the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ + the experience of fellowship with the risen and glorified Christ

The text seeks to understand this change rather than to scold it. In their triumph over the forces that had sought to destroy their Master, and in their overwhelming assurance that he lived and their friendship was not ended, it is easy to see how they came to preach a new gospel about Jesus. This generous view is worth imitating when we read church history. (194:0.5, 194:0.7)

I. The Pentecost Sermon — From Hiding to the Temple

After forty days in hiding, the apostles appeared boldly in the temple, crowded for the feast of Pentecost. About two o’clock Peter made a fervent appeal from the very spot where the Master had last taught, and more than two thousand souls responded. For more than an hour and a half six apostles proclaimed the message in Greek, Hebrew, and Aramaic. (194:1.1, 194:1.2)

The Jewish leaders were astonished at their boldness, but so many believed that they dared not interfere. At half past four the new believers went down to the pool of Siloam to be baptized, and the work was not finished until after dark. Yet they had not broken away from Judaism; for a time those who believed in Jesus remained a sect within it. (194:1.3, 194:1.4, 194:1.5)

II. The Significance of Pentecost — What the Spirit of Truth Does

Jesus’ message was an effective solvent for the spiritual difficulties of the people of his day. The Spirit of Truth, sent after his departure, restates that message for each new generation, so that every age has its own new version of the gospel. Truth is not bound to the forms of one age; it comes alive anew in each generation. (194:2.1)

The text corrects a common misunderstanding. Do not expect to become strongly conscious of the Spirit of Truth in your intellect. The spirit creates a consciousness not of himself but of Michael, the Son. (194:2.4)

The proof, therefore, of your fellowship with the Spirit of Truth is not to be found in your consciousness of this spirit but rather in your experience of enhanced fellowship with Michael. (194:2.4)
Jesus lived a life which is a revelation of man submitted to the Father’s will, not an example for any man literally to attempt to follow. (194:2.8)

His life and death soon became a new gospel of ransom. Yet the text says that even this greatly distorted message carried many of the fundamental truths of the gospel of the kingdom, and that the concealed truths of the fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of men will one day emerge to transform civilization. Nor did these intellectual errors block spiritual growth: within a month of the spirit’s coming, the apostles made more spiritual progress than in almost four years with the Master. (194:2.8, 194:2.9)

Man now receives a threefold spirit endowment: the spirit of the Father in the Thought Adjuster, the spirit of the Son in the Spirit of Truth, and the spirit of the Spirit in the Holy Spirit. The text goes on to list seven higher spirit influences that rest upon a progressing humanity. (194:2.11, 194:2.12)

III. What Happened at Pentecost — God Seeking Man

In later times Pentecost was confused with outbursts of rampant emotionalism. But the chief mission of the outpoured spirit is to teach the truths of the Father’s love and the Son’s mercy. When people bear the fruits of the spirit, they are simply showing forth the traits the Master manifested in his own earthly life. (194:3.1)

The Creator Son, in the flesh, revealed God to men; the Spirit of Truth, in the heart, reveals the Creator Son to men. (194:3.1)

In a world where sin seems to win, does faith finally prevail? The text answers yes. The day of the cross was dark, but the resurrection morning was bright, and Pentecost brighter still. The religion of Jesus is not a means of escape from life; it is a religion of faith, hope, and love that exalts the life we now live in the flesh. (194:3.2, 194:3.3)

If religion is an opiate to the people, it is not the religion of Jesus. (194:3.4)

Pentecost was a liberation on many levels. Gathering what the text lists in turn shows how many fetters the gift of the spirit loosed from religion. (194:3.5, 194:3.6)

For all time, Pentecost disassociates the idea of spiritual experience from the notion of especially favorable environments. (194:3.10)

The text stresses that the Spirit of Truth did not come to the apostles alone. All one hundred and twenty men and women in the upper room received the new teacher, as did all the honest of heart throughout the world. Each soul received him according to its love of truth and its capacity to grasp spiritual realities. Since the same thing happened at the same time to the believers at Philadelphia and Alexandria, the company of believers was truly of one heart and soul. (194:3.6, 194:3.17)

The text says that Pentecost made possible a religion neither radical nor conservative. Jesus’ earth life is the fixed point that anchors it in time, while the Spirit of Truth enables it to grow without end. Pentecost was also designed to lessen the self-assertion of individuals, nations, and races. Amid destructive wars, the one conqueror whose fame has grown is the gospel of Jesus, which overcomes evil with good. (194:3.8, 194:3.12, 194:3.18)

since Pentecost, man is still searching for God, but there shines out over the world the spectacle of God also seeking for man and sending his spirit to dwell within him when he has found him. (194:3.13)

Prayer, too, is put in its proper place. Prayer did not bring the spirit, but it had much to do with the capacity of receptivity of each believer. Prayer does not move God’s heart to give more; it digs deeper channels through which the divine bestowals may flow. The apostles’ experience, likewise, was not an emotional outburst but an intelligent self-surrender. (194:3.16, 194:3.20)

Prayer did not bring the spirit on the day of Pentecost (194:3.20)

IV. Beginnings of the Christian Church — From the Brotherhood of Jesus to the Church

The apostles, broken before the cross, were delivered from despair by the resurrection, and with the new teacher of Pentecost they became bold followers of a living Lord. The text sums up the change in a single sentence. (194:4.1, 194:4.2)

God is not a doctrine in their minds; he has become a living presence in their souls. (194:4.2)

The early community was simple. They met daily in the temple and broke bread in their homes, ending their common meals with the Lord’s Supper. At first they baptized in the name of Jesus, and baptism was the only requirement. It was simply the brotherhood of Jesus, without organization. (194:4.3, 194:4.8, 194:4.9)

But the message had become the gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ. The fact of his first coming and the hope of his soon second coming became the center of their preaching. A marvelous brotherly love sprang up in these communities, but it was a fellowship of believers in Jesus, not of brothers in the family of the Father in heaven. (194:4.5, 194:4.6)

They made the great mistake of using the living and illustrative commentary on the gospel of the kingdom for that gospel, but even that represented the greatest religion mankind had ever known. (194:4.6)

The experiment in common ownership did not come directly from Jesus’ teaching; it grew out of the conviction that his return was imminent. The text frankly records that this unthinking brotherly love ended in calamity and sorrow. Good motives, without wisdom, do not bear fruit. (194:4.7)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 194 shows two things together. One is the glory of Pentecost, in which God seeks out man and dwells within him; the other is the human limitation that mistook the results of the gospel for the gospel itself. Yet because the Spirit of Truth restates Jesus’ message for every generation, the concealed truths of the fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of men can always emerge again. (194:2.1, 194:2.8)
