Introduction — Melchizedek, the Emergency Son

The previous paper named Melchizedek of Salem as the third of Urantia’s five epochal revelations. Paper 93 introduces in detail the one who brought it. The Melchizedeks are widely known as emergency Sons, because when an extraordinary problem arises or something unusual is to be attempted, it is quite often a Melchizedek who takes the assignment; they have the peculiar ability to function even on the physical level of personality manifestation. (93:0.1)

The Melchizedeks have been exceedingly active on Urantia. One corps of twelve served with the Life Carriers, and after the Caligastia secession a later corps of twelve became receivers for the planet. These receivers returned after the default of Adam and Eve and remained in charge until Jesus became the titular Planetary Prince. Machiventa was one of these twelve receivers. (93:0.2)

I. The Machiventa Incarnation — A Self-Chosen Emergency Bestowal

In the millenniums after the miscarriage of the Adamic mission, revealed truth was threatened with extinction. Mankind progressed intellectually but slowly lost ground spiritually, and by about 3000 B.C. the idea of God had grown very dim. The receivers knew that Michael’s bestowal was coming, but not how soon. (93:1.1, 93:1.2)

About 3000 B.C. the concept of God had grown very hazy in the minds of men. (93:1.1)

They appealed to the Most Highs of Edentia and then to the Father Melchizedek, but were told only to uphold truth in the manner of their own election. So Machiventa volunteered to do what had been done only six times in all the history of Nebadon: to personalize on earth as a temporary man of the realm. The story shows the responsibility of finding a way forward from one’s own post when not everything is settled from above. (93:1.2, 93:1.3)

II. The Sage of Salem — Ninety-Four Years as a Man

Machiventa was materialized, unseen by human eyes, 1,973 years before the birth of Jesus. He was first observed when he entered the tent of Amdon, a Chaldean herder, and that evening he ate material food for the first time. His whole mission was contained in his first words. (93:2.1, 93:2.2)

“I am Melchizedek, priest of El Elyon, the Most High, the one and only God.” (93:2.1)

He soon became known as the priest of El Elyon and the sage of Salem, and Salem later became Jerusalem. His body resembled a human one, but it was unique in several ways. (93:2.4)

III–IV. Melchizedek’s Teachings; The Salem Religion — Favor Gained by Faith

Melchizedek founded schools at Salem patterned on the old system of the Sethite priests. He taught one universal God, yet allowed people to associate this teaching with the Most High of the constellation of Norlatiadek. To most he taught no more than that, while to gifted disciples such as Nordan the Kenite he taught even the truths of the superuniverse and Havona. He taught each person only as much as he could receive. (93:3.1, 93:3.2, 93:3.4, 93:3.6)

But he subordinated everything to the doctrine of one God, the universe Deity and divine Father. He also taught that another Son of God would come, born of a woman, and so prepared the way for Michael’s bestowal. Salem worship was very simple: each believer memorized and subscribed to three beliefs. (93:3.6, 93:3.7, 93:4.1)

Even this brief creed was too advanced for the people of that day. So convinced were they that man was born under forfeit to the gods that they could not grasp the idea of divine favor given freely, by faith. Melchizedek therefore offered a sacrament of bread and wine in place of blood sacrifice, but he never succeeded in fully uprooting the urge to sacrifice. (93:4.5, 93:4.14)

But Abraham did believe halfheartedly, and even that was “counted for righteousness.” (93:4.5)

Like Jesus, Melchizedek attended strictly to the mission of his bestowal. He did not set out to reform the mores or spread scientific knowledge, but concentrated on two tasks: to keep alive the truth of the one God and to prepare the way for the bestowal of a Paradise Son. (93:4.15)

And Michael, when he appeared on earth, confirmed all that Melchizedek had taught concerning the Paradise Father. (93:3.8)

V–VI. The Selection of Abraham; Melchizedek’s Covenant with Abraham — From Conquest to a Spiritual Kingdom

Although it may be an error to speak of “chosen people,” it is not a mistake to refer to Abraham as a chosen individual. (93:5.1)

Salem was chosen for two reasons. Palestine was then central to the world’s trade, travel, and civilization, and thus well suited for Michael’s later appearance; and there contact could be made with the children of Terah, marked by intelligence, initiative, and sincerity. Terah’s family had been half converted in Ur through the preaching of Ovid, and Abraham came to Salem with his nephew Lot. (93:5.2, 93:5.3, 93:5.4, 93:5.5)

Abraham, however, had kingly ambitions. He confederated seven tribes, defeated the petty kings who had raided Lot’s property, and gave a tenth of the spoils to Salem. Melchizedek, who was not militaristic, accepted a defensive policy but would not approve of conquest. (93:5.9, 93:5.11, 93:5.12, 93:5.13)

When Abraham came to him, troubled that he had no son to succeed him, Melchizedek persuaded him to give up material conquest for the spiritual concept of the kingdom of heaven. Then, bidding him number the stars, he made a formal covenant with him. (93:6.1, 93:6.3)

God agrees to do everything; man only agrees to believe God’s promises and follow his instructions. (93:6.4)

When Isaac was born, Abraham had the covenant put in writing and changed his name from Abram to Abraham. After the reconciliation he again took up the civil and military leadership of Salem and greatly improved its school and missionary work. The text portrays him as a shrewd and efficient businessman—not overly pious, but thoroughly sincere, and one who truly believed in Melchizedek. (93:6.5, 93:6.8)

VII. The Melchizedek Missionaries — A Gospel Submerged

The Salem missionaries went to Egypt, Mesopotamia, and Asia Minor, and from Lake Van onward to Europe, the British Isles, Iceland, China, and Japan. The text honors their lives as a heroic chapter in the annals of the human race. Yet outside Palestine the idea of one God never claimed the continued allegiance of a whole tribe or race, and the gospel was absorbed into older cults such as those of the Great Mother and the Sun. (93:7.1, 93:7.2, 93:7.3)

A new revelation is always contaminated by the older evolutionary beliefs. (93:7.4)

VIII–IX. Departure of Melchizedek; After Melchizedek’s Departure — The Forgotten Teacher

Shortly after the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah, Machiventa decided to end his bestowal. The chief reason was that people were beginning to regard him as a demigod and to revere him with superstitious fear; he also wished to leave while Abraham was still living, so that the truth would become firmly established in his followers’ minds. One night he said good night, retired to his tent, and in the morning was gone. (93:8.1)

Abraham never fully overcame his grief at losing his teacher, and for a time he became fearful and timid. Even so, he again became the spiritual leader of Palestine and died confident in the faith he had learned from Melchizedek. In later generations the story came to seem a myth, but Joseph firmly believed in Melchizedek and chose civil administration over command of the Egyptian armies. (93:9.2, 93:9.3, 93:9.4, 93:9.5)

The text explains that what the Old Testament describes as conversations between Abraham and God were really conferences between Abraham and Melchizedek, and that during the editing in the Babylonian captivity the records were altered and most traces of Melchizedek erased. As a result his teaching about the promised bestowal Son was also lost, and very few recognized Michael when he came. The passage shows how easily the preservation of truth can fail. (93:9.7, 93:9.9, 93:9.10)

X. Present Status of Machiventa Melchizedek — An Open Future

On the third day after his disappearance from Salem, Machiventa appeared among his eleven fellows and resumed his career as a planetary receiver. His coming and going were marked by no special announcement and no resurrection roll call. Yet he did not leave the flesh until he had been released by the Father Melchizedek and his bestowal approved by Gabriel. (93:10.1, 93:10.2)

Such prospects make Urantia one of the most interesting and intriguing spheres in all of Nebadon. (93:10.6, 93:10.8)

This is the story of Machiventa Melchizedek, one of the most unique of all characters ever to become connected with the history of Urantia (93:10.11)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 93 is the story of an emergency Son who kept alive the truth of one God and the gospel of faith in a time of deepening darkness. The covenant in which God promises to do everything and man agrees only to believe reads as an invitation to receive salvation as a gift, not a bargain. At the same time, Abraham’s ambition and fear, and the slow forgetting of the truth, show that the road of faith is never smooth.
