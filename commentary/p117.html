Introduction — What Happens When We Do God’s Will

Near the close of Part III, Paper 117 takes up the Supreme Being directly. Its author is a Mighty Messenger temporarily sojourning on Urantia, and its first sentence is the key to the whole paper: whenever anyone, anywhere, does the will of God, the potential of the Supreme becomes one step more actual. (117:0.1, 117:7.18)

To the extent that we do the will of God in whatever universe station we may have our existence, in that measure the almighty potential of the Supreme becomes one step more actual. (117:0.1)

The will of God is the purpose of the First Source and Center. It exists in different ways on different levels of Deity, and God the Supreme is becoming the place where that will finds its highest expression in the finite. (117:0.1)

So on the day the whole universe fully lives the will of God, the time-space creations will be settled in light and life, and the personality of God the Supreme will emerge as fact. The parts of the grand universe grow as a reflection of the Supreme’s evolution, and the Supreme is the cumulative total of all that evolution. (117:0.2, 117:0.4)

I. Nature of the Supreme Being — The Deity Where Creator and Creature Meet

The first section paints the Supreme in many aspects. He is the oversoul of the grand universe, the consciousness of the finite cosmos, and the personification of Creator-creature experience. Above all, he is where the three values of truth, beauty, and goodness come together in the finite. (117:1.1, 117:1.7)

The Supreme is the beauty of physical harmony, the truth of intellectual meaning, and the goodness of spiritual value. (117:1.1)

The Gods descend into time and space in the persons of the Supreme Creators, and creatures ascend in quest of the Father. This great procession of descent and ascent reveals the evolution of the Supreme, in whom those who descend and those who ascend come to understand each other and discover their brotherhood. The will of the Supreme therefore becomes something more than the will of either Creator or creature. (117:1.2, 117:1.5)

The section makes room for human love too. When a person who has received the Father’s love revealed by Michael seeks to show that love to others, such creature affection is a true reflection of the love of the Supreme. (117:1.8)

II. The Source of Evolutionary Growth — An Emptiness That Can Be Filled

To mortal man, existence is equivalent to growth. The author holds that this growth is a function of the Supreme and is peculiar to the age of the Supreme’s growth. That is why not every being living in this age takes part in such growth. (117:2.1, 117:2.2)

This contrast lets us see our place anew. Our incompleteness is an opportunity before it is a defect. Because the Supreme is not yet complete, there is room for creatures to grow and to add to his completion. (117:2.5, 117:2.8)

Emptiness does have its virtue, for it may become experientially filled. (117:2.8)

III. Significance of the Supreme to Universe Creatures

As Jesus is the living way from self-consciousness to God-consciousness, so the Supreme is the living way from finite consciousness to what lies beyond it. The Creator Son could be such a way because he actually traveled it, from the humanity of Joshua ben Joseph to the divinity of Michael; likewise the Supreme is a way because he actually embodies the evolution of every creature. (117:3.3, 117:3.4)

The author says that human growth and the Supreme’s growth are alike. Both depend on help from above, yet both take part willingly in their own completion. Deity is existential in the Trinity, experiential in the Supreme, and creature-realized in mortals through Adjuster fusion. (117:3.7, 117:3.9, 117:3.10)

The Supreme did not create man, but man’s life was derived from his potentiality. He does not initiate original causation, but he appears to be the catalyzer of all universe growth. (117:3.12, 117:3.13)

From the finite standpoint, we actually live, move, and have our being within the immanence of the Supreme. (117:3.12)

IV. The Finite God — Divinity Placed in Human Hands

Because God the Supreme is the finite Deity, he must deal with the problems of the finite himself. The struggles of creatures for perfection reflect the Supreme’s own struggle for self-realization. When a person chooses survival, the Supreme gains a wider experiential sovereignty; when someone rejects it, that part of the Supreme suffers inescapable delay. (117:4.1, 117:4.2)

God is so trusting, so loving, that he gives a portion of his divine nature into the hands of even human beings for safekeeping and self-realization. (117:4.3)

No choice can destroy the Adjuster, but the evolving self can be lost. All that was worthwhile in such a life persists, yet that personality never appears again, returning to the Supreme as a drop of water returns to the sea. However small the individual may seem beside the whole, each personality carries an irreplaceable meaning-value. (117:4.3, 117:4.4, 117:4.5)

From this comes cosmic morality. It transcends any temporal sense of relative right and wrong and rests on the creature’s recognition of an experiential obligation to experiential Deity. In a certain sense Supreme Deity depends on the choices of the mortal will, and so the author asks us again and again not to pass into the realm of the unrealized. (117:4.8, 117:4.9, 117:4.13)

The more closely man approaches God through love, the greater the reality—actuality—of that man. (117:4.14)

V. The Oversoul of Creation — What We Take and What We Leave

A creature who submits to the Creator’s will does not lose his personality; he grows ever larger by sharing in this Deity adventure. At the same time, the repercussions of all human experience become part of the Supreme’s divine experience. When man acts, the Supreme reacts, and this exchange is progress itself. (117:5.2, 117:5.5, 117:5.6)

“The act is ours, the consequences God’s.” (117:5.5)

In the end, what we take with us is not the circuits but character. Decisions illuminated by sincere worship, glorified by intelligent love, and consummated in brotherly service accumulate into a cosmically moral character. The small decisions of each day become eternal capital. (117:5.13)

VI. The Quest for the Supreme — Slowly, Like a River

As God is our divine Father, so the Supreme is our divine Mother, in whom we are nurtured throughout our universe lives. The evolving soul is the child of the Adjuster’s action and of the Universal Mother’s reaction. Yet until the soul awakens, this assurance of kinship must be realized by faith. (117:6.2, 117:6.5, 117:6.8)

This section traces the circuit of love. Love comes from the Father, passes through sons to brothers, and so reaches the Supreme. It cannot be locked in the heart; it becomes real only when given to others. (117:6.10)

Love is dynamic. It can never be captured (117:6.10)

In the present age the only approach to the Supreme is through experience, and the text names three avenues. Paradise Citizens come to understand him by descending through Havona and exploring the work of the Supreme Creators; time-space ascenders draw near him as they traverse Havona; and Havona natives comprehend him through contact with pilgrims going both ways. Every avenue opens not through solitary thought but through living and meeting. (117:6.11, 117:6.12, 117:6.14)

Man can discover the Father in his heart, but he will have to search for the Supreme in the hearts of all other men (117:6.23)

So the Supreme is found not suddenly like an earthquake but quietly and patiently, as a river wears away the soil. On that road a God-knowing person is never lonely, for the Father walks beside him and the very way he travels is the presence of the Supreme. (117:6.25, 117:6.27)

But no God-knowing mortal can ever be lonely in his journey through the cosmos (117:6.27)

VII. The Future of the Supreme — A New Challenge after Completion

When all finite potentials are realized, the Supreme will emerge as an almighty Deity presence. The author believes he will then be as personalized as the Eternal Son, as powerized as the Isle of Paradise, and as unified as the Conjoint Actor. Finaliters, in taking their oath to the Paradise Trinity, already pledge fidelity to God the Supreme. (117:7.1, 117:7.7)

The author does not hide the problems inherent in this picture. The Supreme can hardly function fully before his evolution is complete, yet his Unqualified Supervisors already exercise a qualified sovereignty in universes settled in light and life. Where the finite touches the superfinite, the future may cast its image ahead—and the Thought Adjuster within us is a true prediction of future attainment in eternity. (117:7.3, 117:7.6)

In that day the boundaries between the superuniverses will gradually disappear and the grand universe will function as a perfected whole. But rest will not last long: the enigma of the emerging God the Ultimate will challenge the perfected citizens anew. The author speaks of all this with care, as belief and conjecture rather than settled fact. (117:7.15, 117:7.16, 117:7.17)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 117 may look like vast cosmology, but at its center stands one person’s choice. Each time we do God’s will, pass love on to others, and choose survival, our share is added to the growth of the finite God. No one else can make that contribution for us. (117:4.10)
