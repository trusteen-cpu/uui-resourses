Introduction — The Price Paid for Sovereignty

Paper 120 opens Part IV, “The Life and Teachings of Jesus.” Its author is the Melchizedek director of the revelatory commission appointed by Gabriel, and he tells of events just before Michael came to Urantia. He begins by saying that for a Creator Son to live the lives of his creatures is part of the price he pays for sovereignty. (120:0.1)

To live such identical lives as he imposes upon the intelligent beings of his own creation, thus to bestow himself in the likeness of his various orders of created beings, is a part of the price which every Creator Son must pay for the full and supreme sovereignty of his self-made universe of things and beings. (120:0.1)

At any time Michael could have ruled Nebadon in his own right as a Creator Son. But rather than rule by isolated right, he wished to become a ruler who embodies the wisdom of the Supreme Being through actual experience of co-operative subordination to the Trinity. So the seven bestowals had a double purpose: to complete his understanding of creatures, and to represent the maximum authority of the Trinity. (120:0.3, 120:0.4)

In this bestowal Michael chose to subject himself to the personal will of the Father. By that decision he would experience not only the nature of mortal man but also the will of the Father, and he could go with the assurance that Immanuel would keep the universe with the Father’s authority and that the Ancients of Days had decreed its safety. (120:0.7, 120:0.8)

Having chosen the time and the planet, Michael held the usual conference with Gabriel and then presented himself before Immanuel, his elder brother and counselor. He turned over to Immanuel every administrative power not already given to Gabriel, and Immanuel, accepting the custody of the universe, began to impart the counsel that would guide Michael as he grew up a mortal. Most of this paper consists of permitted excerpts from that charge. (120:0.6, 120:0.9)

I. The Seventh Bestowal Commission — Depend Wholly on the Father

Immanuel’s charge opens with trust in his brother. In the six earlier bestowals Michael had appeared fully grown, but now he would come to a disordered planet as a helpless babe. Immanuel calls this a new and untried experience. (120:1.1)

Immanuel points out the place of this bestowal. In the previous ones Michael had submitted to every phase of the will of the Supreme except the personal will of the Paradise Father. Now at last he had chosen to be wholly subject to the Father’s will, and meanwhile Immanuel would hold the universe as the Father’s personal representative. (120:1.2)

You are about to pay the full price of bestowal and to experience the complete enlightenment of the incarnation of a Creator in the likeness of a creature. (120:1.1)

Michael had laid aside all extraplanetary support and every special help his creatures might give. As the creatures of Nebadon depend on him, so now he must depend on the Father. In this way he would come to know in his own experience the meaning of the faith-trust he had always asked of his creatures. (120:1.3)

so now must you become wholly and unreservedly dependent upon your Paradise Father for safe conduct throughout the unrevealed vicissitudes of your ensuing mortal career (120:1.3)

Immanuel tells Michael that he need be concerned with only one thing: unbroken communion with the Father. Immanuel would see to the security of the universe, and by mandate of the Ancients of Days no rebellion could occur during the bestowal. The delegated authority could be reclaimed whenever Michael wished, but if he did so, Immanuel adds, it would be wholly for personal reasons. (120:1.4, 120:1.5, 120:1.6)

Throughout your Urantia bestowal you need be concerned with but one thing, the unbroken communion between you and your Paradise Father (120:1.4)

II. The Bestowal Limitations — Nine Directions

The second section contains the guidance Michael was to follow as he grew aware of his divine identity. What stands out is the request to accomplish in weakness what he could have done by power. Since Michael had refused to crush the rebels by his creator prerogatives, he was now to wrest dominion from them in the likeness of the lowest of his creatures. (120:2.2)

The second and third directions show that this life was not a matter for Urantia alone. If, as the Son of Man, he technically ended the Lucifer rebellion and received the title “Planetary Prince of Urantia,” the whole universe would recognize the justice of doing in mortal flesh what mercy had kept him from doing by power. And after that, Immanuel says, similar challenges to his authority could never recur. (120:2.2, 120:2.3)

The fifth direction has a clear order. First liberate man’s spiritual nature; next illuminate the intellect, heal the soul, and free the mind from fear; and then, with wisdom, minister to physical well-being. (120:2.5)

Live the ideal religious life for the inspiration and edification of all your universe. (120:2.5)

The seventh direction says that this life was meant not as an example for the people of one age to copy, but as an inspiration to every generation on every world of Nebadon. The eighth sums up its heart as a discovery in two directions. (120:2.7, 120:2.8)

The achievement of God seeking man and finding him and the phenomenon of man seeking God and finding him (120:2.8)

The sixth and ninth directions shed light on the inner side of this life. The will of the finite creature and the will of the infinite Creator were to become as one in a single life, and while Michael would in fact live as an ordinary man, he would remain potentially a Creator Son. That is why, after his Adjuster came, he was to refrain from any superhuman will-to-power, and no superhuman repercussions were to attend his life apart from the will of the Father. (120:2.6, 120:2.9)

III. Further Counsel and Advice — What Not to Leave Behind

The third section offers advice, worked out with Gabriel, on the lesser phases of Michael’s life. This counsel is a key that explains in advance why Jesus did some things and not others. (120:3.1)

The advice to leave no writings or images was given to prevent idolatry and crystallized religious systems. Immanuel also says that this life would not necessarily seem perfect to any one generation, yet would be supremely replete as judged on more perfected worlds. Having finished his charge, he took up the rule of Nebadon as acting sovereign and committed to Gabriel the safekeeping of the Son of Man about to be. (120:3.7, 120:3.9, 120:3.11)

The charge ends in blessing. Immanuel prays that the Father will guide and sustain Michael from the moment he surrenders consciousness, through his gradual recognition of his divine identity, until his ascension to the Father. When he had finished, Michael vanished in the presence of all. (120:3.10, 120:3.12)

IV. The Incarnation—Making Two One

Those who had accused Michael of selfishly seeking rulership were left silenced before the self-forgetful service of the Son of Man, lived all the while in subjection to the Father’s will. The charge of rebellion was answered not by argument but by a life. (120:4.1)

He was not God in association with man but, rather, God incarnate in man. (120:4.2)
Source 120:4.3
MisunderstandingWhat the text says

He gradually became GodHe did not progressively become God
He became man at some momentGod did not become man at some vital moment; he was always God and man
What grewThe human mind’s self-conscious realization of being God and man

The author compares this mystery to the Trinity. Just as the Paradise Trinity of three beings is in reality one Deity, so God and man in Jesus were, and are, one. What grew in this relationship was not Jesus’ nature but the human mind’s awareness of it. Whoever reads the life of Jesus, then, is invited not to search for the moment divinity began, but to follow how one human being came to recognize who he was. (120:4.2, 120:4.3)

Never lose sight of the fact that the supreme spiritual purpose of the Michael bestowal was to enhance the revelation of God. (120:4.4)

To citizens of the local universe, the most intriguing miracle is the incarnation of a Paradise Son. Yet even in this extraordinary event, the Father chose the ordinary way, as he always does. As we read the life of Jesus, we are guided to see God first in natural, everyday life rather than in dazzling wonders. (120:4.5, 120:4.6)

God the Father chose to manifest himself as he always does—in the usual way—in the normal, natural, and dependable way of divine acting. (120:4.6)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 120 is a guide for reading the life of Jesus. Why he came as a baby, why he stayed clear of politics and economics, why he left neither writings nor images, why he chose weakness over power—all of this is already contained in this charge. And at the center of every choice stands unbroken communion with the Father. (120:1.4, 120:3.7)
