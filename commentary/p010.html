Introduction — The One Inevitability in the Universe

Having considered the Son and the Spirit in turn from Paper 6 through Paper 9, The Urantia Book now takes up the Paradise Trinity, which unites the three. Written by a Universal Censor, the paper begins with the claim that the Trinity frees the Father from personality absolutism. Because of the Son and the Spirit and their children, the Father is liberated from the limitations otherwise inherent in primacy, perfection, changelessness, and infinity. (10:0.1)

The Trinity is Deity unity. It rests eternally on the divine oneness of three original, co-ordinate, and coexistent persons: God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit. Looking back into the endless past, the author confesses that he can find only one inescapable inevitability in universe affairs, and that is the Trinity. (10:0.2, 10:0.3)

The present master universe, viewed in retrospect or in prospect, is unthinkable without the Trinity. (10:0.3)

Given the Trinity, the affairs of the universe may be done in many different ways. Without it, however, there is no way to conceive how the Infinite could appear as three co-ordinate persons without losing the absolute oneness of Deity. The strength of the Trinity concept is that it holds together the absoluteness of unity and the freedom of three persons. (10:0.3)

I. Self-Distribution of the First Source and Center

Back in eternity the Father inaugurated a policy of profound self-distribution. He has divested himself of every part of himself that could be bestowed on any other Creator or creature, and he has delegated to his Sons every power and all authority that could be delegated. In the affairs of a local universe he has made each Creator Son just as perfect and authoritative as the Eternal Son is in the central universe. (10:1.1, 10:1.2)

Divine personality is not self-centered (10:1.3)

Sharing oneself is the mark of divine selfhood. Creatures crave association with other persons; Creators are moved to share divinity with their children. The Father bestowed the “personality of infinity” upon the Son, and the two of them bestowed the “conjoint personality” of their union upon the Infinite Spirit. So the Father no longer exists alone as the unqualified personality, and if we want to know him, we must come to him through the revelation of the Son and the activity of the Spirit. (10:1.3, 10:1.4, 10:1.5)

The section closes with practical guidance. Because the Paradise Sons visit the evolutionary worlds and sometimes even live there in the likeness of mortal flesh, the creatures of the planets should look to these bestowals for trustworthy information about the Father, the Son, and the Spirit. For us, that bestowal is the life of Jesus. (10:1.6)

II. Deity Personalization — Father, Son, and Spirit

When the Father divests himself of the unqualified spirit personality that is the Son, by that very act he becomes the Father of the Son and gains the capacity to become the Father of all future will creatures. Then, from the infinite power of the Father-Son union, the Infinite Spirit comes forth and completes the existential personalization of Deity. The Son is indispensable to the fatherhood of God, and the Spirit is indispensable to the fraternity of the Second and Third Persons. (10:2.1, 10:2.2, 10:2.3)

Here the author reveals that he himself is of origin in the Paradise Trinity. He testifies that the three persons act individually, collectively, and in various groupings, so that in the end they function in seven different capacities. This is why the realities of the universe appear in seven variations of values, meanings, and personality. (10:2.8)

III. The Three Persons of Deity — Seven Ways the Father Works in the Universe

There is only one Deity, yet there are three positive personalizations of Deity. The plural wording, “Let us make mortal man in our own image,” bears witness to the recognition of the three Sources and Centers. The Son and the Spirit stand in equal relation to the Father, yet in time they disclose relationships of very diverse kinds. (10:3.1, 10:3.2)

God speaks through the Son and, with the Son, acts through the Infinite Spirit (10:3.3)

The Father was never alone without a Son. Fatherhood is not real without sonship, and the Son and the Spirit are coeternal with the Father. The Father has divested himself of all direct manifestations of absoluteness except absolute fatherhood and absolute volition. As a result, outside of Havona he functions in the following seven ways. (10:3.5, 10:3.6, 10:3.9)

All these relinquishments and delegations are wholly voluntary and self-imposed, not forced upon him. In an all-powerful Father who purposely limits his own authority, we see the way of love. The Son functions as a person only in the domain of the spiritual universe, while the Spirit is amazingly versatile in the spheres of mind, matter, and spirit. (10:3.17, 10:3.18, 10:3.19)

IV. The Trinity Union of Deity

Of all absolute associations, the Paradise Trinity is unique as an exclusive association of personal Deity. God functions as absolute Deity only in the Trinity and in relation to universe totality. The Trinity makes it possible to express at once the diversity of the three persons and the unity of undivided Deity. (10:4.1, 10:4.2)

The author offers a crude illustration: a father, son, and grandson could form a corporate entity that is not a person yet remains subject to their personal wills. The three persons can also collaborate as individuals in any way they choose, but that collaboration is not the Trinity. (10:4.3, 10:4.4)

It would be futile to try to explain fully the mystery of three as one and one acting for two. Living on the finite level, we must be content with a finite concept of the Trinity and view it according to our own enlightenment and the reactions of mind and soul. As we ascend, we will be astonished again and again as the supremacy and ultimacy of the Trinity are successively revealed. (10:4.5, 10:4.6, 10:4.7)

V. Functions of the Trinity — A Union Greater Than the Sum

The personal Deities have attributes, but the Trinity is better understood in terms of functions: justice administration, totality attitudes, co-ordinate action, and cosmic overcontrol. And this union is not an arithmetical sum of the attributes of the three; it brings forth new meanings, values, and powers. (10:5.1, 10:5.2)

The group potential is always far in excess of the simple sum of the attributes of the component individuals. (10:5.2)

At the end of the section the author frankly admits that he does not command language that could convey the full truth and eternal significance of the Trinity. Passages like this, where a revelator acknowledges his own limits, teach the reader to read with humility. (10:5.8)

VI. The Stationary Sons of the Trinity — Justice Is a Plural Function

The paper traces the course of law and justice in four steps. Law originates in the Father; the administration of spiritual law belongs to the Son; the revelation and interpretation of law is the Spirit’s function; and the application of law, which is justice, is the province of the Trinity. (10:6.1)

Justice is never a personal attitude; it is always a plural function. (10:6.2)

Judgment is carried out by the Stationary Sons of the Trinity on the basis of evidence submitted by the personalities of the Infinite Spirit. These Sons form ten orders, from the Trinitized Secrets of Supremacy through the Ancients of Days and the Divine Counselors to the Universal Censors. The author of this paper belongs to the tenth order. (10:6.4, 10:6.16)

Justice is the collective thought of righteousness; mercy is its personal expression. (10:6.18)

When fully understood, the righteous justice of the Trinity and the merciful love of the Father are coincident. But because we do not yet understand divine justice so deeply, in time we see love and law ministering side by side. (10:6.18)

VII–VIII. The Overcontrol of Supremacy; The Trinity Beyond the Finite

The three persons are equal, and they are one. Seen from the finite level, the Trinity, like the Supreme Being, is concerned only with the total: the total planet, the total universe. For this reason its overcontrol is not wholly predictable, an unpredictability that seems linked to the incompleteness of the Supreme. (10:7.1, 10:7.2, 10:7.4)

The section speaks honestly about painful events such as disasters, illnesses, and accidents. The authors admit they are not sure whether these are related to the function of the Supreme. But they add that, as time passes, they observe all such difficult situations always working out for the welfare and progress of the universes. (10:7.5)

In the progress of eternity the acts of the Trinity will be revealed as altogether meaningful and considerate, but they do not always so appear to the creatures of time. (10:7.6)

The final section looks at the Trinity beyond the finite. Collectively the three persons fill all Deity potential, and the finaliters, having found God as Father, must sometime begin the quest for the superfinite Father. The author confesses that he loves and worships God the Father while he respects and honors the Deity Absolute, and he closes the paper with one short sentence. (10:8.4, 10:8.5, 10:8.7)

Only infinity can disclose the Father-Infinite. (10:8.9)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 10 is one of the most demanding papers theologically, yet its heart is surprisingly simple. God is one who shares himself, and through the relationships that sharing creates, he works on a larger scale. Union is greater than the sum; justice is a judgment made together, not alone; and mercy is the personal face of that justice. Even what we cannot understand now will be revealed as meaningful in eternity.
