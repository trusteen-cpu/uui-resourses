Introduction — Speaking of Eternity in the Language of Time

After five papers on the Father, Paper 6 turns to the Second Person of Deity, the Eternal Son. The Eternal Son is the perfect and final expression of the Father’s “first” personal and absolute concept. Therefore, whenever the Father expresses himself personally and absolutely, he does so through the Eternal Son. The Son is the living and divine Word who has always been and always will be. (6:0.1)

From the outset the author points to the limits of language. Expressions like “first thought” or “sprang from the Father” are compromises made for time-bound minds. In the sequential sense the Father could never have had a first thought, and the Son never had a beginning. The mystery of a Son who is derived from the Father and yet equally eternal with him is something no time creature can fully comprehend. (6:0.2, 6:0.4)

who ever has been, now is, and ever will be, the living and divine Word (6:0.1)

I. Identity of the Eternal Son — The Second Source and Center

The Eternal Son is the original and only-begotten Son of God, God the Son, and the associate creator of all things. As the Father is the First Source and Center, so the Son is the Second Source and Center. The Father never personally creates except in conjunction with the Son. The paper says that had the New Testament writer been referring to the Eternal Son, he would have spoken the truth in writing, “In the beginning was the Word.” (6:1.1, 6:1.3)

This section also points out a confusion peculiar to Urantia. On our world the Eternal Son has been confused with the Creator Son who bestowed himself here, Michael of Nebadon. Any Paradise Son may fittingly be called a Son of God, but the designation “the Eternal Son” is reserved for the Original Son, who with the Father created the central universe. (6:1.5, 6:1.6)

II. Nature of the Eternal Son — He Who Has Seen the Son Has Seen the Father

The Eternal Son is just as changeless and infinitely dependable as the Father, and just as spiritual. To us, however, he would appear more personal, since he is one step nearer in approachability. The Son is God the Father personally manifest to the universe, and when we worship the Father, we actually worship God the Son and God the Spirit at the same time. (6:2.1, 6:2.2, 6:2.3)

And thus it was and is and forever will be true of the Eternal Son and of all the co-ordinate Creator Sons: “He who has seen the Son has seen the Father.” (6:2.2)

The Son possesses all the Father’s righteousness and holiness, and he shares with the Father the responsibility of helping imperfect creatures attain perfection. There is hardly any difference between Father and Son. They are equal, except that the Son seems to devote himself more to the realization of spiritual beauty; in goodness no difference can be discerned. Only their standpoint is slightly different. (6:2.4, 6:2.7, 6:2.8)

The Father loves his universe children as a father; the Eternal Son looks upon all creatures both as father and as brother. (6:2.8)

III. Ministry of the Father’s Love — Love and Mercy

The Son shares the justice and righteousness of the Trinity, but he overshadows these traits by the infinite personalization of the Father’s love and mercy. The Son cannot love more than the Father. Yet he can show mercy in one additional way: being both a creator and the Father’s Son, he shares in the sonship experience of all the Father’s other sons. (6:3.1)

God is love, the Son is mercy. (6:3.5)

This section again corrects a misconception that Papers 2 and 4 warned against. To imagine the Eternal Son pleading with the Father to show mercy is a crude and grotesque concept of God. All the merciful ministrations of the Sons are a direct revelation of the Father’s heart, and the Father’s love is the very source of the Son’s mercy. (6:3.4)

The Father’s love is the real and eternal source of the Son’s mercy. (6:3.4)

IV. Attributes of the Eternal Son — The Pull of Spirit

The Son motivates the spirit level of cosmic reality. Through his absolute grasp of spirit gravity he controls all spiritual reality, and all pure spirit and spiritual values respond to his drawing power. But the Son is omnipotent only in the spiritual realm, for the Deities are not given to useless duplication of universe ministry. (6:4.1, 6:4.2)

The spirit of the Son is everywhere present and binds all creation together spiritually. Yet the Son’s spirit and the Father’s fragment relate to us in different ways. Understanding this difference shows how our spiritual growth takes place. (6:4.3, 6:4.6)

In wisdom and knowledge, too, the Son is the full equal of the Father. He is never surprised by any event and comprehends the end from the beginning. And yet, in his contacts with ascending beings of the lower realms, he is just as kind and considerate, just as patient and long-suffering, as the Paradise Sons who bestow themselves on the evolutionary worlds. Infinite power and tender patience live together in one personality. (6:4.7, 6:4.9)

V. Limitations of the Eternal Son — Personal and Nothing But Personal

Even the infinite Son has self-defined limitations. These do not lessen his spiritual omniscience, omnipresence, or omnipotence, but they show clearly the role each person plays within the Trinity. (6:5.1)

So the Son cannot personally take part in the bestowal of the Thought Adjusters. Yet the paper records a beautiful scene. In the eternal past, when the Father, planning the bestowal of the Adjusters, proposed, “Let us make mortal man in our own image,” the Son approved the plan and pledged endless co-operation. So now the Father’s fragment dwells within us, the Son’s spirit presence envelops us, and the two work forever as one for our spiritual advancement. (6:5.7)

VI–VII. The Spirit Mind; Personality of the Eternal Son

The Eternal Son is spirit and has mind, but not a mind that mortal mind can comprehend. Mind directly allied with spirit cannot be compared with mind allied with matter. Spirit is ever conscious and minded, and Deity is never mindless. The paper says that the mind of the Father and the Son is perhaps best illustrated in the premind of a Thought Adjuster: Adjusters know as they are known. (6:6.1, 6:6.2, 6:6.3)

Much about the Son is beyond our knowing for now. We will see these mysteries clearly only after we complete the morontia ascension, attain spirit status, and begin to be endowed with the “mind of the spirit.” Not knowing now is not a failure; it is a stage of the journey. (6:6.4)

The seventh section presents the Son as absolute personality. Through the technique of trinitization the Father escaped the fetters of unqualified personality, and ever since he has bestowed himself in endless profusion upon his universe. The Son’s personality is the pattern for the Father’s bestowal of personality upon the Conjoint Actor and upon countless creatures. The Son is the sum and substance of the First Source and Center, divested of all that is nonpersonal and nonspiritual. (6:7.1, 6:7.2, 6:7.3)

VIII. Realization of the Eternal Son — Thought and Word

In identity and nature the Son is the full equal of the Father, his perfect complement and eternal counterpart. Here the paper uses a bold expression: in the same sense that God is the Universal Father, the Son is the Universal Mother, and all of us, high and low, make up their universal family. (6:8.1)

For us it is not easy to tell the workings of the Father and the Son apart, nor is the attempt always profitable. The paper offers one simple key. And in each local universe the Creator Son stands for both Father and Son at once. (6:8.2, 6:8.3)

merely recall that God is the initiating thought and the Son is the expressionful word (6:8.3)

We grasp the Father and the Infinite Spirit more easily, because the Father bestowed our personality and the Infinite Spirit is the source of our mind. We approach the Son through the bestowal service of the Paradise Sons and the loving ministry of the Infinite Spirit. With their help the God-conscious mortal will certainly attain Paradise and stand before the Son. The author testifies that he himself has stood in that presence times almost without number, and that the Son is truly a person. (6:8.4, 6:8.5, 6:8.8)

doubt not, he is a person (6:8.8)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 6 portrays the Eternal Son as the Word that expresses the Father’s thought, the mercy that puts the Father’s love into action, and the drawing power that pulls all spirit toward him. The Son does not dwell within us, but he surrounds us and works as one with the Adjuster within us to lead us on. For now, the closest way we can know the Son is through the life of the Creator Son who came to this world—Jesus.

The papers that follow take up the Eternal Son’s relation to the universe and then the Infinite Spirit, the Third Source and Center. If we read on holding the key of the Father’s thought and the Son’s word, the relationships within the Trinity will come into sharper focus.
