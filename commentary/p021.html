Introduction — The Son Who Became Our Universe Father

The story of the order of Michael, held back in Paper 20, unfolds in Paper 21. The Creator Sons are the makers and rulers of the local universes of time and space, and they are of dual origin, embodying the characteristics of God the Father and God the Son. Yet each differs from every other; each is unique in nature as well as in personality, the “only-begotten Son” of his own origin. The one we know as Jesus is one of this order, Michael of Nebadon. (21:0.1)

From its opening the paper stresses how warm the relationship is between the Creator Sons and the Father. Throughout the long work of building and perfecting a local universe, these Sons enjoy the Father’s sustaining approval. And that love is linked to the love found in our own homes. (21:0.2)

No doubt the profound affection of the Deity parents for their divine progeny is the wellspring of that beautiful and well-nigh divine love which even mortal parents bear their children. (21:0.2)

The name Michael comes from the first Son of this order. The original Michael never incarnated as a material being, but seven times he passed through the experience of spiritual creature ascent on the seven circuits of Havona. So the order of Michael knows the grand universe from one end to the other. (21:0.3, 21:0.4)

there is no essential experience of any of the children of time and space in which the Michaels have not personally participated (21:0.4)

I. Origin and Nature of Creator Sons — The Only-Begotten of Two Infinite Minds

When the fullness of absolute spiritual ideation in the Eternal Son encounters the fullness of absolute personality concept in the Universal Father, a new Creator Son flashes into full-fledged being. He is the only Son that particular union could ever produce, the complete expression of every possibility of the divine potentials that brought him forth. There never can be another like him. (21:1.1, 21:1.2)

So some Michaels appear more like the Father, some more like the Son, and some equally like both. The trend of administration in Nebadon suggests that our Creator Son more resembles the Eternal Mother Son. The revelator adds that this is no criticism, simply a record of fact. The Creator Sons appear to number more than seven hundred thousand, and their number keeps growing. (21:1.3, 21:1.4)

This uniqueness spreads through the whole universe. Because each Michael is the architect of the life plans of his realm, no two local universes have identical native beings. Half of a native creature’s inherent attributes come from the uniform Creative Spirits, and the other half from the diversified Creator Sons. Diversity is not a defect; it is God’s way of creating. (21:2.10)

II. The Creators of Local Universes — Freedom and Its Limits

A Creator Son chooses the space site of his universe himself, but first he must spend a long time observing the work of his older brothers in the same superuniverse, and before that he completes his Paradise observation and Havona training. It is striking that even a Creator learns before he creates. When he leaves Paradise, he is accompanied by a Creative Daughter of the Infinite Spirit, the future Mother Spirit of his universe. (21:2.1, 21:2.2)

His creator prerogatives are wide, but not unlimited. Certain things remain with the Paradise Sources and Centers. (21:2.3)

When Michael is absent, the Bright and Morning Star directs the government, and the Mother Spirit remains at headquarters, extending her care to the uttermost parts of the universe. Even without the Creator Son the material universe runs on, for no material organization is independent of the absolute-gravity grasp of Paradise and the overcontrol of the Unqualified Absolute. (21:2.11, 21:2.12)

III. Local Universe Sovereignty — Authority Earned, Not Seized

Sovereignty is the heart of this paper. A Creator Son receives his universe by the consent of the Trinity and the confirmation of the Master Spirit, but this is only a kind of “cosmic leasehold.” He could assert full sovereignty from the start, but he wisely chooses not to. He elects to earn it through the experience of bestowal, and until then he rules as vicegerent of the Universal Father. (21:3.1, 21:3.2, 21:3.3)

but they all could, they are truly freewill Sons. (21:3.3)

So Michael takes an oath to the Trinity not to assume supreme sovereignty until his seven bestowals are complete. If he could not do otherwise, the oath would have no meaning. The sight of one who holds power deferring it of his own accord, and qualifying himself through service, shows what true authority is. (21:3.12)

In a universe without rebellion the limits of prebestowal sovereignty never show; in a universe with rebellion they do. If his authority is challenged, the Creator Son is eternally pledged to uphold and, if necessary, retrieve his creation. He can be troubled only by creatures of his own making or by higher beings of his own choosing. (21:3.13, 21:3.14)

Virtue is volitional with personality; righteousness is not automatic in freewill creatures. (21:3.14)

The technique of earning supreme sovereignty is to experience seven creature levels by incarnation, each time consecrating himself to one phase of the sevenfold will of Deity personified in the Seven Master Spirits, and to unify the two. In this way the Creator Son not only proves his fitness to rule but reveals the nature of Deity. The creature’s grasp of the Father’s primacy is bound up with the Creator’s adventure in condescending to take the form of his creatures. (21:3.16, 21:3.24)

IV. The Michael Bestowals — What “It Is Finished” Means

Avonal bestowals are always in the likeness of mortal flesh, but the seven bestowals of a Michael span seven creature levels. The final one is always a mortal bestowal, usually as a member of the race carrying the largest legacy of Adamic stock. Only once in his sevenfold career is a Michael born of woman, and only once does he live and die as the lowest order of will creature. (21:4.2, 21:4.3)

After each bestowal he goes to the “right hand of the Father” to gain acceptance and prepare for the next. The Michael who came to Urantia had already completed six phases. So when he gave up his incarnated life and said, “It is finished,” it was literally finished. His death completed his bestowal career, and from then on he rules not as a vicegerent but in his own right as “King of Kings and Lord of Lords.” (21:4.4, 21:4.5)

A Creator Son who has completed his bestowals is reckoned a separate order, a sevenfold Master Son. He is still a Creator, but he has added the experience of a creature to his nature. Here the revelator speaks to us directly: if even the Gods must pass through experience before they are fully qualified to rule, we have no reason to lament our own humble beginnings. (21:4.6)

Why should man bemoan his lowly origin and enforced evolutionary career (21:4.6)

V. Relation of Master Sons to the Universe

The power of a Master Michael is unlimited because it comes from experienced association with the Trinity, and unquestioned because it comes from actual experience as one of the creatures subject to his authority. His sovereignty is supreme for three reasons. (21:5.1)

Upon attaining supreme sovereignty, Michael gives up the power to create entirely new types of beings in the present age. Instead he devotes himself personally to fostering and administering what has already been made. From this point the long uncertainty and confusion begin to end. It is worth noting that justice prevails only after endless mercy and patience have been exhausted. (21:5.6, 21:5.7)

That which mercy cannot rehabilitate justice will eventually annihilate. (21:5.7)

A Master Son makes and carries out plans of his own choosing for the worlds of his sojourn, and above all for the planet where he incarnated in mortal flesh. He keeps in touch with those worlds through the Spirit of Truth, which he “pours out upon all flesh.” The Spirit of Truth at work in each of us is the spiritual presence of this sovereign Michael. (21:5.9, 21:5.10)

They possess a sympathetic reach which extends from the Universal Father on high to the lowly races of planetary life in the realms of time. (21:5.10)

VI. Destiny of the Master Michaels — The Way, the Truth, and the Life

No one can speak with final authority about the destiny of the Master Sons. The revelators can only speculate that each Michael is the absolute of the dual deity concepts of his origin, holding superfinite capacities not yet revealed. The restriction on creating new types of beings seems connected with the liberation of this potential. (21:6.1, 21:6.2)

In the far future, in the universes of outer space, the liaison of a sevenfold Master Son and a seventh-stage Creative Spirit may bring forth new meanings and values. The seventh career of the Mother Spirit we met in Paper 17 here meets the future of Michael. Just as the Supreme is actualizing through experiential service, the Creator Sons are realizing the divine potentials bound up in their natures. (21:6.3, 21:6.4)

I am the way, the truth, and the life. (21:6.4)

The revelators believe that in eternity the Michaels are literally destined to be “the way, the truth, and the life,” ever blazing the path for all universe personalities toward eternal deity finality. It is an ending that shows these words of Jesus to be more than the confession of one planet; they are a universal promise. (21:6.4)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 21 tells us who the Creator of our universe is. He had all power from the beginning, yet he did not seize it; seven times he became one of his creatures and earned his sovereignty through understanding and sympathy. His final bestowal was Jesus on Urantia. After reading this paper, the life of Jesus appears anew as the event that completed the history of an entire local universe.

With Paper 21 the main account of the Paradise Sons is complete, and the papers that follow turn to the Trinitized Sons of God (Paper 22) and the family of the Infinite Spirit. If we keep in mind the Michael we have met here, we will sense that the whole story of the local universe that follows unfolds under the care of a fatherly Son.
