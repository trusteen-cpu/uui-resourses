Introduction — The Oneness Beneath Diversity

Paper 56 is the last paper of Part II, which deals with the local universe. Where the earlier papers introduced the Sons and the spirits, the angels and the planetary ages one by one, this paper declares the principle that binds them all together. The physical, intellectual, and spiritual domains are divinely correlated, and that is why a finite creature can ascend to Paradise. (56:0.1)

GOD is unity. Deity is universally co-ordinated. (56:0.1)

To the limited vision of mortals, the universe often seems disharmonious and poorly co-ordinated. But the more widely we observe, and the better we learn to detect the basic unity beneath creative diversity, the more clearly we perceive one divine purpose in every manifestation of energy. This paper is an exercise in cultivating that vision. (56:0.2)

I. Physical Co-ordination — All Forces from One Source

The material creation is not infinite, but it is perfectly co-ordinated. There are force, energy, and power, yet all are one in origin. The superuniverses seem dual and the central universe triune, but Paradise is single. The Unqualified Absolute upholds the physical universe, the Deity Absolute motivates its overcontrol, and the two are unified in the Universal Absolute. (56:1.1, 56:1.2)

The point of this section is that duality does not exist within the Father’s infinite nature. The division between physical and spiritual appears only when we look aside from his infinite level. And to bridge that divergence, mind is indispensable. (56:1.5, 56:1.6)

in him all things consist. (56:1.5)

Gravity unification is universal and unvarying, and pure energy and pure spirit are wholly preresponsive to gravity. Because these primal forces are personally controlled by the Father, all gravity finally centers in his personal presence. The hidden comfort of this section is that at the very bottom of the forces holding the universe together there stands not an impersonal law but a Person. (56:1.3, 56:1.4)

II–III. Intellectual Unity; Spiritual Unification

The Father’s primal thought is eternalized in a dual expression—the Isle of Paradise and the Eternal Son—and this duality makes the mind God, the Infinite Spirit, inevitable. Cosmic mind extends from the primitive ministry of the adjutant spirits to the magnificent mind of a universe’s chief executive, yet it is unified under the Seven Master Spirits. (56:2.2, 56:2.3)

The material evolutionary creature can conceive and comprehend the indwelling spirit only by the ministry of mind. (56:2.2)

The same is true of spirit. As mind gravity centers in the Infinite Spirit, spirit gravity centers in the Eternal Son. In time and space, spirit appears in three ways: the spirit personalities of the Eternal Son, the spirit personalities associated with the Infinite Spirit, and pure-spirit fragments such as the Thought Adjusters, the Father’s bestowal. These three spirit endowments become perfectly unified at last in the finaliters. (56:3.1, 56:3.2, 56:3.5)

This is also something that happens within us. The mortal mind is the creation of the children of the Son and the Spirit, and when it fuses with the Adjuster it shares in the threefold spirit endowment. When our thinking and our spiritual longing seem at odds, it has practical meaning to know that it is the ministry of mind that joins the two. (56:3.5)

IV. Personality Unification — No Child Has Two Fathers

The Universal Father is a divinely unified personality, and so all his ascending children will likewise be fully unified personalities before they reach Havona. Personality inherently reaches out to unify all its constituent realities, and because the personality of mortal man is a direct bestowal of the Father, it has the potential to unify body, mind, and spirit. (56:4.1, 56:4.2)

To personality God cannot be plural. (56:4.4)

Philosophically we may, and must, conceive of plural levels of Deity and even plural Trinities. But in the experience of worship, God is always one—our Paradise parent. However complex the cosmology we learn, the one who prays can return to the simple relationship of Father and child. (56:4.5)

V–VI. Deity Unity; Unification of Evolutionary Deity

The oneness of Paradise Deity is existential and absolute. There are three personalizations, yet in the Trinity they are one undivided Deity. In the evolving universes, things are different: the first three levels of God the Sevenfold—the Creator Sons and Creative Spirits, the Ancients of Days, and the Seven Master Spirits—are being co-ordinated in the Supreme Being. (56:5.1, 56:5.3)

On Paradise and in the central universe, Deity unity is a fact of existence. Throughout the evolving universes of time and space, Deity unity is an achievement. (56:5.4)

On any level, God cannot exceed the conceptual capacity of the beings on that level. So mortals come to know the God of love by recognizing truth, appreciating beauty, and worshiping goodness, and then progress through ascending Deity levels to comprehension of the Supreme. That even the finaliters have not yet found the Supreme Being shows how long this journey is. (56:6.3, 56:6.4)

VII–VIII. Universal Evolutionary Repercussions; The Supreme Unifier

Each time a world, system, constellation, or universe reaches the height of evolutionary progress, Deity function expands in that unit, with repercussions in every other realm. When a system is settled in light, the Son-Spirit liaison reaches its fullness of function; when a local universe is completed, the ministry of the Master Spirits becomes more direct. About what will happen in the outer universes, the author says again and again, “We do not know.” (56:7.1, 56:7.2, 56:7.6)

Finaliters are born in the local universes, nurtured in the superuniverses, and trained in the central universe, and then serve in turn in superuniverses other than their own, gathering the full sevenfold range of creature experience. Through the Adjusters they find the Father, but it is by these techniques of experience that they come to know the Supreme Being, and they are destined to serve and reveal him in the outer universes of the future. (56:8.2)

The experience of love, joy, and service in the universe is mutual. (56:8.3)

The Father does not need his children to return to him what he gives them. Instead, they can pass that love and service on to their fellows and to the evolving Supreme Being. In time we may begin to reveal the Supreme to our fellows, and we can enlarge that revelation as we ascend. Letting the grace we have received flow on to the person beside us is itself a way of taking part in the growth of God. (56:8.3, 56:8.4)

IX. Universal Absolute Unity — All Converges on the Father

This section takes up the great philosophic question of the master universe: does the Absolute come before the Trinity, or the Trinity before the Absolute? The author says that the eternity of the Trinity invalidates any conclusion that makes the Absolute the ancestor of all things. The Absolute is the Trinity’s impersonal reaction to basic space situations, and when we follow the question to its end, we return to the Universal I AM, the Paradise Father. (56:9.2, 56:9.4, 56:9.5)

The most beautiful promise is that at the end of every quest we meet the same One. Whether we attain God the Sevenfold, comprehend God the Supreme, or find God the Ultimate, at the close of each adventure we will rediscover, on a new level of experience, the Father of all personalities. (56:9.10)

God is all and in all, and no things or beings exist without him. (56:9.14)

X. Truth, Beauty, and Goodness — and Love

On worlds settled in light and life, the chief pursuit of mortals is the comprehensible elements of Deity: truth, beauty, and goodness. Truth is the basis of science and philosophy and the intellectual foundation of religion; beauty sponsors art, music, and the rhythms of life; goodness embraces ethics, morality, and religion. Beauty is the unification of contrasts, and its height is the drama of man finding God and God finding man. (56:10.2, 56:10.3, 56:10.10)

Standing opposite unity is sin. Every impulse of electron, thought, or spirit acts within the whole, but sin alone is isolated, resisting gravity on the levels of mind and spirit. That is why antisocial self-realization is potentially evil, and why cosmic socialization is the highest form of personality unification. (56:10.14)

Only sin is isolated and evil gravity resisting on the mental and spiritual levels. (56:10.14)

The paper also calls materialism and atheism the maximation of ugliness. If beauty is what binds variety into one, then a worldview that severs everything from its source stands at the opposite extreme. Goodness is similar: recognizing goodness is not enough; actually possessing goodness—greatness—is the measure of real divinity attainment. (56:10.4, 56:10.12)

When truth, beauty, and goodness are expressed in the life of a God-knowing person, they bear the fruits of divinity: intellectual peace, social progress, moral satisfaction, spiritual joy, and cosmic wisdom. And the sum of these three qualities is love. All of Part II ends, finally, with one very short sentence. (56:10.17, 56:10.20)

Love is the desire to do good to others. (56:10.21)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 56 gathers a vast cosmology into the simplest conclusion. The universe is one, God is one in power and personality, and all beings converge on the Paradise Father. In the evolving universes that unity is not a given fact but an achievement in progress, and each person’s unified personality and love for others become part of that achievement. With this paper Part II closes, and Part III turns to the history of Urantia. (56:5.4, 56:9.14, 56:10.23)
