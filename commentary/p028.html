Introduction — The Angels of the Superuniverses

If the supernaphim are the angels of the central universe and the seraphim the angels of the local universes, the seconaphim are the ministering spirits of the superuniverses. They are children of the Reflective Spirits, and in degree of divinity and potential they are much nearer the supernaphim than the seraphim. The paper introduces the three orders that serve as spirit ministers in the superuniverses, first treating briefly the two that are less directly concerned with mortal ascension. (28:0.1, 28:0.6)

I–II. The Tertiaphim and the Omniaphim — Helpers Behind the Scenes

When a Michael Son sets out on his universe adventure, the Infinite Spirit brings forth a corps of one thousand tertiaphim to accompany him. During the turbulent era of universe building they serve as the Creator Son’s only personal staff and gain great experience. Once the Bright and Morning Star of the local universe is personalized and the native angels appear, they retire from active duty to become liaison ministers between the Creator Son and the Ancients of Days. Those who began the work step aside and take up the task of connecting. (28:1.2, 28:1.3)

The omniaphim are the exclusive servants and messengers of the Seven Supreme Executives. In Orvonton they have a courtesy colony north of Uversa, yet they are neither registered on Uversa nor part of its administration; they take direction only from the Supreme Executive of Orvonton. (28:2.1, 28:2.2)

III. The Seconaphim — Created in Sevens

Seconaphim are produced in groups of seven by the seven Reflective Spirits at the headquarters of each superuniverse. Every seven always contains one primary, three secondary, and three tertiary seconaphim, an unchanging ratio, and each seven is at once divided among three levels of the supergovernment. (28:3.1)

Source 28:3.1

Tertiary seconaphimThreeMighty Messengers · Those High in Authority · Those without Name and Number

Their nature is reflectivity. They respond reflectively to everything that originates in the Third Source and Center and in the Paradise Creator Sons, but they do not directly reflect beings of sole origin in the First Source and Center. The author says that even without any other proof, the reflective performances of the seconaphim would suffice to demonstrate the real presence of the infinite mind of the Conjoint Actor throughout the universe. (28:3.2)

IV. The Primary Seconaphim — Living Mirrors of the Ancients of Days

The primary seconaphim are living mirrors serving the Ancients of Days. By turning to such a mirror, they can instantly and unerringly see and hear the response of a being tens of thousands of light-years away. The superuniverse rulers, standing midway between man and God, thus know both what is above and what is below, and each superuniverse stays in close touch with the central universe and Paradise. (28:4.1, 28:4.2)

The seven supergovernments, though individually segregated, are thus perfectly reflective of the authority above and are wholly sympathetic, as well as perfectly conversant, with the needs below. (28:4.2)

The third serial is especially remarkable. When the Ancients of Days wish to know the attitude of Michael of Nebadon, they need not call him over the lines of space; they perceive his voice through a registered seconaphim. The author admits that he does not fully understand how this is done, but says that it certainly works. (28:4.6, 28:4.7)

in all the history of Uversa the secoraphic voices have never erred in their presentations. (28:4.7)

The Ancients of Days deduce the Father’s will by equating the Spirit voice from above with the Michael voices from below. But to deduce the will of one God from knowledge of the other two, all three Ancients of Days must act together, and that is why the superuniverses are always presided over by three. The principle that no one alone can reach a complete judgment is written into the very structure of universe government. (28:4.10)

V. The Secondary Seconaphim — Wisdom, Counsel, and Discernment

The labels primary, secondary, and tertiary mark not a difference of status but simply the order of creation; the three groups share the same endowments. The seven types of secondary seconaphim are assigned to the Trinity-origin administrators of the superuniverse. (28:5.1, 28:5.2)

Among these, the Union of Souls deals with the petty frictions and minor misunderstandings that arise in commerce and trade, friendship and marriage. The observation that even trivial misunderstandings, if allowed to multiply, mar the smooth working of the universe applies just as well to our daily relationships. (28:5.13)

One of the most important lessons to be learned during your mortal career is teamwork. (28:5.14)

The Joy of Existence seeks to show that there is joy in free-will existence itself, whatever the outward conditions. The Discerner of Spirits reveals the true motive and nature of any information and even reflects the presence of Adjusters. Yet the paper is clear about a limit: the content of the minds of the Mystery Monitors cannot be deciphered. (28:5.16, 28:5.19, 28:5.22)

It should, however, be mentioned that the knowledge and plans of the Father fragments are not reflectible. (28:5.22)

VI. The Tertiary Seconaphim — Seven Mirrors of Character

The tertiary seconaphim are assigned collectively to the Trinitized Sons of Attainment, and this is the part of the paper richest in lessons for living. The names of the seven types alone sketch the path along which character grows. (28:6.1)

The Memory of Mercy is a living trial balance that sets each person’s spiritual account against the saving credit established by the Sons of God. If we are sincere of purpose and honest of heart, our drawing credits always far exceed our ability to exhaust them. Mercy, however, is not thrust upon those who despise it. The paper teaches that mercy is not a feeling that appears all at once but a quality that grows through stages. (28:6.5, 28:6.6, 28:6.7, 28:6.8)

you should even now understand that mercy is a quality of growth. (28:6.8)

Time is the one universal endowment of all will creatures, and it is fatally squandered only when it is buried in neglect and not used to make certain the survival of the soul. Failing to make the most of one’s time brings no fatal penalty; it merely delays the pilgrim. (28:6.9)

If survival is gained, all other losses can be retrieved. (28:6.9)
Trustworthiness is the true measure of self-mastery, character. (28:6.13)

Our superiors advance us with greater trusts just as fast as our character can bear them, for overloading a person too early only courts disaster. Once trustworthiness is found, the privilege of service follows, and it is purposeful service, not slavery. And the paper states plainly that this universe was not made just for us. (28:6.15, 28:6.16, 28:6.17, 28:6.18)

This is not an egocentric universe. (28:6.18)

The last two types always work together. Greatness cannot be estimated without knowing the content of goodness, and goodness cannot be portrayed without exhibiting its divine greatness. On a world like Urantia greatness shows itself as self-control, and to the extent that we become good through grace, we are becoming great. (28:6.20, 28:6.21, 28:6.22)

To be great is to be Godlike. (28:6.22)

VII. Ministry of the Seconaphim — Guideposts at the Crossroads of Time

The seconaphim range from the shores of Paradise to the evolutionary worlds, and they teach ascenders on the four hundred ninety study worlds surrounding Uversa, the finishing schools of the universes of time that prepare pilgrims for Havona. The tertiary seconaphim in particular are timesavers, error detectors, faithful teachers, and living guideposts placed at the crossroads of time to guide the feet of anxious pilgrims. (28:7.1, 28:7.3)

Urantia, however, is still under partial spiritual quarantine, and these angels cannot function freely here because some of the reflective circuits are absent. The author says that even under these restrictions they go on joyfully with the means at hand. That attitude—doing gladly what can be done even when conditions are not ideal—is the last impression the paper leaves. (28:7.4)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 28 looks like a classification of angels, but within it is a map of character. What the seconaphim reflect—wisdom and teamwork, joy and service, mercy and time, trust and greatness—are qualities we can begin to learn here and now. The universe reflects these qualities as in a mirror, and before that mirror words cannot hide our motives. (28:6.19)
