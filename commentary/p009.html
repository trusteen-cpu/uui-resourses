Introduction — The Universal Unifier

If Paper 8 introduces who the Infinite Spirit is, Paper 9 turns to what the Spirit does in the universe. It opens with a strange fact. Nothing in the eternity situation foreshadowed that the Conjoint Actor would personalize as a spirit co-ordinated with absolute mind and endowed with unique prerogatives of energy manipulation. Yet with the Spirit’s coming into being, the Father is fully liberated from the bonds of centralized perfection and the fetters of personality absolutism. (9:0.1)

This liberation shows itself in the Spirit’s power to create beings fit to serve as ministering spirits even to material creatures. Where the Father does not go directly, the Spirit and his offspring go, down to the humblest levels. The paper then sets the four great realities side by side and shows the distinctive part the Spirit plays among them. (9:0.1, 9:0.2)

I. Attributes of the Third Source and Center

Each of the many names of the Third Source and Center points to a relationship or a function. God the Spirit, the Infinite Spirit, the Universal Manipulator, the Conjoint Actor, the Absolute Mind, the God of Action: every name shows a different face. Some of his attributes come from the Father and some from the Son, but others are observed in neither. The paper explains these by linking them to the absoluteness of Paradise. (9:1.1, 9:1.2)

If we picture the Father as an original creator and the Son as a spiritual administrator, we should think of the Spirit as a universal co-ordinator, a minister of unlimited co-operation. No actual reality can escape eventual relationship with the Third Source. The paper adds one qualification to the Spirit’s omnipotence: he is actually omnipotent only in the domain of mind, and there his sovereignty is unqualified. (9:1.3, 9:1.5)

The section closes with some of the warmest sentences in the paper. The Spirit is endowed with patience, mercy, and love no less superbly than with power over energy. Even the hard process through which our universe is being shaped has been placed in the hands of the children of mercy. (9:1.8)

The Spirit is supremely competent to minister love and to overshadow justice with mercy. (9:1.8)
The universe of your origin is being forged out between the anvil of justice and the hammer of suffering; but those who wield the hammer are the children of mercy, the spirit offspring of the Infinite Spirit. (9:1.8)

II. The Omnipresent Spirit — God Is Spirit in a Threefold Sense

God is spirit in a threefold sense. He himself is spirit; in his Son he appears as spirit without qualification; and in the Conjoint Actor he appears as spirit allied with mind. Just as the Son is a complement of the Father, the Infinite Spirit is a complement of the Son. (9:2.1, 9:2.2)

The Eternal Son is a spiritualized personalization of the Father; the Infinite Spirit is a personalized spiritualization of the Eternal Son and the Universal Father. (9:2.2)

The people of Urantia are linked with the Deities of Paradise through many lines of spiritual connection. The section names these connections one by one and then stresses that they all act in unison, in perfect co-operation. (9:2.3, 9:2.5)

III. The Universal Manipulator — The God of Action and Energy

The Isle of Paradise is the source of physical gravity, and gravity is among the most dependable things in the whole universe. Yet gravity can be modified or annulled only by forces entrusted to the Third Source and Center. The Infinite Spirit possesses a unique power, antigravity, which is not observably present in either the Father or the Son. (9:3.1, 9:3.2)

Antigravity annuls gravity within a local frame by exerting an equal force presence, and it acts only on material gravity. The Spirit also displays powers that transcend force and neutralize energy, slowing energy down to the point of materialization. The universe is filled with creatures entrusted with such work: physical controllers, power directors, power centers, and others. (9:3.3, 9:3.4, 9:3.6)

The Conjoint Creator is not energy nor the source of energy nor the destiny of energy; he is the manipulator of energy. (9:3.5)

This does not mean that the Spirit works for Paradise. Paradise is not a person; the Spirit acts personally for the Father and the Son. Even those doings of the Spirit that look impersonal are all his own volitional acts. The paper sums up the relationship in a short pair of parallels. (9:3.7, 9:3.8)

IV. The Absolute Mind — Energy Is Thing, Mind Is Meaning, Spirit Is Value

The Third Source has an intellectual nature distinct from his physical and spiritual attributes. The absolute mind is the mind of the Third Person and is inseparable from the personality of God the Spirit. Mind is not inherent in energy, though it can be superimposed upon it; and mind does not need to be added to pure spirit, for spirit is innately conscious. (9:4.1, 9:4.2)

The mind endowment of the seven superuniverses comes from the Seven Master Spirits, and our local universe is pervaded by the Nebadon variant of the Orvonton type of cosmic mind. Mind stands in a different relation to time and space at each of its levels. (9:4.3, 9:4.4)

Source 9:4.4

Energy is thing, mind is meaning, spirit is value. (9:4.5)

This short sentence may be the most useful key in the whole paper. Mind transmutes the values of spirit into the meanings of intellect, and volition brings those meanings to fruit in both the material and the spiritual domains. Personality then unifies the growth that takes place in spirit, mind, and energy. When we come to understand something and carry it into choice and action, that very process becomes the material of our ascent. (9:4.6)

V. The Ministry of Mind — Divine Origin, Immature Present

Creature mind has its origin solely in the Third Source and Center. The Spirit is the bestower of mind, and even the Adjusters, fragments of the Father, cannot indwell human minds until the way has been prepared by the Spirit’s mind action. Through the adjutants the Spirit ministers to human and subhuman intellect, and through the physical controllers even to the most primitive forms of life. (9:5.2, 9:5.3)

This is why evolutionary creatures find it easier to form concepts of the Infinite Spirit than of the Son or the Father: the very existence of our minds discloses, however imperfectly, the reality of the Conjoint Creator. But the paper immediately adds an important warning. (9:5.4)

The Conjoint Creator is the source of mind, but mind is not the Conjoint Creator. (9:5.5)

Human intellect is rooted in the material origin of the animal races, so we must not assume that every phenomenon of mind is divine. All too often we mar our minds by insincerity, subject them to animal fear, and distort them by useless anxiety. Mind has a divine origin and a divine destiny, but our mortal minds have not yet reached that dignity. The right attitude toward our own intellect, then, is not pride but humility. (9:5.5, 9:5.6, 9:5.7)

VI. The Mind-Gravity Circuit — Three Kinds of Drawing Power, and Personality

Much as the Father draws all personality to himself and the Son attracts all spiritual reality, the Conjoint Actor exercises a drawing power on all minds. All true intellectual values, all divine thoughts and perfect ideas, are drawn into this absolute circuit of mind. Read together with the spirit gravity of Paper 7, it gives us the whole system of universe gravities at a glance. (9:6.1, 9:6.2)

Personality arises from none of these. Selfhood of personality dignity does not originate in spirit, mind, or matter; it is the bestowal of the Universal Father alone, and the operation of personality gravity is always his volitional act. Beings like us, whose minds are associated with both energy and spirit, respond to spirit gravity on the spiritual side and to material gravity on the material side. (9:6.4, 9:6.5)

The mind-gravity circuit is dependable, yet not every function of mind is predictable. The author conjectures that part of this unpredictability comes from the function of the Universal Absolute or the incompleteness of the Supreme Being, but states one thing he is sure of. (9:6.8, 9:6.9)

The Infinite Spirit is the perfect expression of the mind of the Creator to all creatures; the Supreme Being is the evolving expression of the minds of all creatures to their Creator. (9:6.9)

VII. Universe Reflectivity

The Conjoint Actor co-ordinates all levels of universe actuality so that the mental, the material, and the spiritual can be recognized at the same time. This is universe reflectivity: the power to see, hear, sense, and know all that happens throughout a superuniverse and to focalize all this information at any desired point. It is shown in perfection on the headquarters world of each superuniverse and finally focalizes on Paradise. (9:7.1)

Lines of spirit can be traced back to the Son, physical energy to Paradise, and mind to the Third Source; but in reflectivity all three are uniquely unified. The authors hold that certain features of this phenomenon can be explained only by postulating the activity of the Supreme Mind, and they suggest that reflectivity may be omniscience within the limits of the finite, the emergence of the presence-consciousness of the Supreme Being. (9:7.2, 9:7.4, 9:7.5)

VIII. Personalities of the Infinite Spirit — A Ladder from Chaos to Glory

The Infinite Spirit can transmit many of his powers to co-ordinate and subordinate personalities. His first Deity-creating act personalized the Seven Master Spirits, and every time the Father and the Son become parent to a Creator Son, the Infinite Spirit becomes ancestor to a local universe Creative Spirit. What the Infinite Spirit is to the total creation, a Creative Spirit is to a local universe. (9:8.2, 9:8.4, 9:8.5)

The section also states the marks of personality. Personality of the finite-creature variety has subjective self-consciousness and objective response to the Father’s personality circuit. There are also Third Source personalities, personal to the Spirit but not part of the Father’s personality circuit. Once we receive a morontia form, we will be able to see even those spiritual associates who are invisible to us now. (9:8.7, 9:8.9, 9:8.13)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 9 deals with difficult ideas such as energy, mind, and reflectivity, but at its center stands a Spirit who is near us. Our very ability to think and to find meaning is the Spirit’s gift, and the countless spiritual personalities who lead us upward are the Spirit’s family. At the same time the paper honestly reminds us that our minds are still immature.

Paper 10 now turns to the Paradise Trinity, which unites the three Deities. Having followed the relation of the Son and the Spirit to the universe through Papers 7 to 9, the reader will see in the next paper how the three persons function together as one.
