Introduction — A Planet Where Experiment Was Allowed

If Paper 57 showed how the body of the planet was formed, Paper 58 tells how life took hold upon it. The author is a member of the Life Carrier Corps still resident on this planet. He begins by explaining what kind of world Urantia is: in all Satania there are only sixty-one life-modification planets like ours. (58:0.1, 58:7.13)

On most inhabited worlds life is placed according to established techniques, and the Life Carriers have little leeway. But about one world in ten is designated a decimal planet, and there they are permitted to experiment in an effort to modify or improve the standard types of living beings. Urantia is such a planet, which is why life here has its own distinctive character. (58:0.1)

I. Physical-Life Prerequisites — Waiting for Salt Water

Six hundred million years ago a commission of Life Carriers from Jerusem arrived to study Urantia’s physical conditions. The principle they started from is clear. Life Carriers cannot initiate life until a sphere is ripe for it, and they cannot make life develop faster than the physical progress of the planet can support. (58:1.1, 58:1.2)

The Satania Life Carriers had projected a sodium chloride pattern of life, so they had to wait until the ocean had become salty enough. The commission returned to Jerusem to await the further breakup of the continent, which would create more inland seas and sheltered bays. Those shallow seas, through which sunlight could reach the bottom, offered the ideal conditions for life implantation. (58:1.3, 58:1.6, 58:1.7)

The Urantia type of protoplasm can function only in a suitable salt solution. (58:1.3)

This passage also tells us something about our own bodies. Our primitive ancestors swam in the salty ocean; today a similar salt solution circulates in our blood stream, bathing every cell. In a sense we carry within us the sea in which life first began. (58:1.4)

II. The Urantia Atmosphere — More Than Two-Score Protections

The atmosphere is life’s protective covering. The ozone layer, which begins about ten miles above the surface, would be only one tenth of an inch thick at surface conditions, yet it shields us from destructive ultraviolet radiation. Were it just a trifle thicker, it would also block the health-giving ultraviolet rays we need. At night the blanketing effect of the air keeps heat from escaping too quickly. (58:2.2, 58:2.5)

The author uses this delicate balance to answer mechanistic interpretations. The Urantia midwayers have gathered more than fifty thousand facts of physics and chemistry that they believe show intelligent purpose in the material creation. And there are more than two-score “fortuitous” protective operations similar to the ozone layer. (58:2.3, 58:2.4)

And yet some of the less imaginative of your mortal mechanists insist on viewing material creation and human evolution as an accident. (58:2.3)

Sunspots are solar cyclones whirling in opposite directions above and below the sun’s equator, and their magnetic fields hurl charged particles to the earth’s outer atmosphere, producing the auroras. The passage shows that the phenomena of the sky form one connected system. (58:2.7, 58:2.8, 58:2.10)

III. Spatial Environment — Life Unshaken by the Short Space Rays

Space is flooded with short space rays pouring from vast hydrogen clouds and blazing nebulae. This short-ray energy charge is four hundred times greater than all other forms of radiant energy in organized space. The great hydrogen clouds are veritable cosmic chemical laboratories, holding every phase of evolving energy and changing matter. (58:3.1, 58:3.2, 58:3.4)

Yet all this tremendous energy activity does not alter the inheritance factors of the germ plasm. It belongs to the environment needed for establishing life, but the implanted life is fully resistant to the flood. And these cosmic conditions, too, had to reach a favorable stage before the Life Carriers could begin their work. (58:3.4, 58:3.5)

The implanted life of the Life Carriers is fully resistant to all of this amazing flood of the short space rays of universe energy. (58:3.4)

IV. The Life-Dawn Era — Three Marine Implantations

The author first corrects a misunderstanding. Despite their name, the Life Carriers did not bring life to Urantia. Life here was formulated on the planet itself, and no other world in all Nebadon has a life existence just like ours. (58:4.1)

Urantia life is unique, original with the planet. (58:4.1)

Five hundred fifty million years ago the Life Carrier corps, in co-operation with spiritual powers and superphysical forces, organized the original life patterns and planted them in the waters. The implantation was made in three places at once, out of foresight: as the continents separated, each great land mass would carry life with it in its warm seas. All planetary life down to the days of Caligastia came from these three implantations. (58:4.2, 58:4.4)

We foresaw that in the later era of the emergence of land life large oceans of water would separate these drifting continental land masses. (58:4.4)

And indeed, by 500 million years ago marine plant life was well established and the continents were already separating. Greenland and the arctic lands, with North and South America, began their slow westward drift; Africa moved slightly south, forming the trough of the Mediterranean basin; and Antarctica, Australia, and the Pacific islands broke away to the south and east. The Life Carriers’ foresight proved exactly right. (58:4.3)

V. The Continental Drift — Land Floating on a Sea of Basalt

The earth’s core is as dense and rigid as steel, and at the center its temperature is slightly above that of the sun’s surface. The outer thousand miles consist mainly of rock; the heavy metals sank deep when the planet was molten. The outer crust, about forty miles thick, rests on a layer of molten basalt, and the continents still float on this cushiony sea. (58:5.1, 58:5.2, 58:5.3, 58:5.4)

The continents stay above water because the sea bottoms are made of denser basalt. Continental pressure at the level of the ocean floor is about 20,000 pounds per square inch, while the water pressure on the ocean floor is only about 5,000 pounds, so every continent tends to creep toward the sea. Earthquakes, the author adds, are caused not by volcanoes but by the sliding and shifting of the solid outer crust. (58:5.4, 58:5.6, 58:5.7)

Figures for density support the explanation. Urantia’s average density is a little more than five and one-half times that of water; granite is less than three times as dense, and the core twelve times. In the prelife ages the sinking ocean floor thrust up a single continent, whose fringes then slid toward the Pacific; ever since, the eastern coast of the ancient Asiatic continent has hovered over the precipice of the ocean depths. (58:5.5, 58:5.8)

VI. The Transition Period — From Plant to Animal

Four hundred fifty million years ago the transition from vegetable to animal life took place. This development was inherent in the original life patterns and came about gradually; even today the transition slime molds can hardly be classified as either plants or animals. But between the great divisions of the animal kingdom, and between the highest prehuman animals and the dawn men, no connecting links will be found. (58:6.1, 58:6.2)

These so-called “missing links” will forever remain missing, for the simple reason that they never existed. (58:6.2)

New species do not arise from the slow accumulation of small variations; they appear suddenly as full-fledged new orders of life. At the same time the author makes clear that this is wholly biologic and strictly natural. The section also distinguishes between body and mind. (58:6.3, 58:6.4)

There is nothing supernatural connected with these genetic mutations. (58:6.4)

Organisms that attain cosmic unity persist; those that fall short cease to exist. This observation—that life never stops adapting—applies just as well to the way we live. (58:6.6, 58:6.8)

VII. The Geologic History Book — A Story Written in Stone

The rocks of the life-dawn era lie at or near the surface over about one eighth of today’s land area, holding fossils of algae, corallike plants, primitive Protozoa, and spongelike transition organisms. The rocks around Lake Superior and in the Grand Canyon are especially easy to interpret, and the St. Croix valley of Wisconsin records one hundred and twenty-seven successive lava flows. (58:7.2, 58:7.3, 58:7.5, 58:7.7)

These strata also foreshadow the age to come. Dark shales and slates in some upper layers contain organic carbon, witnessing to the ancestors of the plants that would overrun the earth in the coal age. The lava flows of the era brought iron, copper, and lead near the surface, and some of the iron mines of North America and Europe lie in the rocks of these transition periods. (58:7.6, 58:7.10)

By the end of this era marine life had spread through all the waters of the world, and the floors of the shallow inland seas were covered with luxuriant vegetation. The whole story remains in the fossil pages of the stone book, which tell the truth to anyone who learns the skill of reading them. (58:7.11, 58:7.12)

It is literally true, as your poet has said, “The dust we tread upon was once alive.” (58:7.12)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 58 shows that life did not arise by accident. It was planted according to a plan that waited for the right time, studied conditions carefully, and looked far into the future. The Life Carriers waited for the seas to become salty, and foreseeing the breakup of the continents, they planted life in three places. Yet what followed unfolded within natural law, and mind was given as a gift from above.
