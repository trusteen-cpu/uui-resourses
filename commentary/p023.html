Introduction — The Infinite Spirit’s First Solitary Creation

If Paper 22 dealt with the Trinitized Sons, Paper 23 introduces the Solitary Messengers, the first and senior order of the Higher Personalities of the Infinite Spirit. They represent the first creative act of the Infinite Spirit functioning alone, without the direct participation of the Father or the Son. Because they were personalized in a single creative episode, their number is fixed. (23:0.1, 23:0.2)

Solitary Messengers are the personal and universal corps of the Conjoint Creator (23:0.1)

The author, a Divine Counselor, tells us that a Solitary Messenger accompanies him on this very mission. Yet even he does not know their total number. Almost 7,690 trillion were reported operating within Orvonton, and he conjectures that this is considerably less than one seventh of the whole. (23:0.2)

I. Nature and Origin of Solitary Messengers — Alone, but Not Lonely

The Infinite Spirit brought the Solitary Messengers into being immediately after creating the Seven Spirits of the Havona Circuits. Except for Paradise and the Havona circuits, no part of creation is older than they are. Even so, all of them are aware of a beginning of selfhood; they are the first of the Infinite Spirit’s creatures to possess time consciousness. They came forth fully grown, and since all are equal, they are classified only by the work they are currently assigned. (23:1.1, 23:1.2, 23:1.3)

Despite their name, they are not lonely spirits. They truly like to work alone and are the only beings in all creation who can enjoy a solitary existence. They can also “listen in” on all the broadcasts of the realms where they serve, so they stay in touch with the wealth of the intellect of all creation. There is no record of a Solitary Messenger falling into darkness, and the council of the Master Spirits has never once had to adjudicate one of their cases. (23:1.5, 23:1.6, 23:1.7)

Though denominated Solitary Messengers, they are not lonesome spirits, for they truly like to work alone. (23:1.5)
There is no record that a Solitary Messenger ever stumbled into darkness. (23:1.6)

There is also a technical reason they must travel alone. When in transit or working in the circuits of time and space, if two or more are close together they are thrown out of liaison with the higher circulating forces; they are “short circuited.” So they have an automatic alarm that warns of approaching conflicts, and they can also detect the nearness of Inspired Trinity Spirits and Thought Adjusters. (23:1.9)

They have no power to extend or reproduce personality, yet there is almost no work of the universes to which they cannot contribute. They live on Paradise and act by the authority of the Infinite Spirit as personalized in the local universes, and even under the influence of a local Mother Spirit they remain constant partakers of the direct circuit from the Infinite Spirit. The author says they are great timesavers for everyone, from the highest to the lowest. (23:1.8, 23:1.10)

II. Assignments of Solitary Messengers — Seven Divisions of Service

Solitary Messengers are not permanently attached to any individual or group, and among themselves they have no organization or government. The Infinite Spirit assigns them to seven divisions of service, but they move freely and constantly from one to another. They are usually designated by number, but the Infinite Spirit knows each by a personal name. (23:2.1, 23:2.2, 23:2.10)

While describing the first division, the author turns suddenly to the confusion of Urantia. The Creators could make this world a veritable paradise, he says, but such an Eden would not develop strong, noble, and experienced characters. Our anxieties, sorrows, and disappointments are as much a part of the divine plan here as perfection is in the central universe. (23:2.12)

such an Eden would not contribute to the development of those strong, noble, and experienced characters which the Gods are so surely forging out on your world between the anvils of necessity and the hammers of anguish. (23:2.12)

Among the superuniverse messengers, the author’s own companion was sent from Uversa to the central universe long before there was life on Urantia; he was absent from the Orvonton roll calls for almost a million years and returned with the desired information. Of all the supercreations, the Solitary Messengers most delight to serve in Orvonton, because here the need is greatest and the opportunities for heroic effort are multiplied. (23:2.16, 23:2.17)

In the more needy realms we all enjoy the satisfaction of a more replete function. (23:2.17)

The explorers of the fifth division volunteer when the reserve corps is overrecruited, and there is never a lack of volunteers. Being highly sensitive to gravity, they can detect even very small dark planets suited to life experiments, and working with the celestial astronomers they bring back much of what is known about outer space. The seventh division, revealing truth, is the work this order regards as its highest trust, and they are often attached to commissions sent to enlarge revelation to worlds and systems. (23:2.19, 23:2.20, 23:2.21, 23:2.24)

The Solitary Messengers regard the assignment to reveal truth as the highest trust of their order. (23:2.24)

III. Time and Space Services — The Fastest Messengers Who Are Persons

The universe has gravity traversers that move instantly, and it has personalities, such as angels and men, bound by time and space. Between the instantaneous velocity of the gravity traversers and the comparatively slow speed of the seraphim, the Solitary Messengers are the only personal messengers who function. So they are used where personality is essential and the loss of time must be avoided. (23:3.2, 23:3.3)

What the author finds hardest to explain is that Solitary Messengers are without form and yet are true individuals. In the seven superuniverses, liberation from time and space ordinarily diminishes personality prerogatives, but the Solitary Messengers are an exception to this general law. They have the advantages of a formless spirit together with all the prerogatives of a full personality. (23:3.7, 23:3.8)

They are true persons, yet endowed with nearly all of the attributes of impersonal spirit manifestation. (23:3.7)

The author admits he cannot explain to a material mind how a spirit can be a real person and still travel through space at such speeds. But because it is so, universe administration does not lose its personal character. Speed and personality together: that is the unique place the Solitary Messengers fill. (23:3.4)

the whole economy of universal administration would be largely deprived of its personal element were this not a fact. (23:3.4)

However much the universe grows, no more Solitary Messengers will probably ever be created. The expanding work of administration will increasingly fall to other spirit ministers and to beings originating in the new creations. (23:3.9)

In remote regions of space beyond the established circuits, they serve as emergency lines of communication, able to send a message to a fellow messenger about one hundred light-years away. The author declares that of all the beings who co-operate in superuniverse affairs, none are more important in practical help and timesaving, for through personal communication they are somewhat independent of space, and through their tremendous speed nearly independent of time. (23:3.5, 23:3.6)

IV. Special Ministry of Solitary Messengers — Those Who Join Personalities Together

The Solitary Messengers seem to be personality co-ordinators for all types of spirit beings. They help spirits of different types and orders understand and fraternize with one another, and they foster in all spirit beings a consciousness of group identity. That those who work alone should be the ones who make everyone kin is the lovely paradox of this paper. (23:4.1)

Their ministry helps to make all the personalities of the far-flung spiritual world akin. (23:4.1)

When a finaliter and a Paradise Citizen trinitize a “child of time and eternity” and that son is sent to Vicegerington, a Solitary Messenger is always assigned as his guardian-companion and never again leaves. Since the messengers are of fixed number and the trinitization of sons of destiny seems unlimited, it appears that the supply will one day run out. Here the author frankly lists questions he cannot answer. (23:4.3, 23:4.4)

The author interprets such cases as showing that the personnel of the grand universe, even of Havona and Paradise, is being reorganized with reference to the vast energy evolutions of outer space. And he receives his unanswered questions not with anxiety but with anticipation. (23:4.5, 23:4.6)

And we anticipate such tremendous adventures, even as you should, with keen relish and ever-heightening expectation. (23:4.6)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 23 reads like a description of a cosmic communications network, yet it holds a deep insight into solitude and fellowship. The Solitary Messengers enjoy working alone, yet they bind all personalities together as kin. The author also tells us that a world of suffering is a forge for character, and that greater need brings greater satisfaction. Even high beings admit what they do not know and meet the future with expectation. (23:1.5, 23:2.17, 23:4.6)

Paper 24 introduces other Higher Personalities from the same family of the Infinite Spirit: beings who supervise the spirit circuits, keep count of will creatures, and guide pilgrims through Havona. Remembering that the Solitary Messengers are the eldest of that family makes the place of the orders that follow easier to see.
