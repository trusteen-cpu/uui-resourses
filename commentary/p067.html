Introduction — Why We Need to Know About the Rebellion

Paper 67 deals with the darkest turning point in Urantia’s history, the planetary rebellion. Its author, a Melchizedek of Nebadon, says that the problems of human existence on this world cannot be understood without knowing of it. The rebellion did not seriously disturb biologic evolution, but it markedly changed the direction of social evolution and spiritual development. (67:0.1, 67:8.6)

Although this upheaval did not seriously interfere with the progress of organic evolution, it did markedly modify the course of social evolution and of spiritual development. (67:0.1)

The paper is a historical record and, at the same time, a short philosophy of sin. It sets side by side a leader who betrayed his trust and fell, and an ordinary human being who stood firm under the same pressure. And it calmly distinguishes how far the consequences of sin reach and where they stop.

I. The Caligastia Betrayal — The Sin of a Broken Trust

Caligastia had been in charge of Urantia for three hundred thousand years when Satan, Lucifer’s assistant, made one of his periodic inspection calls. Satan looked nothing like the familiar caricatures; he was a Lanonandek Son of great brilliance. He told Caligastia of Lucifer’s proposed “Declaration of Liberty,” and the Prince agreed to betray the planet when the rebellion was announced. (67:1.1, 67:1.2)

The author treats this betrayal with special gravity. No trust is more sacred than that of a Planetary Prince responsible for the welfare of evolving mortals, and no evil is more destructive of personality than betraying those who rely on us. By this deliberate sin Caligastia so distorted his personality that his mind has never fully regained its equilibrium. (67:1.3)

of all forms of evil, none are more destructive of personality status than betrayal of trust and disloyalty to one’s confiding friends. (67:1.3)

The section then distinguishes error, evil, sin, and iniquity. They can be read as stages in one’s attitude toward reality, with the will more deeply involved at each step. (67:1.4, 67:1.5)

Sin chosen again and again can become habitual, and habitual sinners can easily become iniquitous. All manner of sins may be forgiven, but the author doubts whether the established iniquiter would ever sincerely feel sorrow or accept forgiveness. It reads as a warning that repeated small choices finally set the direction of a personality. (67:1.6)

II. The Outbreak of Rebellion — A Planet Cut Off

Shortly after Satan’s inspection, in the midwinter of the northern continents, Daligastia called the ten councils into extraordinary session. Caligastia, he announced, was about to proclaim himself absolute sovereign of Urantia, and every administrative group was to resign its powers into Daligastia’s hands. Van, chairman of the supreme council of co-ordination, branded this an act bordering on planetary rebellion and urged all to abstain until an appeal could be taken to Lucifer. (67:2.1, 67:2.2)

Meantime the system circuits had been severed; Urantia was isolated. (67:2.3)

Isolation was a test for celestial beings too. Seraphim, cherubim, and other groups that happened to be on the planet had to choose between sin and righteousness, between the ways of Lucifer and the will of the unseen Father. For more than seven years the authorities of Edentia did not intervene, until every personality concerned had made a final decision. Here we see the universe’s way of respecting each one’s choice rather than forcing it. (67:2.5, 67:2.6)

III. The Seven Crucial Years — A Time When Mind and Spirit Were Tested

When the rebellion was broadcast, the whole system of Satania was quarantined, and “war in heaven” spread to every planet. On Urantia forty of the one hundred, including Van, refused to join the insurrection. But almost half of the administrator and transition seraphim, and 40,119 primary midway creatures, sided with Caligastia. The loyalists lived in a poorly protected settlement east of Dalamatia, guarded by loyal midwayers, and kept the tree of life. (67:3.1, 67:3.2, 67:3.4)

The author lists the reasons Van could not be shaken: clear thinking, wise reasoning, sincere motivation, intelligent loyalty, disciplined character, and the unquestioning dedication of his personality to doing the Father’s will. Loyalty, in other words, is not a passing feeling but the fruit of a long-cultivated inner life. (67:3.6)

This seven years of waiting was a time of heart searching and soul discipline. (67:3.7)

The most striking passage contrasts Caligastia with Amadon. Amadon, a descendant of Andon and Fonta and Van’s human assistant, stood unmoved by the sophistries of Daligastia for all seven years. The size of one’s intelligence and experience does not guarantee loyalty, and a consecrated will, in contact with the indwelling spirit, can find amazing strength. (67:3.7, 67:3.8, 67:3.9)

IV–V. The Caligastia One Hundred after Rebellion; Immediate Results of Rebellion

At the final roll call the Prince’s staff divided clearly along council lines. The forty who remained loyal were later transferred to Jerusem, where they resumed their Paradise journey. The sixty who rebelled chose Nod as their leader, but they soon lost the sustenance of the system life circuits and were degraded to the status of mortal beings. (67:4.1, 67:4.2)

The descendants of the rebel staff became known as the Nodites, and their mingling with the peoples of earth gave rise to stories of gods coming down to mate with mortals. Without spiritual sustenance they eventually died a natural death, and much later idolatry grew out of the wish to honor their memory. The final fate of the sixty still awaits adjudication, and the text records the hope that all sincere penitents will be rehabilitated. (67:4.3, 67:4.4, 67:4.5, 67:4.7)

Caligastia tried to rebuild society all at once according to his own ideas of freedom. But for those who were not ready, liberty became license; when revolution displaced evolution, confusion and regression followed. One hundred and sixty-two years after the rebellion a tidal wave swept over Dalamatia. The lesson is that hasty reform can lead to an even deeper retreat. (67:5.1, 67:5.3, 67:5.4)

Liberty was quickly translated into license by the half-evolved primitive men of those days. (67:5.1)

VI. Van — The Steadfast

Van’s followers withdrew to the highlands west of India and there planned the rehabilitation of the world. Van placed human affairs in the hands of ten commissions of four each, and when the loyal staff returned to Jerusem the Amadonites took up these responsibilities. The Amadonites were the group of 144 loyal Andonites—thirty-nine men and one hundred and five women—who became the “biologic leaven” that furnished leadership through the long dark ages after the rebellion. (67:6.1, 67:6.2, 67:6.3)

Urantia was then governed by a council of twelve Melchizedek receivers, whose policies Van faithfully carried out. Within a thousand years of the rebellion he had more than three hundred and fifty advanced groups scattered over the world. Sustained by the tree of life and the special ministry of the Melchizedeks, Van and Amadon lived for over one hundred and fifty thousand years, fostering human evolution until the coming of Adam. (67:6.4, 67:6.5, 67:6.6, 67:6.7)

The text adds a remarkable fact. The Constellation Fathers of Edentia had immediately sustained Van’s appeal on every point, but the decision never reached him because the circuits were cut; it lay lodged in a relay energy transmitter until it was recently discovered through the investigations of the midwayers. Justice may come to light late, but it is not lost. (67:6.9, 67:6.10)

VII. Remote Repercussions of Sin — Personal and Collective Consequences

This section is the theological heart of the paper. The author divides the consequences of sin in two directions: the personal consequences reaped by the creature who rejects light, and the impersonal consequences shared by all who live within its range of effect. Fifty thousand years after the rebellion, mankind had gained very little over its condition when Caligastia arrived. (67:7.1, 67:7.2, 67:7.3)

Source 67:7.2

From this distinction comes the paper’s most comforting conclusion. Sin greatly delayed the progress of civilization and society, but it cannot prevent the spiritual achievement of any individual who chooses to know God and do his will. No one’s eternal survival is endangered by another’s sin. (67:7.5, 67:7.6, 67:7.7)

never does the sin of any being rob another of the realization of the divine right of personality survival. (67:7.5)
No person is ever made to suffer vital spiritual deprivation because of the sin of another. (67:7.7)

VIII. The Human Hero of the Rebellion — Amadon

The author recalls the exhilaration he felt on Edentia as he read the Salvington broadcasts of those days. The records of Salvington portray Amadon as the outstanding character of the entire system. For seven years one question came first from all subordinate celestial life. (67:8.1, 67:8.2)

“What of Amadon of Urantia, does he still stand unmoved?” (67:8.3)

The loyalty of Amadon and his 143 comrades, the author says, has already done more good in Nebadon and Orvonton than can ever be outweighed by all the evil and sorrow of the Lucifer rebellion. It also illuminates the wisdom of the Father’s plan to recruit the Corps of Mortal Finality largely from the common clay of ascending mortals—mortals just like Amadon. (67:8.4, 67:8.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 67 shows that high position and great intelligence do not guarantee loyalty, and that the unwavering choice of one ordinary person can move a universe. It also says plainly that the confusion of the world we inherited may come from the faults of others, but that no one can block an individual’s path to God.

PracticeThe teaching behind it

Turn back from a wrong choice before it becomes a habitRepeated sin becomes habitual and leans toward iniquity
Under pressure and clever argument, stand on your everyday dedicationVan’s loyalty was the fruit of thinking, motive, character, and dedication
Do not use the world’s confusion as an excuse for your own spiritual growthAnother’s sin cannot prevent my survival

Paper 68, “The Dawn of Civilization,” turns to how human society slowly built co-operation and institutions under the pressure of fear and need. Remembering how deep the setback was in this paper helps us feel the weight of the slow advance that followed.
