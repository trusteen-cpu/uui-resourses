Introduction — The Attributes Converge in Fatherhood

If Paper 2 portrayed God’s character, Paper 3 takes up his attributes: his everywhereness, infinite power, universal knowledge, limitlessness, supreme rule, and primacy. From the start the paper notes that the infinite Father is invisible, and that the ones who compensate for this invisibility are the Creator Sons. They are the personal expression of God to the children of time and space. (3:0.1, 3:0.2)

The author is careful about ranking the attributes. Still, if one had to be put first, it would be creatorship. And that creatorship reaches its height in the truth that God is a Father. All the vast attributes this paper discusses point toward that single fact of fatherhood. (3:0.3)

And the creatorship of Deity culminates in the universal truth of the Fatherhood of God. (3:0.3)

I. God’s Everywhereness — The Friend Within Our Own Heart

God alone can be in numberless places at the same time. He is present in all parts and in all hearts of his creation, and it is literally true that God is all and in all. But the paper adds at once that while the cosmos reveals God, it cannot hold the whole of his infinity. The living God is immeasurably greater than the sum of creation. (3:1.1, 3:1.2)

God is revealed throughout the cosmos, but the cosmos can never contain or encompass the entirety of the infinity of God. (3:1.2)

The warmest passage in this section is its confession that God lives in the creature. A paragraph gathering the sayings of ancient sages portrays God not as one far away but as part of us. (3:1.4)

Man goes forth searching for a friend while that very friend lives within his own heart. (3:1.4)

This presence is not untouched by our wrongdoing. The Thought Adjuster must go through the consequences of evil thinking together with the mind it indwells. So when we do wrong, we are in effect tormenting the gift of God within us. (3:1.5)

This section also explains why God’s presence feels different from one person to another. Whether an Adjuster indwells a mind determines the fact of God’s presence, but his effective presence depends on how far that mind co-operates with the Adjuster. The Father does not withdraw because he has been slighted; his children, by their own choices, determine how far his influence reaches in their hearts. (3:1.11, 3:1.12)

II. God’s Infinite Power — A Power That Is Not Blind

All the universes know that the Lord God omnipotent reigns. God is the primal cause of all physical phenomena; viewed as an unspiritual phenomenon, God is energy. He has ordained the circuits of all energy and decreed the time and manner in which energy-matter appears, and all of it is held in the gravity control centering on nether Paradise. (3:2.1, 3:2.3, 3:2.4)

Yet the weight of this section lies less in the size of God’s power than in its character. The Father is not a transient force or a fluctuating energy. When emergencies arise in human experience, he has already foreseen them all. When natural laws seem to be suspended or hastily corrected, it only looks that way because our view is so narrow. (3:2.6, 3:2.7)

Regardless of appearances, the power of God is not functioning in the universe as a blind force. (3:2.6)

For the same reason, many of God’s acts seem arbitrary or even cruel to us. His doings consider the best good not only of one person or one planet but of all concerned. Since we are part of God’s family, we must sometimes share in the family discipline. And many things happen on the evolutionary worlds that are not the personal doings of the Father. This is an important teaching: we should not at once attribute suffering to the will of God. (3:2.8, 3:2.9, 3:2.10)

III. God’s Universal Knowledge — Down to a Single Sparrow

God knows all things. The Universal Father is the only personality in all the universe who actually knows the number of the stars and planets of space. Quoting Jesus, the paper says that not one sparrow falls to the ground without the Father’s knowledge, and that the very hairs of our heads are numbered. God’s knowledge is supplemented indirectly through the divine Sons and directly through the indwelling Adjusters. (3:3.1, 3:3.2, 3:3.3)

Your Father knows what you have need of even before you ask him. (3:3.2)

If God knows everything in advance, what becomes of our freedom? The paper does not decide whether God chooses to foreknow events of sin. It says only that even if he does, such foreknowledge does not in the least abrogate his children’s freedom. Omnipotence, moreover, is not the power to do the ungodlike, and omniscience is not the knowing of the unknowable. (3:3.4, 3:3.5)

One thing is certain: God is never subjected to surprise. (3:3.4)

IV. God’s Limitlessness — Given Away, Yet Never Diminished

The Father has given himself unstintingly to his Sons, his creations, and their countless creatures, yet he has lost nothing of what he possesses. However many new universes come into being, the controlling power of Paradise remains adequate; however freely mind is distributed to countless beings, the source of divine wisdom is not impoverished. Nor is there any limit to the number of Adjusters he can send. (3:4.1, 3:4.2, 3:4.3, 3:4.4)

Finite man cannot think his way through God’s infinity. But here the paper offers a striking turn: that same finite human being can actually experience the full and undiminished impact of the infinite Father’s love. The quantity of that experience, however, is limited by our capacity for spiritual receptivity—our capacity to love the Father in return. (3:4.6)

Therefore man’s nearest and dearest approach to God is by and through love, for God is love. (3:4.7)

V. The Father’s Supreme Rule — Why Uncertainty Is Needed

In the creations beyond Havona the Father does not exercise his power directly; he rules through his Sons and their subordinate personalities. He acts independently only when a delegated personality fails in its trust. In the hearts of men the Father may not always have his way, but in the conduct and destiny of a planet the divine plan prevails. (3:5.1, 3:5.2, 3:5.3)

The heart of this section is its explanation that the uncertainties of life do not contradict God’s sovereignty. The author asks nine questions. Each virtue we desire can grow only in an environment where its opposite is possible. (3:5.5)

VI. The Father’s Primacy — A Person, Not a Machine

The Father delegates authority, but he reserves all final decisions. The universe is neither inevitable nor accidental; it is a work of creation and therefore wholly subject to the Creator’s will. And that will is divine truth and living love. The paper argues that universe causes cannot be lower than universe effects, and that man as a moral being cannot be explained unless the reality of the Universal Father is acknowledged. (3:6.1, 3:6.2, 3:6.3)

The will of God is divine truth, living love (3:6.2)

The remarks on the mechanistic philosopher are pointed. He rejects a sovereign will, yet that very will made the laws he so deeply reverences. To humanize God too much is a blunder, but to mechanize him completely is still more foolish. (3:6.4, 3:6.5)

Asked whether the Father suffers, the author honestly answers that he does not know. The Creator Sons certainly can suffer, and he thinks the Father too experiences a fatherly and sympathetic understanding. God is power, pattern, and principle, but he is more: he is personal, and he shows a Father’s love to his children. (3:6.6, 3:6.7)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

The attributes of Paper 3 sound distant and abstract, but they converge on a single fact: the One who is everywhere present, who knows all things, and who holds all power is our Father, and he lives within us. The paper also urges us not to read life’s uncertainties and pains as the absence of God, but as the soil in which virtues grow.

PracticeThe teaching behind it

Increase your co-operation with the Adjuster to deepen God’s presenceHis effective presence depends on the mind’s co-operation
Do not at once blame God for hardshipMany things occur that are not the Father’s personal doings
Treat uncertain situations as chances to grow courage and faithVirtues grow where their opposites are possible
Come to the Father with trust instead of worryHe knows what we need before we ask

Paper 4 goes on to God’s relation to the universe—how he deals with it, and the misunderstandings people have held about him. If we remember the attributes of Paper 3 as a Father’s attributes, the discussion of the next paper will feel much closer to home.
