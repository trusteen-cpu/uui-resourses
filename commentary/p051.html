Introduction — The Second Sons to Arrive

When primitive man reaches the limit of natural evolutionary development during the dispensation of a Planetary Prince, that attainment signals the System Sovereign to send the second order of sonship. These are the biologic uplifters, the Material Son and Daughter, usually known on a planet as Adam and Eve. The original Material Son of Satania was named Adam, and all who go out to the worlds of the system carry his name. (51:0.1)

These Sons are the material gift of the Creator Son to the inhabited worlds. (51:0.2)

They remain on their planet, together with the Planetary Prince, throughout its evolutionary course. On a world with a loyal prince the venture holds little hazard, but on an apostate planet, with no spiritual ruler and cut off from communication, the mission is fraught with grave danger. Urantia was just such a place. Even so, the paper says plainly that Adam’s mission was not in vain and that the councils on high do not reckon it a total loss. (51:0.2, 51:0.3)

I. Origin and Nature of the Material Sons of God — A Link Between Spirit and Matter

The Material Sons and Daughters are the offspring of the Creator Son; the Universe Mother Spirit does not take part in producing them. The Creator Son makes only one pair for each system, attuned to the life pattern of that system—otherwise the reproductive potential of the Adams could not function with the mortals of those worlds. The Adam and Eve who came to Urantia descended from the original Satania pair. (51:1.1, 51:1.2)

They stand eight to ten feet tall, and their bodies glow with a violet light. Material blood flows in them, yet they are surcharged with divine energy, and they sustain their immortality by a dual nutrition: they eat materialized energy and also take in cosmic energies directly. Adam and Eve are equal yet different, male and female, complementing each other, and they are designed to serve almost always in pairs. (51:1.3, 51:1.4)

They are equal but differential, male and female—hence complemental—and are designed to serve on almost all assignments in pairs. (51:1.3)

This immortality, however, is conditional. If they fail in their mission or rebel, they are cut off from the source of light and life, become practically material beings, and finally meet material death. In Satania thirteen Planetary Adams and 681,204 Material Sons in subordinate posts have been lost through rebellion and default, most of them at the time of the Lucifer rebellion. (51:1.4, 51:1.5)

Material Sons do not have Thought Adjusters, but through planetary service they acquire the experiential capacity for Adjuster indwelling and the Paradise ascent. Above all, unlike other created Sons, they can be seen by mortals and can mingle with them. Children born to them on an evolutionary planet are not immune to natural death, and their longevity decreases with each generation. (51:1.6, 51:1.7, 51:1.8)

These unique and wonderfully useful beings are the connecting links between the spiritual and physical worlds. (51:1.6)

II. Transit of the Planetary Adams — A Road with No Return

When a world reaches the height of physical evolution, the System Sovereign convenes the corps of Material Sons and chooses, from among the volunteers, an Adam and Eve of the senior corps. Because they are semimaterial, seraphim cannot carry them as they are; they are dematerialized over about three days and then enseraphimed for the journey. On arrival the Life Carriers direct their rematerialization, which takes ten to twenty-eight days, and they remain unconscious the whole time. (51:2.1, 51:2.2, 51:2.4)

What stands out is that this is a one-way road. There is a technique for sending them from Jerusem to an evolutionary world, but no equivalent method for bringing them back. The exception is a planet doomed by physical catastrophe, when the technique of dematerialization is installed for all the salvable survivors, who are carried to a new world. Adam and Eve leave home having chosen to share the destiny of the world they go to serve. (51:2.3)

III. The Adamic Missions — The Garden and the Violet Race

On arrival, the Material Son and Daughter build their garden home on a site chosen by the Planetary Prince. These gardens are called Eden in honor of Edentia, the constellation capital, and they are usually placed in a secluded, near-tropic region. The pair’s chief business is to multiply and uplift the children of time, but they do not intermingle at once; for many generations they remain apart and build up a strong race of their own order, the violet race. (51:3.1, 51:3.2, 51:3.3)

A Planetary Adam and Eve are, in potential, the full gift of physical grace to the mortal races. (51:3.3)

Urantia’s Adam and Eve were at a great disadvantage from the start. Caligastia mounted crafty opposition, and despite the warnings of the Melchizedek receivers, the Edenic pair were outmaneuvered and violated the covenant of their trusteeship. Yet the attempt to draw them into the Lucifer rebellion failed. (51:3.4)

The paper reveals that the voice that rebuked them in the Garden was not God but the seraphim Solonia, chief of the planetary helpers. It notes that because tradition ascribes everything supernatural to the Lord God, Urantians have often been confused about the nature of the Universal Father. This is worth remembering when we read the biblical story. (51:3.5)

The secondary midway creatures belong to the Adamic missions. On Urantia most of the primary midwayers rebelled with Caligastia and have been interned since Pentecost; on that day the loyal primary and secondary midwayers voluntarily united, and they have served as one unit in world affairs ever since. (51:3.6, 51:3.7, 51:3.8)

IV. The Six Evolutionary Races — The Gift of Diversity

On the inhabited worlds the red man is usually the first to reach human levels, and the other colored races appear soon afterward. The Life Carriers give the full bestowal of living energies to the first race, and each later race shows variation at the expense of that original endowment. The paper explains that the first, third, and fifth races are the more highly endowed. (51:4.1, 51:4.2, 51:4.3)

Yet this differentiation is not simply a loss. The appearance of several races provides desirable variations in mortal types and allows an expression of diverse human potentials that could not be had otherwise. When this diversity is later upstepped by the Adamic race, it serves the progress of mankind as a whole. On normal worlds, primitive slavery is abolished soon after the arrival of the violet race. (51:4.4, 51:4.7)

On Urantia this plan of amalgamation was not extensively carried out. The paper therefore says that we cannot understand the status of these peoples on an average inhabited planet by looking at the remnants of the early races on our world. The picture of a normal world is not a yardstick for judging our present reality but a reference map showing where things went astray. (51:4.4)

V. Racial Amalgamation — Bestowal of the Adamic Blood

The violet people usually do not begin to blend with the natives until they number over a million. On normal worlds the Planetary Adam and Eve themselves never mate with the evolutionary races; that work belongs to their offspring. The prince’s staff bring superior men and women to the Garden for voluntary mating with the Adamic children, and on most worlds being chosen is considered the highest honor. (51:5.2, 51:5.3)

This plan reduces racial wars and sets the races competing to qualify for admission to the Garden. The violet race is monogamous, and every man or woman who unites with an Adamic son or daughter pledges to take no other mate and to teach their children single-matedness. (51:5.4, 51:5.5)

in one hundred thousand years more progress is made than in a million years of former struggle. (51:5.6)

On Urantia the plan miscarried, yet great progress has been made since Adam’s life plasm was given to our peoples. The paper says that we must now work out the problem of race improvement chiefly by human methods of adaptation and control rather than by the Adamic technique. (51:5.6, 51:5.7)

VI. The Edenic Regime — Two Centers of Culture and Seven Fathers

On normal worlds the garden headquarters becomes the second center of world culture, setting the pace of civilization together with the city of the Planetary Prince. The two schools are not far apart and work in harmony, eventually merging into one. The author invites us to imagine what it would mean if somewhere in the Levant there were a world university of culture that had functioned without interruption for 37,000 years. (51:6.1, 51:6.2, 51:6.3)

It is custom which eventually spreads the ideals of Eden to a whole world. (51:6.3)

VII. United Administration — A World Governed Together

With the first Magisterial Son the fourth dispensation begins, and the planet prospers under the joint rule of the Planetary Prince, the Material Son, and the Magisterial Son. Meanwhile the educational and administrative work of the prince and of Adam is united, and when the Magisterial Son departs, Adam assumes the outward direction of the sphere. When the era of light and life arrives, the prince becomes Planetary Sovereign, and Adam and Eve serve as what might be called joint prime ministers. (51:7.1, 51:7.2)

The roles are divided as well. The prince and his staff foster the spiritual and philosophic domains, Adam and Eve attend to the physical, scientific, and economic status of the realm, and both promote the arts, social relations, and intellectual achievement. Once the new capital is established, from fifty to one hundred subcapitals are organized. (51:7.3, 51:7.4)

if Urantians could only observe life on such a planet, they would immediately appreciate the value of those things which their world has lost through embracing evil and participating in rebellion. (51:7.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 51 keeps us from seeing Adam and Eve only as ancestors who went wrong. They were a gift sent by the Creator Son, volunteers who took a road of no return to share the destiny of their world. Despite their failure, the lineage they left behind hastened human progress. Knowing what was lost should lead not to resentment but to recognizing the part that now falls to us. (51:0.3, 51:5.6)
