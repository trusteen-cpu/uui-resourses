Introduction — Picturing Infinity as an Ellipse

Paper 105 is one of the most philosophical papers in The Urantia Book. Its author, a Melchizedek of Nebadon, admits his limits from the outset. Even to high orders of universe intelligences, infinity is only partially comprehensible, and the finality of reality only relatively understandable. So he suggests that we picture eternity-infinity as an almost limitless ellipse, produced by one absolute cause, moving through endless diversification, and ever seeking an infinite potential of destiny. (105:0.1, 105:7.19)

reality totality is infinity and therefore can never be fully comprehended by any mind that is subinfinite in concept capacity. (105:0.2)

The author tells us he makes the attempt knowing full well that his concepts must suffer profound distortion as they are brought down to the level of mortal understanding. We do well, then, to read this paper not as a precise blueprint but as a humble gesture toward what cannot be fathomed. (105:0.3)

I. The Philosophic Concept of the I AM — and the Face of the Father

The philosophers of the universes attribute absolute primal causation in infinity to the Universal Father functioning as the I AM. The I AM is the Infinite and is also infinity. He is neither deified nor undeified, neither personal nor impersonal. No qualification can be applied to him except the statement that the I AM is. (105:1.1, 105:1.3, 105:1.4)

Presenting this idea to human minds carries real dangers. It is so remote from experience that it easily distorts meanings and leads to misconceptions of values. The author therefore makes one thing clear: in all personality meanings and values, the I AM is the same as the First Person of Deity, the Universal Father of all personalities. (105:1.2)

Because the finite mind needs a beginning, the text imagines a hypothetical static moment in the infinitely distant past. At that moment infinity is filled by the Infinite and there is no differentiation at all; actuals are still contained within their potentials, and potentials have not yet appeared within the I AM. Yet even then, we must assume the possibility of self-will. (105:1.5)

Ever remember that man's comprehension of the Universal Father is a personal experience. (105:1.6)

This sentence anchors the whole paper. The Father we worship is that phase of the Infinite which we actually experience in our personalities, while the I AM remains our hypothesis for all that we feel is unknowable. Even the immensity of the master universe may be only a partial revelation of the Infinite, yet our concepts of infinity will keep growing throughout our endless careers. (105:1.6, 105:1.7, 105:1.8)

And the face which the Infinite turns toward all universe personalities is the face of a Father, the Universal Father of love. (105:1.8)

II–III. The I AM as Triune and as Sevenfold; The Seven Absolutes of Infinity

The seven absolute realities—the three existential persons of Deity, the Isle of Paradise, and the three Absolutes—are all co-ordinately eternal and without beginning. To explain them to human beings, however, we must arrange them in the language of time. The text postulates a “first” volitional expression and a “first” repercussional reaction within the I AM, and describes how this self-differentiation expands from a dual to a triune conception, and then to a sevenfold self-relationship. (105:2.1, 105:2.2, 105:2.4)

The first phase can be partially experienced on spiritual levels even by mortals still in the flesh, through worship. The creative acts of the third phase are always marked by unity, because they reflect outwardly the oneness of the Father-Son and of the Trinity. The seventh phase is the one that embraces everything. (105:2.5, 105:2.7, 105:2.11)

in him we all live and move and have our being, from the creatures of space to the citizens of Paradise (105:2.11)
The seven Absolutes are the premise of reality. (105:3.1)

Section 3 introduces the seven Absolutes one by one. The statement that touches us most closely concerns the Second Source and Center. Without the action and aid of this absolute pattern for all personalities, no personality can reach the spirit levels of existence. (105:3.3)

No personality can hope to attain the Universal Father except through his Eternal Son (105:3.3)

The Isle of Paradise is the absolute of patterns and the foundation of gravity control. The eternal Isle is absolutely at rest, while all other organized energy is in motion. The Conjoint Actor integrates the cosmic energies of Paradise with the spirit energies of the Son and is the source of mind. The Deity Absolute is the destiny inceptor; the Unqualified Absolute is a quiescence that even eternity cannot exhaust; and the Universal Absolute compensates the tension between the two. (105:3.4, 105:3.5, 105:3.6, 105:3.7, 105:3.8)

Paradise is not in space; space exists relative to Paradise, and the chronicity of motion is determined through Paradise relationship. (105:3.4)

To human minds the First Source and Center appears to come before all the other Absolutes, yet the Son, the Spirit, the three Absolutes, and the Paradise Isle coexist with him eternally. This relationship cannot be stated in the language of time without paradox. Even so, it is both fact and truth that all reality rests on the eternal existence and relationships of the seven Absolutes. (105:3.9, 105:3.10)

V–VI. Promulgation of Finite Reality and Its Repercussions

The possibility of the finite lies within the Infinite, but it was the free will of the First Source and Center that made it actual. Only the infinity of the Father’s will could so qualify the absolute level as to create a finite. With that act begins the growth cycle—a majestic downsweep from the heights of infinity to the finite, forever swinging back inward toward Paradise—and time itself comes into being. (105:5.4, 105:5.5, 105:5.6)

The author adds that although he tells this as a sequence, transcendentals both preceded and succeeded all that is finite. Transcendental ultimates are both the cause and the consummation of the finite. The volitional act that promulgates the finite also means a qualification of absoluteness and the appearance of relativities. (105:5.2, 105:5.3)

Even the material mind of the mortal creature thus becomes partner with the divine Adjuster in the dualization of the immortal soul. (105:6.5)

VII. Eventuation of Transcendentals — Between the Finite and the Absolute

Transcendentals are subinfinite and subabsolute, yet superfinite and supercreatural—an integrating level. To creatures they appear to follow from the finite; from the eternity viewpoint they anticipate it. Havona illustrates the paradox. It is neither absolute nor an evolutionary creation, and it acts as a buffer between Paradise and the finite creations. (105:7.1, 105:7.2)

But Havona itself is not a transcendental—it is Havona. (105:7.2)

As the Supreme is associated with finites, so the Ultimate is identified with transcendentals—but the difference between them is one of quality, not merely of degree. Our universe exists on finite, transcendental, and absolute levels, and it is unified absolutely by the triunities, functionally by the Architects of the Master Universe, and relatively by the Seven Master Spirits. (105:7.3, 105:7.15, 105:7.16)

God the Sevenfold reveals the personality and divinity of the Universal Father to creatures of both maximum and submaximum status. The forces of the Absolutes, the spirits of the Deities, and the personalities of the Gods, which stirred in the eternal past in response to the primordial self-will, now echo in this universe age as the vast cosmic panorama before our eyes. (105:7.17, 105:7.18)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 105 deals with heights that can make the head spin, but its conclusion is simple. The face the Infinite turns toward us is the face of a loving Father, and in him we all live and move. Even the imperfection of evolution is a space left open so that we might become partners in divine creation. The text also leaves open the possibility that the diversification of original reality may go on, onward and outward, age after age. (105:1.8, 105:6.5, 105:7.18)
