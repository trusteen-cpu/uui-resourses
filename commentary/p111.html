Introduction — An Ancient Intuition, a New Revelation

Paper 111, the last in the series on Adjusters, deals with the soul that grows between the Adjuster and the mind. The author first states plainly that, because of the Adjuster’s presence, neither science nor philosophy can ever satisfactorily comprehend the evolving soul. The soul is the child of the universe and can really be known only through cosmic insight and spiritual discovery. (111:0.1)

The idea of a soul and of an indwelling spirit is not new. People have long felt that something is growing within human nature that will endure beyond the brief span of temporal life. The savage associated the soul with blood, breath, and shadows, and various civilizations tried in their own ways to distinguish the indwelling spirit from the soul. (111:0.2, 111:0.3)

At first only kings were thought to have kas, but later all righteous men were believed to possess them. Such intuitions show that the soil for receiving the revelation of the Adjuster and the soul had long been prepared in humankind. (111:0.6)

I. The Mind Arena of Choice — Ship, Pilot, and Captain

The Adjuster’s work is spiritual, yet it must be done on an intellectual foundation. Mind is the human soil from which the Adjuster, with the co-operation of the personality, evolves the soul. Our bodies were provided by material evolution and our Adjusters were given by the Father, but mind alone has been placed in our hands, subject to our decisions. (111:1.1, 111:1.4)

Material mind is the arena in which human personalities live, are self-conscious, make decisions, choose God or forsake him, eternalize or destroy themselves. (111:1.3)

What decides survival is not our present state but our direction. It is not so much what the mind comprehends as what it desires to comprehend, not so much being conscious of God as yearning for God, that leads to ascension. Mind is a cosmic instrument on which we can play either the discords of destruction or the melodies of God identification. (111:1.5, 111:1.6)

The Adjuster is impervious to evil and incapable of sin. But the mind can be twisted and made ugly by a self-seeking will, or made noble and beautiful by a God-knowing will. Until the evolving mind becomes wholly mechanized or entirely spiritualized, its stability and peace depend on personality choice and spirit identification. (111:1.6, 111:1.7)

What you are today is not so important as what you are becoming day by day and in eternity. (111:1.5)

We do not hand our will over to the Adjuster like slaves. We actively choose to follow its leading when that leading differs from our natural desires. To the Adjuster the human will is supreme; it manipulates the mind but never dominates it against our will. The text sums up this relationship with the image of a voyage. (111:1.8, 111:1.9)

Mind is your ship, the Adjuster is your pilot, the human will is captain. (111:1.9)

II. Nature of the Soul — Neither Material nor Spiritual, but Morontial

The material mind is the cosmic loom carrying the morontia fabrics, on which the Adjuster threads the spirit patterns of a character of enduring values. The character so woven is the surviving soul, a potential finaliter. The soul is not a blend of the qualities of mind and spirit but an entirely new and unique universe value. (111:2.2, 111:2.3)

Source 111:2.4
The three antecedents of the soulWhat each includes
1. The human mindTogether with all the cosmic influences antecedent to it and impinging on it
2. The indwelling divine spiritAll the potentials of this fragment of absolute spirituality and its associated spiritual influences
3. The relationship between mind and spiritA value found in neither — neither material nor spiritual but morontial: the soul

The midway creatures have long called this soul the mid-mind, because it lies between the lower, material mind and the higher, cosmic mind. Its potential arises where the creature’s urge to know God meets the Creator’s urge to know man. The soul is the joint offspring of a mind dominated by a will that craves to know God, working in liaison with the Adjuster. (111:2.8, 111:2.10)

III. The Evolving Soul — An Education, Not a Test

The mistakes of the mind and errors of conduct can delay the soul’s evolution, but they cannot inhibit it once the Adjuster has begun it with the consent of the will. Until fusion, however, we remain free to forsake the Father’s will. Fusion is the sign that we have chosen, eternally and unreservedly, to do the Father’s will. (111:3.1)

During life the power of decision resides in the circuits of the material mind, but as we grow, the self becomes more and more identified with the emerging soul. After resurrection the personality is completely identified with the morontia self, so the soul is the embryo of our future personality identity. (111:3.2)

Both the human mind and the Adjuster are conscious of the soul—the Adjuster fully, the mind partially—and the soul becomes increasingly conscious of both as it grows. At first wholly morontial, the soul eventually ascends to true spirit levels, capable of fusion with the Adjuster. (111:3.3, 111:3.4)

The mortal career, the soul's evolution, is not so much a probation as an education. (111:3.5)

Mind knows quantity, reality, and meanings, but quality—values—is felt. As the soul becomes permeated by truth, beauty, and goodness, it becomes indestructible. (111:3.6, 111:3.7)

What you begin in time you will assuredly finish in eternity—if it is worth finishing. (111:3.7)

IV. The Inner Life — The Truly Creative Place

Meanings arise from the combination of recognition and understanding, and meanings and values are perceived only in the inner realm. All the advances of true civilization are therefore born in the inner world. When most of a generation’s youth devote themselves only to the materialistic pursuits of the outer world, civilization is in jeopardy. (111:4.2, 111:4.3, 111:4.4)

Personality is creative, but it functions creatively only in the inner life of the individual. Just as snow crystals are all hexagonal yet no two are alike, personality follows types but is always unique. The higher concepts that will build a more enduring civilization are found only in the higher levels of the superconscious mind, where it touches the spirit realm. (111:4.5, 111:4.6)

Inner creativity brings responsibility. An imagination whose stage is crowded with prejudice, hate, fear, and resentment cannot produce worthy children. The text diagnoses the cause of poverty, war, and racial hatred in nations led by men rich in ideas but poverty-stricken in ideals. Evil is creativity turned toward disintegration, and all conflict is a kind of civil war in the personality. (111:4.9, 111:4.10, 111:4.11)

The past is unchangeable; only the future can be changed by the ministry of the present creativity of the inner self. (111:4.12)

V. The Consecration of Choice — Not Surrender but Expansion

Doing the will of God is simply showing our willingness to share our inner life with God. Sharing is Godlike. And before we ever trust ourselves to God, God has first trusted a part of himself to us, consenting to live in us subject to the human will. (111:5.1, 111:5.3, 111:5.4)

Choosing the Father’s will is therefore not a surrender of the will but a consecration and an expansion of it. That choice consists less in the negation “Not my will but yours be done” than in a positive affirmation. (111:5.5, 111:5.6)

“It is my will that your will be done.” (111:5.6)

VI–VII. The Human Paradox; The Adjuster’s Problem — Security amid Uncertainty

Man is part of nature yet able to transcend it; finite, yet indwelt by a spark of infinity. The courage that conquers nature is a courage that might yield to self-pride. Finiteness itself is not evil; evil and sin arise from the misuse and perversion of the finite. Amid this insecurity, only living faith can sustain us. (111:6.1, 111:6.2, 111:6.3, 111:6.8)

Of all the dangers, pride is the greatest. Reasonable self-confidence is not to be deplored, but egotism is suicidal. Whether in an individual or a nation, pride is deceitful, intoxicating, and sin-breeding. (111:6.9, 111:6.10)

The paper does not set knowledge against faith. Science is the source of facts, and the mind cannot operate without facts. To apply the truth we find in inner experience to the practical demands of daily life, we need a clear knowledge of facts. Only when we discover divine law and divine love and unify them do we begin to appreciate the fullness of the First Source and Center. (111:6.6, 111:6.7)

Uncertainty with security is the essence of the Paradise adventure (111:7.1)

The Adjuster cannot stop the struggle of life or lessen its hardships. But if we allow it, it will fight with us and for us, showing us the real motive and eternal purpose behind all our toil. The author closes with the statement of a guardian seraphim: despite the unending conflict between her subject’s two natures, with the Adjuster’s help that soul ascended to the judgment halls of the mansion worlds. (111:7.2, 111:7.3, 111:7.5)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 111 closes the Adjuster series by turning to our part. The body and the Adjuster were given to us, but the mind was entrusted to us, and the choices we make in that mind build the soul. The soul grows in a school, not on a testing ground, and consecrating the will to God is not losing ourselves but gaining more. (111:1.4, 111:5.5)
