Introduction — Why Does a Creator Become a Creature?

The author of Paper 119 is Gavalia, Chief of the Evening Stars of Nebadon. Sent by Gabriel to tell the story of the seven bestowals of Michael, the Universe Sovereign, he states at the outset that he will keep strictly to the limits of his commission. Bestowal is inherent in the Paradise Sons, and it began when the Eternal Son bestowed himself seven times on the seven circuits of Havona. (119:0.1, 119:0.2)

A Creator Son vows to the Trinity not to assume full sovereignty of his universe until he has completed seven creature bestowals and been certified by the Ancients of Days. Bestowals are not essential to governing a universe efficiently, but they are absolutely necessary to governing a universe of imperfect creatures fairly, mercifully, and with understanding. (119:0.3, 119:0.5)

I. The First Bestowal — A Strange Melchizedek

Almost a billion years ago, Michael announced that his elder brother Immanuel would take charge of Nebadon while he was absent on an unexplained mission. Standing alone on the dispatching field, he said that many would wish to go with him but that this was something they could not do, and then he vanished from the sight of all. (119:1.1, 119:1.2)

I go to do the will of the Paradise Deities (119:1.2)

At noon on the third day, a strange Melchizedek Son, wholly like that order, appeared on the Melchizedek sphere accompanied by a solitary omniaphim. For a hundred years he served on emergency missions, and by universal consent he became chief of the Melchizedeks. The last words of the Melchizedeks’ record bear brief and warm witness to how the Creator lived among his creatures. (119:1.3, 119:1.4, 119:1.5)

He loved us, understood us, and served with us, and forever we are his loyal and devoted fellow Melchizedeks (119:1.5)

The author notes that the records do not specifically name this Melchizedek as Michael, though it is universally believed that he was. The fact of the bestowals is known; the method is recorded only on Sonarington. This honest marking of limits runs through the whole paper. (119:1.6)

II. The Second Bestowal — Savior Sovereign of a Rebel System

The second bestowal took place in the midst of rebellion. After Lutentia, sovereign of system 11 in constellation 37, refused to accept a verdict and led a rebellion, Michael disappeared again, and three days later a new Son appeared among the Lanonandek reserves and became acting System Sovereign. He set the confused system in order with justice and mercy, and even offered to share the throne with the rebel if only he would apologize. (119:2.1, 119:2.4, 119:2.5)

Lutentia knew the stranger was Michael and spurned the offer, but millions of his misguided followers accepted forgiveness. The new ruler became known as the Savior Sovereign of Palonia, and on the day he left, even the rebel sent a message acknowledging him. (119:2.5, 119:2.6)

“Just and righteous are you in all your ways. While I continue in rejection of the Paradise rule, I am compelled to confess that you are a just and merciful administrator.” (119:2.6)

III. The Third Bestowal — Material Son on an Isolated World

The third bestowal was on planet 217, in the system where Nebadon’s second rebellion had occurred. Michael held back the Life Carriers’ request and disappeared; on the third day a strange Material Son appeared with a seconaphim and was made acting Planetary Prince. On a world cut off from outside communication he worked alone for a whole generation and brought about the repentance and reclamation of the defaulting Planetary Prince and his staff. (119:3.1, 119:3.3, 119:3.4)

The author counts this reclamation among the most touching chapters in the salvation annals of Nebadon. Since then no Material Son or Daughter in Nebadon has complained of the difficulty of a mission, and none has knowingly joined a rebellion against Michael. Knowing that their sovereign went through the very trials they face has made their loyalty firm. (119:3.5, 119:3.7, 119:3.8)

the Material Sons know that in the Creator Son of the universe they have an understanding sovereign and a sympathetic friend, one who has in “all points been tried and tested,” even as they must also be tried and tested (119:3.7)

IV. The Fourth Bestowal — The Sovereign Disguised as a Seraphim

In the fourth bestowal Michael became a supreme seraphim and for over forty years served as teaching counselor to twenty-six master teachers. Throughout that age every seraphim was regarded with special solicitude, since anyone might unknowingly have the Universe Sovereign as a colleague. His identity was established with certainty only at the end, when he was attached to the bestowal mission of a Trinity Teacher Son. (119:4.3, 119:4.4)

As the bestowals descended toward lower forms of life, Gabriel became more closely involved, acting as liaison between the bestowed Michael and Immanuel, the acting ruler. Having lived the lives of three orders of his created Sons and of the angels, Michael now turned to the ascending career of his lowest will creatures, the evolutionary mortals. (119:4.5, 119:4.6)

V–VI. The Fifth and Sixth Bestowals — Walking the Path of the Ascending Mortal

In the fifth bestowal Michael appeared on Uversa as Eventod, an unnumbered spirit pilgrim of mortal origin, and for eleven years was tested and tried just like his fellows. His first appearance in a stage of mortal evolution thrilled all Nebadon. Afterward the Melchizedek colleges began to teach the likelihood that Michael would one day come as a mortal of the flesh. (119:5.1, 119:5.2, 119:5.4)

The technique remained a mystery. Even Gabriel confesses that he does not understand how the Creator can take on the personality of one of his creatures and live its life. In the sixth bestowal Michael lived as a morontia mortal on Endantum; the author says it was one of the most extraordinary epochs of all his bestowals, but he is forbidden to reveal the details. (119:5.5, 119:6.3)

Millions gathered on Salvington to welcome Michael back. Addresses of thanks poured in, but his reply was short and humble. By this time it was clear to all that their Creator had become a fellow creature. (119:6.4, 119:6.5)

“I have simply been about my Father’s business. I am only doing the pleasure of the Paradise Sons who love and crave to understand their creatures.” (119:6.5)

VII. The Seventh and Final Bestowal — As a Helpless Infant

Urantia was announced as the scene of the final bestowal shortly after the default of Adam and Eve became known. Apart from the mystery of incarnation, there was no secrecy about any step of this bestowal. What surprised everyone was that Michael came not fully grown, as before, but as a helpless infant. (119:7.2, 119:7.3)

it was a thrilling announcement which was broadcast from Salvington telling that the babe of Bethlehem had been born on Urantia (119:7.3)

The author says this seemed the most precarious step of Michael’s career. A universe that knew of the Lucifer rebellion and the Caligastia disaffection could gauge the intensity of the struggle ahead. The baby Joshua ben Joseph was conceived and born like any other child and raised by ordinary parents in the manner of his time. The only supernatural event connected with his birth was the seraphim’s announcement to Ardnon and his associates. (119:7.4, 119:7.5, 119:7.6, 119:7.7)

VIII. Michael’s Postbestowal Status — Sovereignty Earned by Experience

After the final bestowal Michael was proclaimed settled ruler of Nebadon, followed by the eighth proclamation from Uversa and a joint pronouncement from the Father and the Son. When the Trinity Sons were instructed to withdraw, Michael personally asked them to remain in Nebadon forever. He would not consent to the departure of the Sons of counsel and co-operation, and Paradise attached them permanently to his court. (119:8.1)

Michael was born a creator, educated an administrator, trained an executive, but he was required to earn his sovereignty by experience. (119:8.2)

The seven bestowals augmented not only Michael’s sovereignty but also the sovereignty of God the Supreme. Each one revealed one of the wills of the Deities personalized in the Seven Master Spirits, and the titles Urantia gives him trace that same journey. (119:8.3, 119:8.4, 119:8.8)

Urantia thus became the sentimental shrine of all Nebadon and the mortal home of Christ Michael. This paper, the last of Part III, is the sixty-third of a series narrating the history of Urantia down to Michael’s appearance in the flesh, and it was written in A.D. 1935. (119:8.8, 119:8.9)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 119 shows that power cannot take the place of understanding. Even the Creator of a universe had to live as a creature before he could rule. Anyone placed in a position to lead or judge others can learn the humility of actually entering into their situation. (119:0.6)
