Introduction — Why the Universe Is Divided into Seven

When Paper 15 pictured the grand universe as a wheel, its seven spokes were the radiations of the Seven Master Spirits. Paper 16 explains who these Master Spirits are. They are the primary personalities of the Infinite Spirit, and there are seven of them because there are exactly seven associative possibilities inherent in the three Deities, the Father, the Son, and the Spirit. (16:0.1)

And this explains why the universe is operated in seven grand divisions, and why the number seven is basically fundamental in its organization and administration. (16:0.1)

In spirit character the Seven Spirits are as one, but in every other aspect of identity they are very unlike, and their differences condition the character of the seven superuniverses. Each Master Spirit keeps a force-focal headquarters on the periphery of Paradise, always positioned opposite the superuniverse it supervises. (16:0.11, 16:0.12)

I–II. Relation to Triune Deity; Relation to the Infinite Spirit

If the threefold personalization of Deity as Father, Son, and Spirit is the absolute inevitability, the appearance of the Seven Master Spirits is the subabsolute inevitability. To create universes duly expressive of threefold Deity, seven associative expressions were necessary. Through the Seven Master Spirits the Father, the Son, the Spirit, or any dual association can function, but not the Trinity. (16:1.1, 16:1.2)

Yet when the Seven Spirits vacate their individual seats and assemble about the Conjoint Actor on Paradise, they collectively represent the functional power, wisdom, and authority of the Trinity to the evolving universes. They then encompass the functional domain of the Supreme-Ultimate. (16:1.3)

Their relation to the Infinite Spirit is summed up in an order of divine communication. The Father speaks through the Son; the Father and the Son act through the Infinite Spirit; and outside Paradise and Havona the Infinite Spirit speaks by the voices of the Seven Master Spirits. This is why the spirit presence of the Third Source and Center on our world is conditioned by the unique nature of the Master Spirit who supervises Orvonton. (16:2.2, 16:2.3)

Outside of Paradise and Havona the Infinite Spirit speaks only by the voices of the Seven Master Spirits. (16:2.2)

The Master Spirits are collectively endowed with the supreme-ultimate attributes of the Infinite Spirit. Omnipotence, omniscience, and omnipresence are disclosed only collectively; as individuals, each is limited to the superuniverse he supervises. (16:2.4)

The Master Spirits have names, but the paper introduces them by number. Each expresses one of the seven associations of Deity and speaks for that association in their councils. (16:3.1)

The most important for us is Master Spirit Number Seven. He is a portrait of Father, Son, and Spirit in equal proportions, and because he is personally nonfunctional with regard to the Trinity, he can function personally for the Supreme Being. In the present age, when the person of the Supreme cannot be contacted, he functions in the place of God for ascending creatures. (16:1.2, 16:3.15, 16:3.19)

He is the one high spirit being that all ascenders are certain to recognize and somewhat comprehend when they reach the centers of glory. (16:3.19)

The statement that his administration of Orvonton discloses the symmetry of the co-ordinate blending of the natures of Father, Son, and Spirit fits well with the merciful character of Orvonton we saw in Paper 15. Our superuniverse lives under a Spirit of balance who leans toward no single side. (16:3.20)

IV. Attributes and Functions of the Master Spirits — “The Seven Spirits of God”

The Seven Master Spirits are the full representation of the Infinite Spirit to the evolutionary universes and function on all levels of reality except the absolute. They are the creators of the Universe Power Directors, and they materially assist the Creator Sons in organizing the local universes. Above all, combining matter and spirit to produce morontia substance and morontia mind is their great contribution to the plan of man’s Paradise ascent. (16:4.1, 16:4.2, 16:4.3, 16:4.6)

It is literally true that these Seven Spirits are the personalized physical power, cosmic mind, and spiritual presence of the triune Deity, “the Seven Spirits of God sent forth to all the universe.” (16:4.1)

The presenter considers it highly probable that the Master Spirit of Orvonton has a decided influence on the life initiation of the Life Carriers, the activation of the adjutant mind-spirits, the bestowal of the Holy Spirit and the Spirit of Truth, and the mechanism of reflectivity. On the other hand, there are two realms with which they have nothing whatever to do: the bestowal and ministry of the Thought Adjusters, and the functions of the Unqualified Absolute. The Adjuster within us belongs to the Father alone. (16:4.8, 16:4.16)

V. Relation to Creatures — The Indelible Stamp of Origin

Every universe enjoys the united counsel and wisdom of all Seven Master Spirits, but receives the personal touch and tinge of only one. The Master Spirits do not directly invade the minds of mortals; they influence us through the ministry of the local universe Creative Spirit. And our Creative Mother Spirit is, in nature and character, quite like the Master Spirit of Orvonton. (16:5.1, 16:5.3)

Throughout all eternity an ascendant mortal will exhibit traits indicative of the presiding Spirit of his superuniverse of nativity. (16:5.5)

This stamp is not erased even by the long training of Havona and the culture of Paradise. That is why, in the Corps of the Finality, when a complete relationship to the evolutionary creation is to be portrayed, a group of seven finaliters is assembled, one from each superuniverse. Diversity is not a flaw to be removed but a share that completes the whole. (16:5.5)

VI. The Cosmic Mind — The Roots of Science, Morality, and Religion

The Master Spirits are the sevenfold source of the cosmic mind. On Urantia we live under the immediate influence of the Creative Spirit of Nebadon, yet the basic reactions of all creature mind come from the Master Spirits. The cosmic mind explains why the minds of different people sometimes reach astonishing agreement. It also has a quality of “reality response” that saves will creatures from becoming helpless victims of the unexamined assumptions of science, philosophy, and religion. (16:6.1, 16:6.2, 16:6.3, 16:6.4)

When these three insights are unified, they produce a strong character in which factual science, moral philosophy, and genuine religious experience are joined. The presenter regrets that so few on Urantia take delight in courageous and independent cosmic thinking, and he sums up how the whole of human life serves these endowments. (16:6.9, 16:6.10)

It is the purpose of education to develop and sharpen these innate endowments of the human mind; of civilization to express them; of life experience to realize them; of religion to ennoble them; and of personality to unify them. (16:6.11)

VII. Morals, Virtue, and Personality — Beings Who Look Before They Leap

What distinguishes man from the animals is not so much intelligence as his moral and religious nature. An animal ordinarily learns only by leaping, but a personality can look before it leaps. Intelligence can choose among means, but a moral being judges the worth of the ends themselves. (16:7.1, 16:7.3, 16:7.4)

To name virtues is not to define them, but to live them is to know them. (16:7.6)

Virtue is realized by consistently choosing good rather than evil, and it also requires a sense of proportion to weigh the lesser against the greater. Morality is never advanced by law or by force; it spreads by the “contagion” of contact with morally fragrant persons. And supreme virtue is wholeheartedly to choose to do the will of the Father in heaven. (16:7.7, 16:7.9, 16:7.10)

VIII–IX. Urantia Personality; Reality of Human Consciousness

Personality is a unique gift of the Father, independent of and antecedent to the bestowal of the Adjuster. Adjusters are identical in nature, but personality is diverse, original, and exclusive. However much the body and mind may change, personality is what lets us recognize a person as the one we knew. Creature personality is distinguished by self-consciousness and relative free will. (16:8.3, 16:8.4, 16:8.5)

The ninth section contrasts God-consciousness with social consciousness. We can be more certain of the presence of God within us than of the reality of a fellow being, and social consciousness, being a cultural development, is unstable. Civilizations come and go, but science, morality, and religion always survive the crash. Unselfishness, moreover, does not arise on its own; it comes from knowing God. (16:9.4, 16:9.5, 16:9.7)

Only a God-knowing individual can love another person as he loves himself. (16:9.8)

In the end, self-consciousness is essentially communal. Because we are conscious of God as our Creator Father, we become conscious of other people as our brothers. The paper closes by giving three reasons for worship: because he is, because he is in us, and because we are in him. (16:9.9, 16:9.14)

We worship God, first, because he is, then, because he is in us, and last, because we are in him. (16:9.14)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 16 begins with seven Spirits on high and comes down to the structure of our minds and the virtues of everyday life. The stamp left by the Master Spirit of Orvonton becomes part of our individuality forever, and the cosmic mind they bestow gives rise to the insights of science, morality, and worship. To weave those insights into one and choose the Father’s will is both the purpose of personality and the supreme virtue.
