Introduction — The Names Given to Mankind’s Parents

Paper 62 told how the first human twins were born and set out for the north; Paper 63 tells of their life and of the first human family they founded. Urantia was registered as an inhabited world when the twins were eleven, before they had their first child. On that occasion the archangel message from Salvington gave names to the parents of the new race. (63:0.1)

Man-mind has appeared on 606 of Satania, and these parents of the new race shall be called Andon and Fonta. (63:0.2)

Interestingly, the two never knew these names until they fused with their Thought Adjusters. On earth they called each other by names they had chosen themselves, and those names carried their mutual regard and deep affection. (63:0.3)

I–II. Andon and Fonta; The Flight of the Twins — The First to Kindle Fire

In many respects Andon and Fonta were the most remarkable pair of human beings ever to live on earth. They were superior to many of their immediate descendants and radically different from all their ancestors. While still living with his parents, Andon bound a sharp flint to the end of a club with animal tendons, and on at least a dozen occasions this weapon saved his life and his sister’s. Their vague sense of being more than mere animals came from their possession of personality and was heightened by the indwelling Thought Adjusters. (63:1.1, 63:1.3, 63:1.4)

The decision to leave was not easy. They feared displeasing their father and family, and they knew that their already jealous tribesmen might kill them. But one night, after a violent storm had awakened them, holding each other in a fearful and fond embrace, they finally made up their minds to go. (63:2.1, 63:2.2)

The discovery of fire was the fruit of partnership: the idea was Andon’s, but it was Fonta who unlocked the secret. Their Primates ancestors had often kept alive fires kindled by lightning, but never before had any creature on earth possessed a way of starting fire at will. (63:2.5, 63:2.7)

This was one of the most joyous moments in their short but eventful lives. (63:2.6)

All night they sat watching the fire, dimly realizing that this discovery would let them defy the climate and so be forever independent of their animal relatives in the south. A single discovery had changed the whole setting of their lives. (63:2.6)

III. Andon’s Family — The Instinct to Care and the Spirit of the Clan

Almost two years after their flight their first child, Sontad, was born. He was the first creature on Urantia to be wrapped in protective coverings at birth, and from this time appeared the instinct to care for increasingly helpless infants. The Life Carrier sees in this a sign that mind of the intellectual order was developing. (63:3.1)

The human race had begun (63:3.1)

Andon and Fonta had nineteen children and lived to see almost fifty grandchildren and six great-grandchildren. The family lived in rock shelters, some joined by hallways dug out of the soft limestone, and with a marked clannish spirit they hunted together and stayed close to home. This sense of intimate kinship, we are told, came from the enhanced ministry of the adjutant spirits. The two died together at forty-two, killed by a rock that fell during an earthquake. (63:3.2, 63:3.3, 63:3.4)

Though his foot was seriously injured, Sontad at once took up leadership of the clan and rolled up stones to entomb the dead. The paper cautions, however, against reading too much into this burial: their ideas of survival after death were vague and largely drawn from their dream life. The family held together until the twentieth generation, when food competition and social friction began to scatter it. (63:3.5, 63:3.6)

IV. The Andonic Clans — Foreshadowings of Nobility and Seeds of Division

In describing primitive man, this section shows both what was already present and what was still missing. The men fought heroically for their mates and children, and the women were devoted to their young, but their loyalty stopped at the immediate clan. There was also a touching affection for comrades and a real, if crude, idea of friendship. (63:4.3, 63:4.4)

These early humans were less sensitive to pain and unpleasant situations than many later mortals, and childbirth was not painful for Fonta and her immediate descendants. The clan kept an unbroken line of leadership through Sontad’s descendants until the twenty-seventh generation; when no male heir appeared, two rivals began to fight for supremacy. Even this early, a gap in leadership became a door to division. (63:4.2, 63:4.5)

The paper describes their appearance simply. The Andonites had black eyes and a swarthy complexion, something between yellow and red, and melanin was the original Andonic skin pigment. They were the first creatures to use animal skins for protection against cold. They added new words almost daily and built a well-developed language, which remained the tongue of Urantia until the colored races appeared. (63:4.1, 63:4.6)

But as their numbers grew, hunting and revenge came to occupy their minds, and clan warfare forever destroyed some of the ablest strains. The Life Carriers, knowing this tendency, provide for the eventual separation of evolving humans into several distinct races. (63:4.7, 63:4.8, 63:4.9)

Man is the descendant of fighting animals (63:4.9)

V. Dispersion of the Andonites — North and West

The early Andon races did not go deep into Asia or into Africa; following the lay of the land, they went north until the third glacier stopped them. Before the ice sheet reached France and the British Isles, they had spread west across Europe and founded more than a thousand settlements along the great rivers flowing to the then warm North Sea. That is why so much evidence of them is found along the Somme, a river whose course the glaciers did not change. (63:5.1, 63:5.2, 63:5.3)

They lived under overhanging cliffs along the rivers and in hillside grottoes where they could enjoy their fires, and they built dome-shaped stone huts, closing the entrance at night by rolling a stone placed inside for that purpose. As Andon had invented the stone ax, his descendants discovered the throwing stick and the harpoon, and they traveled far in search of flint. (63:5.4, 63:5.5, 63:5.6)

At last a tool-creating mind was functioning in conjunction with an implement-using hand (63:5.6)

The section ends on a sober contrast. In many ways the Andon tribes showed an intelligence that their retrogressing descendants did not reach again in half a million years; those descendants had to rediscover how to kindle fire again and again. The passage quietly shows that what one generation gains is not automatically inherited by the next. (63:5.7)

VI. Onagar — The First Truth Teacher

As the dispersion spread, the cultural and spiritual state of the clans declined for nearly ten thousand years. Then Onagar became their leader, brought peace among the tribes, and for the first time led them all in worship of the “Breath Giver to men and animals.” Until then primitive religion had drifted with fear and hunger. (63:6.1)

The explanation of the origin of sacrifice is especially worth noting. The Andonites refrained from eating the flesh of the animal their tribe venerated, and to impress the young they held a ceremony around its body. This primitive rite grew into the more elaborate sacrifices of later times, and the text says the idea was carried on by Moses in the Hebrew ritual and preserved in principle by Paul in the doctrine of atonement. (63:6.4)

And this is the origin of sacrifices as a part of worship. (63:6.4)

How central food was to their lives can be seen in the prayer Onagar taught. Daily food, deliverance from the enemies of ice and forest, and hope for the life beyond are all contained in one short prayer. (63:6.5)

“O Breath of Life, give us this day our daily food, deliver us from the curse of the ice, save us from our forest enemies, and with mercy receive us into the Great Beyond.” (63:6.6)

Onagar kept his headquarters at Oban, near the present Caspian Sea, and sent teachers out to the remote settlements. These emissaries were the world’s first missionaries, and also the first to use fire regularly in preparing food. Born 983,323 years ago, he lived to sixty-nine and established a tribal government whose like was not attained for many millenniums. It was in his day that Thought Adjusters and guardian seraphim came to Urantia in great numbers. (63:6.7, 63:6.8, 63:6.9)

This was, indeed, the golden age of primitive man. (63:6.9)

VII. The Survival of Andon and Fonta — Service on the Mansion Worlds

The two founders of the human race were recognized at the adjudication that came with the arrival of the Planetary Prince, and they passed through the mansion worlds to citizenship on Jerusem. They grieved over the Caligastia betrayal and the Adamic failure, but rejoiced exceedingly when they learned that Michael had chosen their world for his final bestowal. On Jerusem, Andon and Fonta, Sontad, and several of their children fused with their Thought Adjusters, though most of their immediate descendants achieved Spirit fusion. (63:7.1, 63:7.2)

Afterward the two returned to the first mansion world to serve with those who welcome the pilgrims of time from Urantia, and they have been assigned to this service indefinitely. They asked to send greetings to Urantia with these revelations, but the request was wisely denied. That mankind’s first parents are still welcoming newly arrived mortals is the warm note on which the paper ends. (63:7.3)

the most heroic and fascinating chapter in all the history of Urantia (63:7.4)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 63 shows that mankind began as one very small family. There was a decision to leave together despite fear, a fire won after repeated failure, a son who led his clan with an injured body, and a teacher who taught the one God again in an age of decline. They did not yet know laughter or altruism, yet within them the noblest traits of humanity were already foreshadowed. (63:4.4)
