Introduction — Religion Is More Than a Moral Movement

Paper 101 is the most philosophical of the Melchizedek series on religion. Religion ranges from the primitive fear slavery of the savage to the faith liberty of those who are conscious of sonship with God. It is the ancestor of advanced ethics and morals, yet in itself it is not merely a moral movement. (101:0.1, 101:0.2, 101:10.10)

From the outset the author says that religious assurance is more than an emotional feeling; it transcends the reason of the mind and even the logic of philosophy. It is this assurance that triumphs over the logic of despair born in the unbelieving material mind. (101:0.3)

Religion is faith, trust, and assurance. (101:0.3)

I. True Religion — Through Thought, Not Feeling

True religion is neither a philosophic system proved by natural evidence nor an ecstasy enjoyed only by mystics. It is not the product of reason, yet seen from within it is altogether reasonable; it is not derived from philosophy, yet as experience it is altogether logical. The Thought Adjuster has no special mechanism for self-expression, so religious experience takes place through the natural mechanism of the mortal mind. (101:1.1, 101:1.2)

It is your thoughts, not your feelings, that lead you Godward. (101:1.3)

The divine spirit contacts us not through emotions but in the realm of the highest and most spiritualized thinking. Religion, then, is not the search for new facts or unusual experiences, but the discovery of new spiritual meanings in facts already known. When the sense of evolutionary duty and the illumination of revelation make a deep enough impression, a person finally concludes that he has no right not to believe in God. (101:1.3, 101:1.4, 101:1.7)

II. The Fact of Religion — The First Cause of Science and the God of Salvation

The fact of religion lies wholly in the religious experience of rational and average human beings. The proof that revelation is revelation lies in the same experience: revelation unites the apparently divergent sciences of nature and the theology of religion into one consistent universe philosophy. (101:2.1)

Nature reveals only a God of motion and offers no logical ground for believing in personal survival. Neither does it rule out seeing the universe as the handiwork of the God of religion. The order matters: the one who first finds God in his own soul then sees the same God in nature. (101:2.9, 101:2.11)

The religious man who finds God in nature has already and first found this same personal God in his own soul. (101:2.9)

The realization of religion has never depended on great learning or clever logic. This is why some of the greatest religious teachers had little worldly wisdom, and why faith is open alike to the learned and the unlearned. Among those with a similar experience no argument about God is needed, and to those not sure of God no argument will ever truly convince. (101:2.15, 101:2.16)

IV. The Limitations of Revelation — The Cosmology Is Not Inspired

This section is remarkable because here revelation states its own limits. The revelators may not impart knowledge that humanity has not yet earned or that would be premature. So they say in advance that while the historic facts and religious truths of these papers will stand, many of their statements about the physical sciences will need revision within a few years. (101:4.1, 101:4.2)

The cosmology of these revelations is not inspired. (101:4.2)

The point of the warning is clear: do not throw away the religious truth because of surface errors in the cosmology. In the end religion is judged by its fruits. Even so, revealed cosmology has great value: it reduces confusion by authoritatively eliminating error, coordinates known facts, restores lost knowledge, fills in gaps, and illuminates the spiritual teachings. (101:4.1, 101:4.4, 101:4.5)

Science deals with facts, religion with values, and philosophy seeks to unite the meanings of both. Feeling and emotion always accompany religion, but they are not religion. (101:5.1, 101:5.2, 101:5.9)

VI. Progressive Religious Experience — The Faith of Jesus and a Sevenfold Salvation

At every stage of religious experience the Thought Adjuster is the secret of the personal realization of the Supreme. Animals have knowledge, but only man has the capacity for wisdom, and truth becomes accessible to the wisdom-endowed mind through the Adjuster and the Spirit of Truth. Michael himself lived under evolutionary religion until his baptism, and from his baptism to the cross under the combined guidance of evolutionary and revealed religion. (101:6.2, 101:6.4, 101:6.5)

The paper says that the teachings of Jesus were the first Urantian religion to harmonize knowledge, wisdom, faith, truth, and love so fully as to give at once tranquillity, certainty, and the assurance of survival. The salvation to which his faith points is sevenfold. (101:6.8)

VII–VIII. A Personal Philosophy of Religion; Faith and Belief

A personal philosophy of religion draws its material from inner and outer experience: social status, education, temperament, vocation, marriage, and more. The paper describes four phases in its evolution. Moral cowards never reach high planes of philosophic thinking; it takes courage to explore unknown realms of thought. (101:7.1, 101:7.2, 101:7.4)

Source 101:7.4
PhaseWhat it looks like
FirstConforms to tradition and authority; may remain mere submission
SecondSatisfied with just enough to steady daily life; arrested early, letting well enough alone
ThirdAdvances to logical intellectuality but stagnates in cultural bondage
FourthFree of conventional handicaps; dares to think, act, and live honestly and fearlessly

Accepting a teaching as true is only belief. Belief becomes faith when it motivates life and shapes the way one lives. Beliefs can be held by groups, but faith rises only in the heart of the individual. Living faith does not betray intellectual integrity, foster bigotry or persecution, or hold an unreasoning prejudice against scientific discovery. (101:8.1, 101:8.2, 101:8.3, 101:8.4)

Belief fixates, faith liberates. (101:8.2)

IX–X. Religion and Morality; Religion as Man’s Liberator

No revelation can be authentic if it ignores the ethical duties fostered by evolutionary religion; revelation always enlarges the ethical horizon. The paper also urges us to judge primitive religion according to the enlightenment and conscience of its people, and not to measure another’s religion by our own standards of knowledge. The search for beauty belongs to religion only insofar as it enriches the moral. (101:9.1, 101:9.2, 101:9.4)

The last section pictures religion as a liberator. Neither physical causation nor the continuity of ideas can free man from the suspense of being a transient creature doomed to extinction. There is no scientific or logical proof of God, but whoever wills to do the will of God comes to comprehend the validity of spiritual values. The person freed by faith is no longer a slave of a mathematical cosmos but a liberated son of the Universal Father, at home in his universe. (101:10.3, 101:10.6, 101:10.7, 101:10.8)

Even time itself becomes but the shadow of eternity cast by Paradise realities upon the moving panoply of space. (101:10.9)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

Paper 101 locates religion not in argument or emotion but in experience within our highest thinking, shared with the Adjuster. Even revelation admits its limits here and asks to be judged by its fruits. What the paper offers the reader is a balance of assurance and humility: spiritual certainty that does not waver, and knowledge that must always keep growing. (101:4.2, 101:4.4)
