Introduction — After Thought and Word Comes Action

If Paper 6 told us that the Father’s “first” thought found its expression in the Eternal Son, the Word, Paper 8 tells of the next step. The Thought-God and the Word-God desire an infinite agent for mutual expression and combined action. The very moment the two conjointly conceive one infinite action—the execution of an absolute thought-plan—the Infinite Spirit, the Third Person of Deity, springs full-fledged into existence. (8:0.1, 8:0.3)

Underlying this eternal event is a covenant. In the dawn of eternity the Father and the Son become infinitely cognizant of their mutual dependence and absolute oneness, and they enter into a divine partnership to carry out their united concepts forever. The Infinite Spirit appears precisely where this partnership passes into action. From the beginning, then, the Spirit is the God of relationship and co-operation. (8:0.2)

Source 8:0.3
Person of DeityThe figure used in this paperRole
The Universal FatherThe Thought-GodThe first infinite thought
The Eternal SonThe Word-GodThe perfect expression of that thought
The Infinite SpiritThe God of ActionThe execution of the united thought and word

This sequence, however, is not an event in time. The author makes clear that he recites the order of origin only to help us think about their relationship. All three exist from eternity, without beginning or ending of days. (8:0.4)

They are and always have been and ever shall be. (8:0.4)

I. The God of Action — The Stage of Creation Is Set

With the personalization of the Infinite Spirit, the divine personality cycle becomes complete. The Spirit’s first act is to recognize his divine parents, the Father and the Son; his next act is remarkable. Though equal with them, he voluntarily pledges eternal loyalty to the Father and acknowledges everlasting dependence on the Son. In this mutual recognition the Paradise Trinity comes into being. (8:1.1, 8:1.2, 8:1.3)

When the God of Action functions, the dead vaults of space are astir, and one billion perfect spheres flash into existence. This section pictures the scene of creation in several stages. (8:1.4)

Source 8:1.5
StageWhat happened
1A billion worlds materialize, and Paradise gravity appears to hold them
2The second form of energy, spirit, flows out and is grasped by the spiritual gravity of the Son
3The soil is prepared for the consciousness of mind in the intelligence circuits of the Infinite Spirit
4The Father acts, and creature personalities appear

The Infinite Spirit eternalizes concurrently with the birth of Havona, and by this act of conjoint creation he becomes forever the Conjoint Creator. No records exist of anything before this event. The paper is also frank about why it explains things in sequence: the material mind needs a First Cause and a starting point for consistency. (8:1.7, 8:1.9, 8:1.10)

Beyond this event lie the unsearchable transactions of eternity and the depths of infinity—absolute mystery. (8:1.9)

The paper goes on to explain that we grasp reality the way a child does—first mastering the relationship with its parents, then gradually enlarging the concept to the family, the community, the world, and the universe. Our way of coming to know God likewise begins with close relationships and widens step by step. (8:1.11)

II. Nature of the Infinite Spirit — A Spirit Inclined Toward Mercy

The Infinite Spirit reflects perfectly the nature of both the Father and the Son, and he is known by many names. Each name throws light on one aspect of the Spirit. (8:2.1, 8:2.2)

Source 8:2.2
Group of namesThe aspect it reveals
Conjoint Creator · Conjoint Actor · Divine ExecutiveHe carries out the plans of the Father and the Son
Infinite Mind · Absolute IntelligenceHe is the source of mind
Universal Spirit · Spirit of Spirits · Omnipresent SpiritHe is present everywhere
Supreme Guide · Final Co-ordinator · Paradise Mother SpiritHe fosters, guides, and co-ordinates

The Infinite Spirit shares all the Father’s perfection, righteousness, and love, yet he inclines especially toward the Son’s attributes of mercy. So the Spirit becomes the mercy minister of the Deities to the grand universe. The Spirit does not have more goodness than the Father, but through his ministry we come to understand that goodness better. (8:2.6, 8:2.7)

This section also shows consideration for those who think in material terms. Creatures who tend to view matter as basic reality would better comprehend the Third Source and Center, it says, if he were called the Infinite Reality, the Universal Organizer, or the Personality Co-ordinator. And because the Conjoint Creator most strikingly discloses the Father’s infinity, even an infinitely expanding universe would find his spirit presence, energy control, and mind potential fully adequate. (8:2.3, 8:2.5)

as the divine Sons reveal the love of God, so the divine Spirit depicts the mercy of God (8:2.6)

The explanation that the Spirit is not better than the Father, but that the Father’s goodness becomes real to us through the Spirit, means much for the life of faith. The Father’s faithfulness and the Son’s constancy usually become real through the concrete ministry and service of the Spirit’s personalities. It is much like someone who has known God’s love only as an idea first feeling it while being cared for by another. (8:2.7)

III. Relation of the Spirit to the Father and the Son — The Way to the Father

Since the personalization of the Third Source, the Father no longer personally participates in universe creation; he delegates everything possible to the Son. The Son in turn bestows all possible authority and power on the Conjoint Creator. Our own local universe was made by a Creator Son and a Creative Spirit together, and the care of its creatures is entrusted to these two. (8:3.2, 8:3.4)

This chain of delegation shows that God is not one who clutches authority but one who shares it. And in all later creation the Infinite Spirit sustains the same personal relation to the Son that the Son sustains to the Father in the first and central creation. The relationships of the Deities repeat the same pattern from above to below. (8:3.3)

The very instant the Son accepted the Father’s plan of perfection attainment, the Infinite Spirit became its conjoint administrator and pledged all his resources to it. The heart of this section is the order laid out along the path of ascent. (8:3.5)

The Eternal Son is the only avenue of approach to the Universal Father, and the Infinite Spirit is the only means of attaining the Eternal Son. (8:3.7)
Source 8:3.8

1. The Infinite SpiritThe Third Person enshrouds the Second and the First and so is always recognized first
2. The Eternal SonDiscovered only by the patient ministry of the Spirit
3. The Universal FatherThe Son is the only avenue of approach to the Father

This order is not a hierarchy of faith but the path that love travels. That the first one we meet on the way to the Father is the Spirit, the minister of mercy, means that the first step of ascent always begins amid help and care. No one reaches the Father alone. (8:3.7, 8:3.8)

IV. The Spirit of Divine Ministry — Love, Mercy, Ministry

The fourth section sums up the Spirit’s character in a word. Everlasting ministry to mind is the essence of the Spirit, and all his offspring share his divine urge to serve. (8:4.1)

God is love, the Son is mercy, the Spirit is ministry (8:4.2)
Source 8:4.2
DeityCore characterHow it is shown
The FatherLoveThe source of all goodness
The SonMercyReveals love through the bestowals of the divine Sons
The SpiritMinistryApplies love and mercy to the creature creation

The Spirit does not come down in mortal flesh as the Sons do. Instead, he joyfully undergoes a series of divinity attenuations, stepping down and down until he appears as angels who stand at our side and guide us through the lowly paths of earthly life. In the local universe, in the person of the Mother Spirit, he is devoted to fostering creatures toward higher spiritual levels. And all the while his existence as the Third Person at the center of all things is not diminished in the least. (8:4.4, 8:4.5, 8:4.6)

In Havona the Infinite Spirit fully participated in the seven appearances of the Eternal Son and in the seven bestowals of the original Michael. So the Spirit became a sympathetic and understanding minister to every pilgrim of time who passes through those perfect circuits. The Spirit’s mercy is not pity looking down from above; it springs from an understanding gained by sharing the experience. (8:4.3)

Indeed is this Spirit “the eyes of the Lord which are ever over the righteous” and “the divine ears which are ever open to their prayers.” (8:4.8)

V. The Presence of God — Many Spirits Working as One

The outstanding attribute of the Infinite Spirit is omnipresence. The paper points out that in sacred writings the term “Spirit of God” seems to refer interchangeably to the Infinite Spirit on Paradise and to the Creative Spirit of the local universe. The Holy Spirit is a circuit indigenous to the local universe Mother Spirit and confined to that universe, but the Infinite Spirit is present everywhere. (8:5.1, 8:5.3)

The spiritual influences at work within us are many, but they are not divided against one another. Even the work of the Adjusters always coincides with the ministry of the Infinite Spirit and the Mother Spirit. Their origins differ, yet in our minds and souls they function as one spirit. (8:5.4)

So the paper explains why the spirit presence of the Infinite Spirit is often called “the spirit of God.” Because the Spirit is the Conjoint Actor, he is present not only as himself but also as the Father, as the Son, and as the Father-Son. That is why it is consistent to call the liaison of all spiritual ministry the spirit of God. (8:5.5, 8:5.6)

There are many spiritual influences, and they are all as one. (8:5.4)

VI. Personality of the Infinite Spirit

Because the Spirit’s bestowal is so widely spread, we can easily think of him as merely an impersonal force or influence. The paper warns against that misunderstanding. The Infinite Spirit is a universe presence, a cosmic power, and a universal mind, but he is also a true and divine personality. He has mind and will; he loves, and he sorrows. (8:6.1, 8:6.3, 8:6.4)

This section gathers scriptural passages that show the Spirit’s personality. The Spirit searches all things, even the deep things of God; he divides his gifts to each one as he wills; he makes intercession for us. The saying that those led by the Spirit of God are the sons of God is also offered as evidence that the Spirit influences us directly and personally. (8:6.3, 8:6.4)

therefore “Grieve not the Spirit of God.” (8:6.4)

In the Infinite Spirit the Father and the Son are mutually present. The Spirit is like the Father, like the Son, and like the Father and the Son as they two are forever one. So in the administration of the universes the three work perfectly and eternally interlocked. (8:6.6, 8:6.7)

Conclusion — Living This Paper

The Infinite Spirit of Paper 8 is not a distant, abstract force but a person who steps down even to the touch of an angel to come to our side. It is through the Spirit’s ministry that the Father’s love and the Son’s mercy actually reach our lives. And the picture of One who is equal yet freely confesses loyalty and dependence shows that service is never a lowly thing.

PracticeThe teaching behind it
Offer someone one small act of ministry todayThe Spirit is ministry
Choose mercy before judgmentThe Spirit inclines toward the Son’s mercy
Receive the various spiritual leadings within you as one guidanceMany spiritual influences are all as one
Do not grieve the Spirit by your words and deedsThe Spirit is a person who loves and sorrows

Paper 9 goes on to the Infinite Spirit’s relation to the universe—especially his work as the source of mind and his many offspring. If we read it remembering the ministering Spirit met in Paper 8, we will see that all that vast activity flows in the end from a single love.
